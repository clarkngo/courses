Accreditation asks whether a school meets the standard. The **Baldrige Excellence Framework** asks a harder question: how good is the school as a *system*, and is it getting better? Baldrige gives leaders one set of questions that connects mission, strategy, students, faculty, data, daily operations, and results. It also gives a scoring method that shows how mature each of those is.

This course applies the **Education Criteria** to one kind of organization: a **school of technology and computing** inside a private, nonprofit university that serves working adults online and in person. Think of a school like City University of Seattle's School of Technology and Computing. The running case is **Northbridge University's School of Technology and Computing (STC)**. Northbridge is a *composite*, and all of its numbers are invented for practice. Nothing in the case describes any real institution's data.

**Module 1** covers what Baldrige is and its core values in an academic culture. **Module 2** builds STC's Organizational Profile. **Modules 3 through 5** work through the six process categories in pairs. **Module 6** covers results, scoring, the 2026 revision, and a 12-month plan, including how a Baldrige self-assessment feeds an NWCCU accreditation report.

Every lesson has a diagram, a common pitfall, a hands-on exercise you can run with your own school, and a knowledge check.

**Edition note (checked November 2026).** The current Education edition is the **2023–2024 Baldrige Excellence Framework (Education)**, which has seven categories. The Baldrige Foundation released a **2026 revision** for business, nonprofit, and government organizations in August 2026. That revision uses six categories, builds results into each one, and adds three Maturity Pathways. The Foundation lists the 2026 Education edition as "available fall 2026," but it had not been released when this course was written. Lesson 6.2 explains the changes and how to move your work over. Check the Baldrige Foundation site for the current edition before you start a formal self-assessment.
