## Energy: A Mill That Fuels Itself

Papermaking is energy-intensive, but kraft mills have a built-in advantage: about half of every log ends up in black liquor and bark, both of which are burned for energy.

- The **recovery boiler** burns black liquor solids.
- A **power boiler** burns bark, wood waste, and sometimes other fuels.
- Steam drives **turbines** for electricity (cogeneration, or **combined heat and power**), then the lower-pressure exhaust steam heats the digesters and dryers.

Many kraft mills generate most of their own energy from these biomass sources. Recycled mills, which don't have black liquor, depend more on purchased fuel and electricity.

**Biggest energy users** to watch: dryer steam (remember the press section!), refiner electricity, and vacuum pumps.

## Water: Closing the Loop

Making paper uses a lot of water, but most of it is reused many times through the white-water loops. Mills reduce fresh water use by:

- Reusing white water and clarified filtrate in place of fresh water for showers and dilution
- **Save-alls** (disc filters, dissolved air flotation) that recover fiber and clarify water for reuse
- Counter-current washing in the fiberline

Water that leaves the mill goes to **effluent treatment** — typically primary clarification to remove solids, then **secondary (biological) treatment** in aerated basins that consume dissolved organics. Regulators track measures like **BOD** (biochemical oxygen demand), **COD**, **TSS** (total suspended solids), and, for bleached mills, **AOX** (adsorbable organic halides).

<Pitfall title="Closing the Water Loop Too Far">

**Common mistake:** cutting fresh water aggressively without a plan for what builds up.

Every time water is reused, dissolved salts, organic material, and anionic trash concentrate. Too much closure can raise temperatures, feed slime and deposits, increase corrosion, and wreck wet-end chemistry. Closure is a good goal — but it has to be paired with good chemistry control and kidney treatment.

</Pitfall>

## Forests and Fiber

- **Certification:** many mills source wood certified under **FSC** (Forest Stewardship Council), **SFI** (Sustainable Forestry Initiative), or **PEFC**, with **chain-of-custody** tracking from forest to product.
- **Residuals:** sawmill chips and thinnings are a major fiber source, so mills use parts of the tree lumber can't.
- **Recycling:** paper and cardboard are among the most recycled materials. OCC recovery in the U.S. has been reported around **70–90%** in recent years, depending on the source and year.

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Basis weight / grammage** | Mass per unit area of paper |
| **Black liquor** | Spent cooking liquor containing dissolved lignin; burned in the recovery boiler |
| **Broke** | The mill's own off-spec or trim paper, repulped |
| **Calender** | Roll stack that smooths and levels caliper |
| **Caliper** | Thickness of the sheet |
| **CD / MD** | Cross direction / machine direction |
| **Consistency** | Percent fiber (solids) in a stock slurry |
| **Couch roll** | Suction roll where the web leaves the forming fabric |
| **Freeness (CSF)** | How fast water drains from pulp — drops as refining increases |
| **Headbox** | Spreads stock into an even jet across the wire |
| **Kappa number** | Measure of lignin remaining in pulp |
| **OCC** | Old corrugated containers — recycled boxes |
| **Parent roll** | Full-width roll wound at the reel |
| **Refining** | Mechanical treatment that develops fibers for bonding |
| **Smelt** | Molten chemicals drained from the recovery boiler |
| **Stickies** | Tacky contaminants from adhesives and coatings |
| **White liquor** | Kraft cooking liquor: NaOH + Na₂S |
| **White water** | Water drained through the wire, reused in the short loop |

---

## Key Takeaway

Mills are energy- and water-intensive, but kraft mills recover chemicals and generate much of their own energy from biomass, and water is reused heavily before biological treatment. Fiber comes from certified forests, residuals, and a very high recycling rate.
