## Why Breaks Are So Expensive

When the sheet breaks, the machine keeps running but makes nothing saleable. Everything goes to the **couch pit** or **broke pulper** until the crew threads a narrow **tail** back through the machine and widens it to full width.

On a large machine, every minute of break time can mean a ton or more of lost production. A mill tracking **break frequency** and **minutes lost per break** is tracking money.

**Web inspection** and **break-detection camera systems** record video around the machine. Rewinding the footage to the moment of the break is often the fastest way to see where it started.

### Repeating Defects: Do the Math

If a defect repeats at a regular interval in the MD, measure the spacing. Then compare it with the **circumference** of rolls and fabrics:

> Repeat distance = π × roll diameter (for a roll) or the fabric length (for a felt or wire)

A spot every 1.88 m? That matches a roll about 0.6 m in diameter. Find which rolls in the machine are that size, and inspect them for a damaged cover, a deposit, or a doctor blade problem.

## Common Defects and Likely Causes

| Defect | What it looks like | Common causes |
|---|---|---|
| **Holes** | Round or ragged openings | Stickies, slime, pitch deposits falling from machine surfaces; foam; shives |
| **Spots / dirt** | Specks in the sheet | Bark, dirt, plastics passing cleaners; scale deposits |
| **Edge cracks** | Breaks starting at the edge | Wet or weak edges, edge trim problems, draw problems |
| **Wrinkles** | Creases in MD | Uneven moisture or caliper profile, misaligned rolls, slack sheet |
| **Wet streaks** | MD bands with high moisture | Plugged felt, steam shower problems, headbox slice issues |
| **Curl** | Sheet won't lie flat | Moisture or fiber-orientation differences between top and bottom |
| **Blisters** | Bubbles in the sheet | Too-rapid drying of a wet sheet; trapped steam |
| **Slime holes** | Gelatinous lumps then holes | Microbiological growth in the white-water system |

### Deposits and Housekeeping

Many breaks trace back to **deposits** — pitch from wood, stickies from recycled fiber, scale from hard water, and biological **slime**. They build up on surfaces, then break loose and land on the sheet. Controls include:

- **Biocide programs** in the white water loop
- **Deposit control chemicals** and fixatives
- **Showers and doctor blades** that keep rolls and fabrics clean
- **Scheduled boil-outs** and washups during shutdowns

<Pitfall title="Changing Three Things at Once">

**Common mistake:** after a bad shift of breaks, the team changes the felt shower, adds retention aid, and slows the machine — all at the same time.

Breaks drop. Great — but nobody knows which change worked, so nobody knows which one to keep, and the same problem comes back next month. Unless safety or a customer order demands it, **change one variable at a time** and let the data settle before the next change.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

Breaks and defects are expensive; a structured approach — **locate, characterize, correlate, hypothesize, test, standardize** — finds root causes faster than guessing. **Repeating defects** can be traced to specific rolls by matching the repeat distance to roll circumference. Many holes and breaks start as **deposits**, so cleanliness is a production strategy.

<TryIt title="Find the Roll">

A customer reports a small dent in their linerboard every **1.41 m** along the roll. The mill has dryer cans at 1.83 m diameter, a calender roll at 0.45 m, and a felt-carrying roll at 0.60 m.

Compute each circumference (π × D): dryer cans ≈ 5.75 m; calender roll ≈ 1.41 m; felt roll ≈ 1.88 m. The **calender roll** matches — check it for a dent or deposit.

</TryIt>
