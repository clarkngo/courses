## The Machine Doesn't Know You're There

A paper machine runs continuously at speeds that can exceed 1,000 meters per minute, with hundreds of rotating rolls, hot surfaces, and tons of stored energy. Most serious injuries in mills come from a short list of hazards.

---

## 1. In-Running Nips

An **in-running nip point** is where two rolls — or a roll and a fabric, felt, or sheet — turn toward each other. Anything that touches the nip gets pulled in: a glove, a sleeve, a rag, a hand.

Controls include **fixed guards**, **interlocked gates**, **tail-threading systems** (rope carriers and air carriers) so nobody hand-feeds the sheet near a nip, and a strict rule: **never reach into a running machine**, even to grab a loose piece of paper.

<Pitfall title="Loose Clothing and Gloves Near Nips">

**Common mistake:** wearing loose gloves, long sleeves, a lanyard, or a rag in a back pocket on the machine floor.

A glove caught in a nip pulls the hand in before you can react. Many mills restrict gloves near running nips, require close-fitting sleeves, and ban rags at the machine. Follow your site's rule exactly — it was likely written after someone was hurt.

</Pitfall>

## 2. Hazardous Energy: Lockout/Tagout

Before anyone works on equipment, **all** energy sources must be isolated and verified at zero — not just the electrical disconnect.

Paper mills have a lot of **stored energy**: a large roll can coast for many minutes after the drive is off, pneumatic loading holds press nips closed, and steam lines stay pressurized. In the U.S., OSHA's lockout standard (29 CFR 1910.147) and its pulp and paper standard (29 CFR 1910.261) apply.

---

## 3. Confined Spaces

Chests, tanks, digesters, headboxes, pulpers, and hoods are **permit-required confined spaces**. Hazards include low oxygen, toxic gases, engulfment in stock, and moving agitators.

Entry requires a **permit**, isolation and lockout of all lines and agitators, **atmospheric testing** before and during entry, an **attendant** outside, and a rescue plan.

---

## 4. Chemical and Gas Hazards

| Hazard | Where | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S)** and reduced sulfur gases | Kraft pulping, liquor tanks, sewers | Toxic; deadens your sense of smell at high concentrations |
| **Chlorine dioxide (ClO₂)** | Bleach plant | Toxic gas; can decompose explosively at high concentrations |
| **Caustic (NaOH), white liquor** | Pulping, recovery | Severe chemical burns, especially to eyes |
| **Sulfuric acid, biocides** | Throughout | Burns, inhalation hazards |

Personal gas monitors, eyewash stations, safety showers, and chemical-specific PPE are part of daily life in a kraft mill.

---

## 5. Steam, Heat, and Rolls

- **Steam and condensate** lines and dryer hoods cause burns. Treat every line as live.
- **Roll handling:** parent and customer rolls weigh tons. **Clamp trucks**, overhead cranes, and roll storage stacks are a leading source of serious incidents. Pedestrians stay out of clamp truck lanes.
- **Dust and fire:** tissue and dry sections generate fine paper dust that can fuel fires; housekeeping is a fire-prevention control.

---

## Key Takeaway

The deadliest mill hazards are **in-running nips, stored energy, confined spaces, toxic gases, steam, and heavy rolls**. Guards, LOTO with verification, confined-space permits, gas monitoring, and traffic rules exist because people were hurt without them. **Never reach into a running machine.**
