## The TAM During an Incident

When a customer has a business-critical event, the TAM is usually **not** the one typing commands. The TAM is the **connector and communicator**:

- Confirm a **case** is open at the right severity, with impact, timeline, and resource IDs
- **Escalate** inside AWS so the right support and service-team engineers are engaged
- Join the customer's **incident bridge** and keep AWS and the customer in sync
- Send clear, regular **updates to executives** on both sides
- Make sure the event ends in a **post-incident review** with real actions

## Escalating Well

Escalation is a skill. A good escalation is **specific**:

> "Customer X, Sev 1 case 1234567: checkout errors at 40% since 14:05 UTC in us-east-1, about $50K/hour revenue impact. Errors align with DynamoDB throttling on table `orders` even though consumed capacity is below provisioned. Need the DynamoDB team to check for a hot partition or a service-side issue. Next customer update at 14:45."

That message contains **who, impact, timeline, evidence, the specific ask, and the next checkpoint**. Compare it with "Customer X is really upset, can someone look at their case?"

**When to escalate:** when the response is too slow for the business impact, when the case needs a service team, when the customer's executives are involved, or when there's a pattern across several customers.

**How often:** escalate **early** for real business-critical impact. A TAM who protects the customer by escalating early is showing **Customer Obsession** and **Bias for Action**. A TAM who escalates everything loses credibility.

## Communicating to Executives

During an event, leaders want three things: **what's the impact, what's being done, and when will I hear next?** Use a consistent update format:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Mitigating |
| **Impact** | ~40% of checkout requests failing in us-east-1 since 14:05 UTC |
| **Current actions** | Shifting writes to a new table partition key; DynamoDB team engaged |
| **Next update** | 14:45 UTC, or sooner if status changes |

Never guess at a root cause in an executive update. Say "**suspected**" or "**under investigation**" until it's confirmed. And never miss your **next update** time. If nothing changed, send the update anyway and say so.

## Correction of Errors (COE)

Amazon's post-incident process is called **Correction of Errors (COE)**. TAMs are often asked to help customers run something similar, or to explain an AWS-side event to a customer. A COE typically covers:

1. **What happened**: a factual summary
2. **Customer impact**: who was affected, how badly, for how long, with data
3. **Timeline**: detection, escalation, mitigation, resolution, with times
4. **Metrics**: graphs that show the event
5. **5 Whys**: asking "why?" repeatedly to get past the surface cause
6. **Lessons learned**
7. **Action items**: each with an **owner, a priority, and a due date**

### The 5 Whys, in practice

- *Why did checkout fail?* DynamoDB throttled writes.
- *Why?* All writes went to one partition.
- *Why?* The partition key was the **date**, so every order today shared a key.
- *Why was that not caught?* The load test used random dates.
- *Why?* There's no load-test standard that requires production-like key distribution.

Action items now target the **mechanism** (the load-test standard), not just the symptom (the key).

<Pitfall title="Blame in the Review">

**Common mistake:** the review concludes "an engineer pushed a bad config" and the action item is "be more careful."

COEs are **blameless**. People make mistakes; **mechanisms** should catch them. Good actions add a guardrail: automated config validation, a staged rollout, an alarm, a runbook. Amazon's phrase is "**good intentions don't work, mechanisms do**."

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

In an incident, a TAM **engages the right AWS experts, escalates specifically, and communicates on a schedule**. Afterward, they drive a **blameless COE**: impact, timeline, **5 Whys** down to the missing mechanism, and **action items with owners and dates**, then track those actions to completion.
