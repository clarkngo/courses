## Why Scenario Questions Matter

Many TAM interviews include a **troubleshooting scenario**: "A customer calls and says their site is down. What do you do?" The interviewer isn't only testing whether you know the answer. They're watching **how you think**: whether you ask clarifying questions, narrow the problem methodically, consider the business impact, and know when to escalate.

**Scope questions** to ask first:
- Is it **all users or some**? One Region, one AZ, one customer segment?
- **When** did it start? Exactly? Does it line up with a deploy or a traffic spike?
- What's the **business impact**? Revenue loss? Safety? Regulatory?
- Has a **support case** been opened, and at what severity?

**Mitigate first.** A TAM's first priority is restoring service: roll back the deploy, fail over, shift traffic, scale out. Root cause comes after customers are no longer affected.

## Signals at Each Layer

| Layer | What to check | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **DNS** | Does the name resolve? Right target? TTLs? | Route 53, `dig` / `nslookup` |
| **Edge** | CDN errors, WAF blocks, certificate expiry | CloudFront metrics and logs, WAF logs, ACM |
| **Load balancer** | Healthy target count, 4xx vs 5xx, latency | ELB CloudWatch metrics, access logs |
| **Network** | Security groups, NACLs, routes, NAT | VPC Flow Logs, Reachability Analyzer |
| **Compute** | CPU, memory, disk, status checks, app logs | CloudWatch, CloudWatch Logs, SSM |
| **Data** | Connections, CPU, storage, replica lag, throttling | RDS Performance Insights, DynamoDB metrics |
| **Control plane** | Who changed what, and when? API errors? | CloudTrail, AWS Config |

## Worked Scenarios

### "I can't SSH to my EC2 instance."

1. Check **status checks**. A system check failure points to AWS hardware (stop and start to move hosts). An instance check failure points to the OS (full disk, bad kernel, broken network config).
2. Check the **security group** (port 22 from your IP?), the **NACL** (both inbound *and* outbound ephemeral ports, since NACLs are stateless), and the **route table** (an internet gateway route for a public subnet).
3. Does the instance have a **public IP** or Elastic IP? Is the key pair right?
4. A better long-term answer: use **Systems Manager Session Manager** so no inbound port is needed at all.

### "Our ALB is returning 5xx errors."

- **502**: the target closed or reset the connection, or returned a malformed response. Check app crashes and keep-alive timeouts.
- **503**: no healthy targets are registered. Check health checks and the target group.
- **504**: the target took too long. Check slow downstream calls, like the database.
- Compare **`HTTPCode_ELB_5XX`** (generated by the load balancer) with **`HTTPCode_Target_5XX`** (returned by your app). That one comparison tells you which side to investigate.

### "S3 says Access Denied."

Permissions on AWS are evaluated across several policies, and an explicit **Deny anywhere wins**. Check, in order: the IAM identity policy, the **bucket policy**, **Block Public Access** settings, **SCPs** from AWS Organizations, **KMS key policy** (for encrypted objects), **object ownership**, and VPC endpoint policies. CloudTrail shows the denied call.

### "We're getting ThrottlingException."

The customer is hitting an **API rate limit or service quota**. Short-term: retries with **exponential backoff and jitter**. Long-term: request a **quota increase**, cache results, batch calls, and set **CloudWatch alarms on quota usage**. This is a classic proactive TAM finding: quotas checked *before* the launch, not during it.

<Pitfall title="Jumping Straight to the Answer">

**Common mistake:** the interviewer says "the site is down," and the candidate immediately says "it's probably the security group."

Even if you're right, you've skipped scoping and impact. Interviewers want to hear **questions first**, a **method**, and an explicit **mitigate-then-investigate** priority. Think out loud.

</Pitfall>

## When to Escalate

As a TAM you are rarely the person with root access to the customer's systems. Your job is to **get the right experts engaged fast**:

- Make sure a **case exists at the right severity** with clear impact and timeline
- Engage the **support engineer** on the case and, if needed, the **service team** through internal escalation paths
- Keep a **single source of truth** for the timeline and next update time
- Tell customer leadership **what you know, what you don't, and when they'll hear next**

---

## Key Takeaway

In scenario questions, **scope first, check what changed, check AWS Health, walk the layers, test one hypothesis at a time, and mitigate before root cause.** Know the classic patterns (status checks, SG vs NACL, 502/503/504, explicit deny, throttling with backoff) and show that you know **when and how to escalate**.

<TryIt title="Talk Through a Scenario">

Set a timer for 8 minutes and answer out loud:

*"It's 9:15 a.m. A retail customer's checkout API latency jumped from 200 ms to 6 seconds at 9:02. Nothing was deployed today. The VP of E-commerce is on the phone."*
