## The Process, Step by Step

Amazon's hiring steps vary by team, level, and country, and they change over time. Ask your recruiter what to expect; they will usually tell you. A typical path looks like this:

### The job posting

Read it closely. **Basic qualifications** are must-haves: they often include a number of years in a technical role and experience in customer-facing or support work. **Preferred qualifications** often list AWS certifications, specific technologies, or industry experience. The team description tells you which customers you'd serve (an industry, a segment, a region), which tells you what to study.

### The recruiter screen

A conversation about your background, why AWS, why TAM, location, and level. Have a crisp **"why TAM"** answer (Module 1) and know your work authorization and timing.

### The phone screen

Usually about an hour with a TAM or manager. Expect a mix of **technical questions** (services, a troubleshooting scenario) and **one or two LP questions**. Treat it like a short loop.

### The loop

Several back-to-back interviews, often virtual. Each interviewer covers **assigned LPs**, and some also cover **technical depth** or a **customer scenario**. One interviewer is usually a **Bar Raiser**: a trained interviewer from outside the hiring team whose job is to make sure every hire raises the bar. Some loops include a **role-play** or a **presentation**. Your recruiter will say if yours does.

### The debrief

The interviewers meet, share written feedback, and decide together. The Bar Raiser has a strong voice in that decision.

<Pitfall title="Studying Only Technology">

**Common mistake:** a candidate spends four weeks on AWS services and one evening on stories.

Most loop time is behavioral. A strong technical candidate with vague stories often doesn't get an offer. Split your prep roughly **evenly** between technical depth and LP stories.

</Pitfall>

## Interview-Day Habits

- **Pause before answering.** "Let me think of the best example" is fine.
- **Ask clarifying questions** in scenarios. TAMs ask before they act.
- **Say what you don't know.** "I haven't used that service. Here's how I'd find out" beats bluffing, and it shows **Earn Trust**.
- **Track the clock.** If a story runs long, the interviewer may not get to their second question.
- **Take notes** on each interviewer's name and focus, for your thank-you notes and your own reflection.
- **Ask good questions** at the end: "What does success look like for a TAM on this team in the first six months?" or "What's the biggest challenge your customers face right now?"

## Three Mock Scenarios

Practice each one out loud, timed, before reading the notes.

### Scenario 1: The Technical Deep-Dive (15 minutes)

*"A media customer streams video to 2 million users. During a live event last night, about 30% of viewers in Europe saw buffering for 20 minutes. Walk me through how you'd investigate and what you'd recommend."*

**What a strong answer covers:** scope questions (which Regions? all devices? did CloudFront error rates rise?), a layer-by-layer check (DNS, CloudFront, origin, origin shield, media services), checking AWS Health, a timeline against traffic, likely causes (origin overload on cache misses, regional edge capacity, a misconfigured TTL), and **proactive** recommendations: Countdown for the next live event, origin shield, load testing, real-user monitoring, and pre-event capacity planning.

### Scenario 2: The Customer Role-Play (15 minutes)

*"I'm the VP of Engineering at a fintech. We're on Enterprise Support, our bill grew 45% this year, and my CFO wants to know why we pay for support at all. Convince me."*

**What a strong answer covers:** **ask questions first** (what's driving growth? which outcomes matter to the CFO?), acknowledge the concern, offer a **cost review** with specific levers, connect the value of support to **outcomes** (incidents avoided, launches supported, risks closed), propose measurable targets for the next quarter, and agree on a follow-up date. Avoid getting defensive or reciting a feature list.

### Scenario 3: The Prioritization Problem (10 minutes)

*"It's Monday. Customer A has a Sev-2 that's been open for four days. Customer B's launch is Thursday and their load test failed. Customer C's CIO wants a meeting today about their contract renewal. You can't do it all. Walk me through your day."*

**What a strong answer covers:** a clear priority order with reasons (B's failed test before a fixed date is time-bound business risk; A needs an escalation, not your hands; C's meeting can be shared with the AM), **delegation and escalation** inside AWS, and **proactive communication** to every customer about when you'll engage.

## Key Takeaway

Expect a **recruiter screen, a phone screen, and a loop** with several interviewers, including a **Bar Raiser**. Ask your recruiter about the format. Split prep **evenly** between technical and behavioral work, ask clarifying questions, say what you don't know, and practice **technical, role-play, and prioritization** scenarios out loud.

- **2 × 30 min**: LP questions, two interviewers each covering three LPs
