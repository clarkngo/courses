### Week 1: Role and Foundations
- Read the **job posting** line by line; list every skill it names and rate yourself 1–5 on each
- Read the current **AWS Support plans** page and the official **Leadership Principles** page
- Review core services (Module 2); fill gaps with AWS documentation and the **Well-Architected Framework** whitepaper
- Build your **LP map** (Module 5, Lesson 1)
- Draft your **"why TAM, why AWS"** pitch

### Week 2: Technical Depth
- Whiteboard **three architectures** for resilience: a web app, a data pipeline, an event-driven system
- Practice **five troubleshooting scenarios** out loud (Module 2, Lesson 2)
- Pick **one deep area** you'll claim as a strength and study it until you can answer three levels of "why"
- If you haven't already, work toward the **Solutions Architect – Associate** level of knowledge (the certification itself is optional)

### Week 3: Stories and Customer Skills
- Write **12–15 STAR stories**, including two failures
- Practice each story aloud; trim each to 2–3 minutes
- Rewrite one past incident as a **COE**, with 5 Whys and owned actions
- Draft a one-slide **QBR** and a **cost review agenda** (Modules 3–4)

### Week 4: Mock Interviews and Polish
- Run at least **two full mock loops** with different people
- Rehearse the **role-play** and **prioritization** scenarios (Module 6, Lesson 1)
- Prepare **five questions** to ask your interviewers
- Re-check anything time-sensitive: support plans, programs, recent AWS announcements
- The day before: rest. Review your story list, not new material.

<Pitfall title="Memorizing Scripts">

**Common mistake:** a candidate memorizes stories word for word, then freezes when a follow-up breaks the script.

Memorize the **structure and the numbers** of each story, not the sentences. You should be able to tell the same story in 90 seconds or 4 minutes, depending on the follow-ups.

</Pitfall>

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **TAM** | Technical Account Manager: the designated technical advocate for an Enterprise Support customer |
| **Enterprise Support** | AWS Support plan that includes a designated TAM and 15-minute critical response |
| **Business Support+** / **Unified Operations** | The paid AWS Support plans below and above Enterprise Support as of 2026 |
| **Account team** | AM, SA, TAM, and others who jointly support a customer |
| **AM** | Account Manager: owns the commercial relationship |
| **SA** | Solutions Architect: leads design of new workloads |
| **Severity** | The urgency level of a support case, which sets the response target |
| **Trusted Advisor** | Automated checks across cost, performance, security, fault tolerance, service limits, and operational excellence |
| **AWS Health** | Personalized notices about events and lifecycle changes affecting a customer's resources |
| **Countdown** | AWS program for planned-event readiness; replaced IEM |
| **Well-Architected** | AWS framework with six pillars and a review process that finds high- and medium-risk issues |
| **RTO / RPO** | Recovery time objective (max downtime) / recovery point objective (max data loss) |
| **Static stability** | Designing a system to keep working through a dependency failure without changes |
| **Game day** | A planned exercise that simulates failure to test alarms, runbooks, and people |
| **COE** | Correction of Errors: Amazon's blameless post-incident review |
| **5 Whys** | Repeatedly asking "why" to reach the missing mechanism behind a failure |
| **Mechanism** | A repeatable process or tool that produces a result without relying on good intentions |
| **PFR** | Product feature request filed with an AWS service team on a customer's behalf |
| **QBR** | Quarterly business review with customer leadership |
| **Success plan** | The TAM's portion of the account plan: goals, risks, initiatives, cadence |
| **Savings Plans / RIs** | Commitment-based discounts for steady usage |
| **Leadership Principles (LPs)** | Amazon's 16 principles that guide hiring and behavior |
| **STAR** | Situation, Task, Action, Result: the format for behavioral answers |
| **Bar Raiser** | A trained interviewer from outside the hiring team who protects the hiring bar |
| **Loop** | The set of interviews in the final round |

## Course Review

**Module 1:** A TAM is the customer's **technical advocate** in Enterprise Support, splitting time between **reactive** incidents and **proactive** prevention, and judged on **outcomes**. Know the 2026 plans and the toolkit.

**Module 2:** Interviewers test **breadth** and then **depth**. Frame answers with the **six pillars**, and troubleshoot by **scoping, checking changes and AWS Health, walking the layers, and mitigating first**.

**Module 3:** In incidents, **escalate specifically and communicate on schedule**. Afterward, run a **blameless COE** that produces **mechanisms**. Proactively, run a **cadence**, prepare **launches**, track **lifecycle** events, and optimize **cost** with trade-offs stated.

**Module 4:** Earn trust by knowing the **business and the people**, co-writing a **success plan**, and running **outcome-led QBRs**. Influence through **credibility, data, and relationships**.

**Module 5:** Map your experience to the **16 LPs**, and build **12–15 STAR stories** told in first person with numbers.

**Module 6:** Know the **process**, practice **scenarios** out loud, and follow a **30-day plan** that balances technical and behavioral prep.

<TryIt title="Your Readiness Checklist">

Before your loop, check that you can:

- [ ] Explain the TAM role and your "why TAM" in 60 seconds
- [ ] Name the current AWS Support plans and the main TAM programs
- [ ] Whiteboard a Multi-AZ architecture and explain RTO/RPO and DR strategies
- [ ] Talk through a troubleshooting scenario using a clear method
- [ ] Write a strong escalation and an executive incident update
- [ ] Explain a COE and the 5 Whys with your own example
- [ ] Lead a cost review conversation with trade-offs
- [ ] Tell 12+ STAR stories covering every LP, including two failures
- [ ] Handle a role-play with an upset customer executive

</TryIt>
