## Why the Principles Run the Interview

Amazon's **Leadership Principles (LPs)** are how Amazon describes the behavior it hires for. In an interview loop, each interviewer is typically assigned **a few LPs** to assess, and most questions are **behavioral**: "Tell me about a time when..."

Your technical answers still matter. But many candidates with strong technical skills don't get offers because they can't show the LPs with **specific, first-person stories**. Read the official list on Amazon's website before your interview. The wording below paraphrases each principle.

---

## All 16 Principles, in TAM Terms

| Principle | Paraphrase | What it looks like for a TAM |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer Obsession** | Start with the customer and work backwards | Proactively finding a risk the customer didn't know about |
| **Ownership** | Act for the long term; never say "that's not my job" | Driving a fix that crosses several teams, to completion |
| **Invent and Simplify** | Find new, simpler ways | Automating a manual health check across 200 accounts |
| **Are Right, A Lot** | Strong judgment; seek different views | Correctly calling which of three risks to fix first |
| **Learn and Be Curious** | Always learning | Learning a new service deeply because a customer adopted it |
| **Hire and Develop the Best** | Raise the bar; coach others | Mentoring a new TAM; building enablement for the customer |
| **Insist on the Highest Standards** | Keep raising the bar | Refusing to close a COE with weak action items |
| **Think Big** | Bold direction | Proposing a multi-year resilience program, not one-off fixes |
| **Bias for Action** | Speed matters; many decisions are reversible | Escalating early during an incident instead of waiting |
| **Frugality** | Do more with less | Cost optimization; reusing existing mechanisms |
| **Earn Trust** | Listen, speak candidly, admit mistakes | Telling a customer AWS got something wrong, and what's next |
| **Dive Deep** | Stay close to details; audit often | Reading the metrics yourself instead of relying on summaries |
| **Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit** | Challenge respectfully, then commit | Pushing back on a risky launch date, then supporting the decision |
| **Deliver Results** | Focus on key inputs; deliver on time | Hitting the incident-reduction target in the success plan |
| **Strive to be Earth's Best Employer** | Safer, more productive, more inclusive work | Reducing on-call pain; supporting teammates |
| **Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility** | Consider wider impact | Considering security, sustainability, and downstream effects |

## The Principles TAM Loops Tend to Emphasize

Every loop is different, but TAM candidates commonly report questions around these. Prepare **at least two stories** for each.

### Customer Obsession
*"Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer."* · *"Tell me about a time you had to balance what a customer wanted with what they needed."*

### Ownership
*"Tell me about a time you took on something outside your responsibility."* · *"Tell me about a time you saw a problem nobody else was fixing."*

### Dive Deep
*"Tell me about the most complex technical problem you've solved."* Expect **follow-ups that go several levels deep**. The interviewer will keep asking "how did you know?" until they reach the edge of your knowledge.

### Earn Trust
*"Tell me about a time you had to deliver bad news."* · *"Tell me about a mistake you made."*

### Bias for Action
*"Tell me about a time you made a decision without all the information."*

### Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit
*"Tell me about a time you disagreed with a customer or your manager."*

### Deliver Results
*"Tell me about a time you missed a deadline."* · *"Tell me about your proudest accomplishment."*

<Pitfall title="Stories Without a Customer">

**Common mistake:** every story is about internal tooling or a manager.

A TAM is customer-facing. Make sure several of your stories involve an **external or internal customer**: someone whose outcome depended on you. If you've never had external customers, use internal ones: the teams your platform served, the users of your system.

</Pitfall>

---

## Tensions Between Principles

The best stories show you **balancing** principles that pull in different directions:

- **Bias for Action vs Dive Deep**: you mitigated fast, then investigated thoroughly
- **Customer Obsession vs Frugality**: you found a cheaper way to meet the customer's real need
- **Have Backbone vs Earn Trust**: you disagreed openly and kept the relationship strong
- **Ownership vs scope**: you took on a problem, and also knew when to hand it to the right owner

Interviewers notice when a candidate can explain **why** they chose one over the other.

---

## Key Takeaway

Amazon interviews are built on the **16 Leadership Principles**, with each interviewer usually assessing a few. For TAM roles, prepare deeply for **Customer Obsession, Ownership, Dive Deep, Earn Trust, Bias for Action, Have Backbone, and Deliver Results**. Use customer-centered stories, and show how you **balance principles that conflict**.

<TryIt title="Map Your Experience to the Principles">

Make a table with the 16 LPs as rows. For each, write the **one or two experiences** from your career that best show it, in a few words each.

Then look for gaps. If you have nothing for **Earn Trust** or **Have Backbone**, think harder about times you admitted a mistake or pushed back. Those are the stories candidates most often lack.

</TryIt>
