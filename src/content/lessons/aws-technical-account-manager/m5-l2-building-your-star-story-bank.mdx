## The STAR Format

Amazon interviewers expect behavioral answers in **STAR** form:

A good split of a 2–3 minute answer is about **10% Situation, 10% Task, 60% Action, 20% Result**. Most candidates spend too long on the situation and rush the action, which is the part interviewers actually assess.

## The Four Rules

1. **"I," not "we."** The interviewer is hiring you. "We migrated the database" hides what you did. "I designed the cutover plan and ran the rehearsal" doesn't.
2. **Specific, not general.** One real event, not "I usually..." or "I would..."
3. **Numbers in the result.** Time, money, incidents, customers, percentages. If you don't have exact numbers, give honest estimates and say so.
4. **Recent and relevant.** Prefer stories from the last few years, at a scope close to the role.

<Pitfall title="The Hypothetical Answer">

**Common mistake:** the interviewer asks "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a customer," and the candidate says "Well, I would first listen to their concerns, then..."

That's not a story. It tells the interviewer you may not have the experience. Always answer with a **real event**. If you truly have none, say so and offer the closest real example.

</Pitfall>

## Weak vs Strong: A Worked Example

**Question:** *"Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer."* (Customer Obsession)

**Weak:**
> "We had a customer whose app kept going down. Our team worked really hard on it, and we figured out it was a database issue. We fixed it and the customer was happy."

**Strong:**
> **S:** "At my last company I was the lead support engineer for our largest healthcare customer. They had three outages in six weeks, each about 40 minutes, and their CTO was reviewing the contract."
>
> **T:** "The cases had each been closed as 'resolved by restart.' I owned the relationship, and I wanted to find out why it kept happening."
>
> **A:** "I pulled six weeks of database metrics myself and lined them up with the outage times. All three started within two minutes of a nightly batch job that opened 900 connections against a pool limit of 1,000. I reproduced it in staging. Then I wrote a one-page proposal for their platform team: connection pooling through a proxy, and moving the batch to a read replica. They pushed back on the replica cost, so I modeled it: about $1,100 a month against roughly $60K of revenue lost per outage. I set up a weekly check-in until the change shipped, and added an alarm at 70% of the connection limit."
>
> **R:** "There were zero recurrences over the next nine months. The customer renewed for three years, and their CTO asked me to present the analysis to their engineering org. I learned to treat a repeated 'resolved by restart' as an open problem, and I added a repeat-incident check to our team's weekly review."

Notice: **first person**, **specific evidence**, a **disagreement handled with data**, a **mechanism** added, **numbers**, and a **lesson** that changed how the team works.

## Surviving the Follow-Ups

Interviewers dig into your stories with follow-up questions. Expect:

- "What exactly did **you** do versus your team?"
- "How did you know that was the cause? What else did you rule out?"
- "What would you do differently?"
- "What did the customer say? How did you measure that?"
- "What was the hardest part?"

**Only tell stories you know in detail.** If the details are fuzzy, the follow-ups will show it. And don't stretch the truth. Interviewers are trained to probe, and inconsistencies are obvious.

## Building the Bank

Aim for **12–15 strong stories**. Most stories can serve **two or three LPs** if you shift the emphasis:

| Story | Primary LPs | Can also show |
|---|---|---|
| Healthcare outage root cause | Customer Obsession, Dive Deep | Ownership, Earn Trust |
| Pushed back on a launch date | Have Backbone | Customer Obsession, Are Right A Lot |
| Missed a migration deadline | Deliver Results (failure) | Earn Trust, Learn and Be Curious |
| Automated account health checks | Invent and Simplify | Frugality, Think Big |
| Admitted a misconfiguration I caused | Earn Trust | Ownership, Insist on Highest Standards |

Include at least **two failure stories**. "Tell me about a time you failed" is a common question, and the strongest answers own the mistake, explain the impact honestly, and describe the **mechanism** you put in place afterward.

---

## Key Takeaway

Use **STAR**, spend most of your time on **Action**, speak in **first person**, and end with **numbers and a lesson**. Build **12–15 detailed, true stories**, each tagged to several LPs, including **failures**. Practice until you can survive three levels of follow-up questions.
