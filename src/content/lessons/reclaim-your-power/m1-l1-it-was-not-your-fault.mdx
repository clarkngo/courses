## Before Anything Else: Your Safety

If you are in danger right now, stop reading and get help.

| If you are... | Reach out to |
|---|---|
| In immediate danger | **911** (US) or your local emergency number |
| Experiencing abuse in a relationship (US) | **National Domestic Violence Hotline**: call **1-800-799-7233** or text **START** to **88788** |
| Affected by sexual violence (US) | **RAINN**: **1-800-656-4673** |
| Thinking about ending your life (US) | **988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline**: call or text **988** |
| Outside the US | **findahelpline.com** lists free, confidential lines by country |

**If someone else can see your phone or computer**, they may be able to see what you read. Think about using a private or incognito window, a device the abuser can't get to (a library, a trusted friend's phone, a work computer), and clearing your history afterward. Hotline advocates can help you plan around monitoring too.

**Need to leave this page fast?** Use the red **Quick Exit** button at the bottom of the screen, or press **Esc** twice. It replaces this page with a weather search and opens a second neutral tab, so pressing Back won't bring the course straight up. It does **not** erase your browser history: this site will still appear there until you clear it, so a private window is still the safer choice.

## Who This Course Is For

This course is for anyone who has been hurt by someone who used power over them. That could be a partner, an ex, a parent, a relative, a caregiver, a boss, or a group. The abuse may have been physical, emotional, verbal, sexual, financial, spiritual, or digital. It may have been loud, or so quiet you're still not sure it "counts."

**It counts.** You don't need bruises, a police report, or a diagnosis to belong here.

It's also for the people who love survivors and want to understand what's going on, so they can help without making things worse.

You might still be in the relationship. You might have left last week or twenty years ago. Every lesson is written to be useful wherever you are.

---

## What This Course Is, and Isn't

This course is **education and self-guided practice**. It gives you language, frameworks, and exercises that survivors and trauma clinicians have found helpful.

It is **not** therapy, legal advice, or a crisis service. If what you read brings up more than you can hold, that isn't a failure. It's a sign to bring in a human: a counselor, an advocate, a support group, or one of the lines above.

<Pitfall title="Pushing Through">

Many survivors learned to ignore their own limits, because ignoring them is how you survive. Don't bring that habit here. If a lesson makes your heart race, your chest tighten, or your mind go blank, **stop**. Look around the room, name five things you can see, and come back another day. Going slowly is not falling behind. Going slowly *is* the work.

</Pitfall>

## Why "Reclaim Your Power"?

Abuse is not mainly about anger, stress, alcohol, or "losing control." At its core, abuse is about **power and control**: one person taking over another person's choices, reality, body, money, relationships, or sense of self.

So recovery is about **getting your choices back**. Not all at once, and not perfectly. One decision at a time, until your life is shaped by what *you* want instead of by fear of someone else's reaction.

In this course, every exercise is optional. You choose what to do, what to skip, and when to stop. That isn't a loophole. It's the point.

## The Map: Three Stages of Recovery

Psychiatrist Judith Herman, in *Trauma and Recovery*, described healing from trauma in three broad stages. This course follows her map:

| Stage | The core question | Where it lives in this course |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Safety and stabilization** | *Am I safe, and can I calm my body down?* | Modules 1–2 |
| **2. Remembrance and mourning** | *What happened to me, and what did I lose?* | Modules 1 and 3 |
| **3. Reconnection** | *Who am I now, and what life do I want?* | Modules 3–4 |

Recovery doesn't move in a straight line. You'll loop back. A court date, an anniversary, or an unexpected text can drop you back into survival mode for a while. That doesn't erase your progress. It shows you which layer needs care next.

## Course Objectives

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

1. **Name the patterns of abuse** you went through, including the less visible ones like coercive control, gaslighting, and financial abuse.
2. **Build a personal safety plan** that covers physical, digital, financial, and emotional safety.
3. **Recognize trauma responses** in your body and use grounding skills to come back to the present.
4. **Notice and answer the inner critic**, the voice that sounds a lot like the person who hurt you.
5. **Set and hold boundaries**, including with people you can't fully cut off.
6. **Rebuild trust and support**, and recognize healthy relationships when you meet them.
7. **Write your own reclaiming plan**, a living document of your values, supports, and next steps.

## The Sentence We Keep Returning To

Hold onto this sentence. Every lesson comes back to it:

> **What happened to you was done *to* you. What happens next can be chosen *by* you.**

The first half puts the responsibility where it belongs. The second half hands your power back.

<TryIt title="A Gentle First Step">

You don't have to write anything down. If you'd like to, keep a private notebook or a note somewhere safe (see the safety note above). Answer only what feels okay:

1. **One word** for how you feel starting this course. Any word is fine, including "nothing" or "skeptical."
2. **One person, place, or thing** that helps you feel even a little safer: a friend, a pet, a song, a park bench.
3. **One reason** you're here. "I don't know yet" is a complete answer.

That's it. You've already made three choices of your own.

</TryIt>

**Abuse is defined by power and control, not by physical violence. Controlling money and monitoring someone's phone are recognized forms of abuse.**

Many survivors downplay non-physical abuse because it doesn't match the picture they had of "real" abuse. Financial control and digital monitoring take away someone's independence and choices, and research on coercive control shows these patterns often come before or alongside physical danger. You don't need injuries to deserve support.
