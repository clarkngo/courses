## Who Are You Now?

Abuse often takes over your identity. Your preferences, opinions, hobbies, and dreams get pushed aside to make room for someone else's needs and moods. Survivors often say, *"I don't even know who I am anymore."*

That isn't a dead end. It's a starting point. This lesson is about rediscovering, or discovering for the first time, the person whose choices your life should follow: **you**.

---

## Reconnecting with Your Values

**Values** are what matter most to you: the qualities you want to guide how you live. They aren't goals you finish. They're directions you keep moving in.

Examples: *honesty, creativity, independence, family, adventure, faith, learning, kindness, justice, health, humor, peace.*

Ways to find yours:

- **Remember who you were before.** What did you love? What made you lose track of time?
- **Notice what makes you angry.** Anger often points to a value that got violated. If injustice makes you furious, you may value fairness.
- **Notice who you admire.** The qualities you admire in others are often your own values.
- **Try things.** A class, a hobby, a new place. You're allowed to experiment and change your mind.

---

## Retelling Your Story

The abuser told a story about you: that you were the problem, too sensitive, unlovable, lucky to have them. **You get to tell a different, truer story.**

Narrative therapists call this **re-authoring**. It doesn't mean pretending the abuse didn't happen or that it was "for the best." It means shifting the center of the story:

| The abuser's story | Your re-authored story |
|---|---|
| "You're weak." | "I survived something designed to break me." |
| "You made me do it." | "They chose to abuse. I chose, again and again, to keep going." |
| "No one will believe you." | "My experience is real whether or not everyone understands it." |
| "You're nothing without me." | "I'm finding out how much I am without them." |

## Post-Traumatic Growth

Psychologists Richard Tedeschi and Lawrence Calhoun studied **post-traumatic growth**: positive changes some people experience *through* their struggle with trauma. These can include deeper relationships, a clearer sense of their own strength, new priorities, and a greater appreciation for life.

Two important notes:

1. **Growth doesn't make the abuse okay.** It shows *your* strength, not the value of what was done to you.
2. **You don't owe anyone growth.** Some days, just surviving is the achievement. Healing doesn't have to become an inspiring story.

## Planning for Hard Days

Healing isn't a straight line. Expect hard days, especially around:

- Anniversaries, holidays, or court dates
- Seeing or hearing from the person who hurt you
- New relationships, or conflict in safe ones
- Big life changes, even good ones

A hard day isn't starting over. **You're not back at the beginning. You're revisiting a layer from a new place,** with more skills, more support, and more knowledge than you had before.

<Pitfall title="Treating Setbacks as Proof You've Failed">

The inner critic loves a bad day: *"See? You're not healing at all."* Watch for this all-or-nothing thinking. Instead, look at the bigger trend over months, not days. Are triggers a little less intense? Do you recover a little faster? Did you use a grounding skill? That's progress, even if today was hard.

</Pitfall>

## Capstone: Your Reclaiming Plan

This is your living document. Bring together what you've built across the course. Keep it somewhere safe and revisit it every few months. Change anything, anytime.

<TryIt title="Write Your Reclaiming Plan">

Answer whatever parts feel right. Skip anything you're not ready for.

1. **My safety basics (Module 2):** my signal, my safe person, and my top safety step.
2. **My grounding kit (Module 2):** the two skills that help me most.
3. **My kinder voice (Module 3):** one critical thought, and how I answer it now.
4. **My boundaries (Module 3):** one boundary I'm keeping, and how I follow through.
5. **My circles (Module 4):** my inner-circle people and one connection I'm building.
6. **My values:** three values I want my life to follow.
7. **My hard-day plan:** warning signs I'm struggling, what helps, and who I'll contact.
8. **My next chosen step:** one thing I'll do in the next month that's mine, just because I want to.

Finish by writing the course sentence in your own words:

> *What happened to me was done to me. What happens next, I choose.*

</TryIt>
