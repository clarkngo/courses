## Speaking Ship

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Fore / forward** | Toward the bow (front) |
| **Aft** | Toward the stern (back) |
| **Port** | Left side, looking forward |
| **Starboard** | Right side, looking forward |
| **Inboard / outboard** | Toward / away from the centerline |
| **Centerline (CL)** | Fore-and-aft line down the middle of the ship |
| **Baseline (BL)** | Horizontal reference line, usually at the bottom of the keel; heights are measured from it |
| **Frame numbers** | Stations along the length, numbered (usually from aft forward); "Fr. 42" locates a point fore and aft |
| **Molded line** | Theoretical surface of the structure, often the inside of the shell plating; dimensions are measured to molded lines |
| **Half-breadth** | Distance from the centerline to a point, measured athwartships (side to side) |

Any point on a ship can be located by **frame number** (fore and aft), **distance off centerline** (side to side), and **height above baseline** (up and down).

---

## Drawing Types

| Drawing | Shows |
|---|---|
| **General arrangement (GA)** | Overall layout of spaces |
| **Lines plan / offsets** | Hull shape as sections, waterlines, buttocks; offset tables give coordinates |
| **Midship section** | Typical cross-section with scantlings (member sizes) |
| **Shell expansion** | The curved shell flattened out, showing every plate, seam, and butt |
| **Construction / structural drawings** | Each block, deck, or bulkhead, with members and welds |
| **Production / work packages** | Cut parts, assembly sequence, weld details for the crew |

Modern yards generate most production drawings from a **3D product model** (CAD), and many parts are cut by **CNC plasma or laser** directly from that model.

### The Golden Rule: Arrow Side vs. Other Side

- Weld symbol **below** the reference line → weld on the **arrow side** of the joint
- Weld symbol **above** the line → weld on the **other side**
- Symbols on **both** sides → weld **both** sides

### Common Weld Symbols

| Symbol | Weld |
|---|---|
| Right triangle | **Fillet** weld |
| Two parallel lines | **Square groove** |
| V shape | **V-groove** |
| Single slanted line with vertical | **Bevel groove** |
| Small circle at arrow junction | Weld **all around** |
| Flag at arrow junction | **Field weld** (done at erection or on site) |

### Size, Length, and Pitch

- **Size** goes to the **left** of the symbol (for a fillet, the leg length).
- **Length-pitch** goes to the **right**. "2-6" means **2-inch** welds on **6-inch** centers (intermittent).
- No length means a **continuous** weld for the full joint.
- **Chain intermittent** (welds opposite each other on both sides) vs. **staggered intermittent** (offset) are shown by how the symbols line up above and below the line.

Ship drawings may use AWS symbols, ISO 2553 symbols, or the yard's own standard, so check the drawing's **legend**.

<Pitfall title="Welding the Wrong Side">

**Common mistake:** reading the fillet triangle without noticing whether it's above or below the reference line.

Above means the **other side**. Welding the wrong side of a tee joint leaves the designed side unwelded, and an inspector will reject it (or worse, it'll be missed). Read every symbol: **side, size, length, pitch, process**.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

Locate any point by **frame number**, **distance off centerline**, and **height above baseline**. Know the main drawings: **GA, lines, midship section, shell expansion, and structural drawings**. On welding symbols, **below the line = arrow side**, **above = other side**; **size left, length-pitch right**; flags mean **field weld**, circles mean **all around**.

<TryIt title="Read These Symbols">

1. A fillet triangle **above** the line with **5/16** on the left and nothing on the right. *(5/16-inch continuous fillet weld on the other side.)*
2. Fillet triangles **above and below**, aligned, with **1/4** and **3-12** on each. *(1/4-inch chain intermittent fillet welds on both sides, 3 inches long on 12-inch centers.)*
3. A V-groove symbol below the line with a **flag** at the arrow junction. *(V-groove weld on the arrow side, made in the field.)*

</TryIt>
