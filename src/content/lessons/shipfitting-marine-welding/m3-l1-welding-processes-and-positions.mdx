## Arc Welding Basics

Arc welding uses an electric arc between an electrode and the work to melt the base metal and filler metal together. The molten pool must be **shielded** from the air, or oxygen and nitrogen cause porosity and brittle welds. Processes differ in how they **feed filler** and **shield** the pool.

---

## The Main Processes

| Process | Common name | Shielding | Where it shines in a shipyard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **SMAW** | Stick | Flux coating on the electrode | Repairs, outdoor work, tight spots, all positions; portable |
| **FCAW** | Flux-core | Flux inside a tubular wire (often plus CO₂ or mixed gas) | **The workhorse** of most steel shipyards: fast, all-position, tolerant of conditions |
| **GMAW** | MIG | Shielding gas (Ar/CO₂ mixes) | Thin steel, **aluminum** (pulsed spray), robotic welding |
| **GTAW** | TIG | Argon | Pipe roots, stainless, copper-nickel, critical thin work; slow but precise |
| **SAW** | Sub-arc | Granular flux blanket | **Panel lines**: long, flat, thick butt and fillet welds; very high deposition |

Other processes you'll see: **electrogas** and **electroslag** for vertical seams on thick side shell, **stud welding** for insulation pins and fasteners, and **friction stir welding** for aluminum panels.

### DCEP, DCEN, AC

- **DCEP** (electrode positive, "reverse polarity"): deeper penetration; used for most SMAW and GMAW and many FCAW wires
- **DCEN** (electrode negative): used for some FCAW self-shielded wires and GTAW of steel
- **AC**: some stick electrodes and GTAW of aluminum

Always set polarity as the electrode or wire manufacturer and the **welding procedure** specify.

## Decoding Filler Classifications (AWS)

### Stick Electrode: E7018

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **E** | Electrode |
| **70** | Minimum tensile strength **70 ksi** |
| **1** | **All positions** (a 2 means flat and horizontal only) |
| **8** | Coating type and current: **low-hydrogen**, iron powder, AC or DCEP |

E7018 is a **low-hydrogen** electrode, important for avoiding hydrogen cracking in ship steels. It must be kept **dry** in a rod oven once the can is opened.

### Flux-Cored Wire: E71T-1

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **E** | Electrode |
| **7** | **70 ksi** minimum tensile |
| **1** | **All positions** (a 0 means flat and horizontal) |
| **T** | **Tubular** (flux-cored) |
| **-1** | Usability group (here, gas-shielded, typically with CO₂) |

Additional suffixes (like **C** or **M** for gas type, and **H8** for diffusible hydrogen) appear on many wires.

### Solid Wire: ER70S-6

**ER** = electrode or rod, **70** = 70 ksi, **S** = solid, **6** = chemistry (extra deoxidizers for welding over light mill scale).

## Welding Positions

| Code | Groove (plate) | Fillet |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **1G** Flat | **1F** Flat |
| **2** | **2G** Horizontal | **2F** Horizontal |
| **3** | **3G** Vertical (up or down) | **3F** Vertical |
| **4** | **4G** Overhead | **4F** Overhead |

Pipe positions include **5G** (horizontal fixed pipe, weld goes around) and **6G** (pipe fixed at 45°). A welder qualified in harder positions is often qualified for easier ones too, according to the applicable code.

Shipbuilding involves lots of **vertical and overhead** welding at erection joints, which is why welder qualifications in **3G and 4G** (or 3F and 4F) are valued.

<Pitfall title="Rod Oven? It's Just a Box">

**Common mistake:** leaving E7018 electrodes out on the deck overnight in humid Northwest air, then using them in the morning.

Low-hydrogen coatings absorb moisture, which puts **hydrogen** into the weld and can cause **delayed cracking** hours or days later, especially on higher-strength or thick steel. Store electrodes in a **rod oven** at the specified temperature, follow exposure limits, and re-bake or discard as the procedure requires.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

**FCAW** is the shipyard workhorse; **SAW** runs panel lines; **SMAW** handles repairs; **GMAW** does aluminum and robotics; **GTAW** does pipe roots and special metals. **E7018** = 70 ksi, all-position, low-hydrogen. **E71T-1** = 70 ksi, all-position, flux-cored. Positions run **1 (flat) to 4 (overhead)**, **G** for groove and **F** for fillet. Keep low-hydrogen consumables **dry**.
