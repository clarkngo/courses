## Common Weld Defects

| Defect | What it is | Common causes |
|---|---|---|
| **Porosity** | Gas pockets in the weld | Wind blowing away shielding gas, moisture, dirty surface, wrong gas flow |
| **Undercut** | Groove melted into base metal at the toe and not filled | Too much amperage, wrong angle, too fast travel |
| **Overlap (cold lap)** | Weld metal rolls over the base metal without fusing | Too slow travel, low heat, wrong angle |
| **Incomplete fusion** | Weld didn't melt into base metal or previous pass | Low heat, poor technique, mill scale, wrong angle |
| **Incomplete penetration** | Weld didn't reach through the root | Tight gap, thick land, low heat |
| **Slag inclusion** | Trapped flux in the weld | Poor interpass cleaning, wrong technique |
| **Cracks** | Fractures in the weld or HAZ | Hydrogen, high restraint, wrong filler, fast cooling, crater cracks from stopping abruptly |
| **Excessive reinforcement / convexity** | Too much weld built up | Technique; creates stress concentration |
| **Arc strikes** | Arc touched base metal outside the joint | Carelessness; can start cracks in some steels |

**Cracks are never acceptable** in structural welds. Most other defects have **acceptance limits** set by the applicable rules or specifications.

## Inspection Methods

| Method | Abbreviation | Finds | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visual testing** | VT | Surface defects, size, profile | The first and most important inspection; done on 100% of welds |
| **Magnetic particle** | MT | Surface and slightly subsurface cracks | Ferromagnetic steel only |
| **Liquid (dye) penetrant** | PT | Surface-breaking defects | Works on aluminum, stainless, and steel |
| **Ultrasonic testing** | UT | Internal defects, lack of fusion | Requires skilled technicians; phased array (PAUT) is common |
| **Radiographic testing** | RT | Internal porosity, slag, lack of penetration | X-ray or gamma; radiation safety exclusion zones |

Classification rules and contracts specify **which welds** get which NDT, and **how much** (often a percentage of critical joints, plus 100% of specific locations).

## The Qualification System

Before production welding, the yard proves that its **procedure** works and that its **welders** can perform it.

| Document | What it is |
|---|---|
| **WPS (Welding Procedure Specification)** | The instruction sheet: process, base metal, filler, position, preheat, amperage range, travel speed, gas, joint design |
| **PQR (Procedure Qualification Record)** | Proof: the actual test weld parameters and mechanical test results (tensile, bend, impact) that support the WPS |
| **WPQ (Welder Performance Qualification)** | Proof that a specific welder can make sound welds in the given process, position, and thickness range |

### Who Sets the Rules?

- **Commercial ships:** classification society rules (**ABS, DNV, Lloyd's Register**, and others), often with **AWS** standards for structural welding
- **U.S. Navy ships:** **NAVSEA** technical publications (for example, the welding qualification standard known in yards as **"Tech Pub 248"**) and related military standards
- **U.S. Coast Guard** oversight for many U.S.-flagged commercial vessels

Welders must stay within their **qualified ranges** and maintain **continuity** (welding regularly in the process) or requalify.

<Pitfall title="Grinding a Crack Smooth">

**Common mistake:** a welder sees a small crack in a crater, grinds the surface smooth so it "looks fine," and welds over it.

Cracks can run deeper than they look and will grow under load. The crack must be **completely removed** (verified by MT or PT), then repaired following an **approved repair procedure**, and re-inspected.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

Know the defects: **porosity, undercut, overlap, incomplete fusion and penetration, slag, and cracks**. **Cracks are never acceptable.** **VT** is first and always; **MT/PT** find surface cracks; **UT/RT** find internal defects. Production welds follow an approved **WPS**, backed by a **PQR**, made by a **qualified welder**, under **class society, NAVSEA, or USCG** rules.

<TryIt title="Diagnose the Defect">

1. A fillet weld on an outdoor deck is full of small round holes. *(Porosity, likely wind blowing away shielding gas. Use screens or windbreaks, or switch to a self-shielded process approved for the job.)*
2. A groove weld's root isn't fused on the back side. *(Incomplete penetration: check root gap, land, amperage, and technique.)*
3. A notch runs along the toe of a fillet weld. *(Undercut: lower amperage, adjust the angle, slow down slightly.)*

</TryIt>
