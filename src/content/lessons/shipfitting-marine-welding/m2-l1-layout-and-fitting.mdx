## What a Shipfitter Does

The **shipfitter** reads the drawings, lays out where every piece goes, positions plates and shapes, pulls them into alignment, and **tack welds** them so the welder can make the final welds. A good fit-up makes good welding possible. A bad one can't be fixed with welding.

## Layout Tools

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| **Tape measure, steel rule** | Basic measurements; check tape accuracy |
| **Framing square, combination square** | Right angles and short layouts |
| **Soapstone, paint marker, center punch** | Marking lines on steel |
| **Chalk line** | Long straight lines on plate |
| **Levels and plumb bobs** | Vertical and horizontal checks |
| **Transit, laser level, total station** | Long reference lines, heights, block alignment |
| **Templates and battens** | Curved shapes; fairing curves |

Layout always starts from **reference lines** (such as the centerline, a frame line, or a deck height above baseline), never from the edge of a plate that might not be square.

---

## Fitting Tools

| Tool | Use |
|---|---|
| **C-clamps, pipe clamps, toggle clamps** | Holding small parts in position |
| **Dogs and wedges** | A **dog** (a small steel bracket tacked to one plate) gives a wedge something to push against to **pull plates flush** or into line |
| **Strongbacks** | A stiff beam tacked across a joint to pull plates flat and hold alignment |
| **Hydraulic jacks, porta-power** | Push members into position with controlled force |
| **Come-alongs, chain falls** | Pull members into position |
| **Fitting bars, pry bars** | Fine adjustments |
| **Clips and spacers** | Set consistent root gaps |

When fitting aids like dogs and strongbacks are removed, their **tack welds must be ground flush and inspected** (and sometimes tested) so they don't leave cracks or gouges in the structure.

---

## What to Check Before Handing Over

| Check | Why |
|---|---|
| **Root gap** within the weld procedure's range | Too tight = lack of penetration; too wide = excess weld, distortion, burn-through |
| **Bevel angle and land** correct | Groove geometry controls fusion and penetration |
| **Alignment (misalignment / high-low)** within tolerance | Offsets concentrate stress and cause fatigue cracks |
| **Member location** to layout lines | Wrong location means wrong strength or interference |
| **Angles and square** | Tee members must be at the specified angle |
| **Cleanliness** | Mill scale, rust, paint, oil, and moisture cause weld defects |

### Tack Welds

Tack welds are part of the final weld in many procedures, so they must be:

- Made by a **qualified welder** with the **approved process and filler**
- **Sound** (no cracks); cracked tacks must be removed
- Correct **size and spacing**, enough to hold but not so large they interfere
- **Preheated** when the base metal requires preheat

## Fairing

**Fairing** means making plates and members smooth and true, with no bumps, dents, or "hungry horse" waviness between frames. Fitters fair plate using strongbacks, jacks, and, under controlled procedures, **heat straightening** (line heating). Heat straightening on ship steels must follow the yard's approved temperature limits, because overheating can change the steel's properties.

<Pitfall title="Welding Can Fill the Gap">

**Common mistake:** a fitter leaves a gap far wider than the procedure allows, figuring the welder will "fill it up."

Oversized gaps use far more weld metal, add heat and **distortion**, risk burn-through, and may not be covered by the weld procedure, which means the weld may be **unacceptable** no matter how good it looks. Fix the fit, or get an approved repair procedure (such as buttering the edge) before welding.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

Shipfitters **lay out** from **reference lines**, **position and pull** parts with **dogs, wedges, strongbacks, and jacks**, **check** gaps, bevels, and alignment, and **tack** with approved procedures. Good fit-up makes good welds possible. Remove fitting aids carefully, and fair plate within approved limits.

<TryIt title="Fit-Up Inspection">

A butt joint in 1/2-inch plate has a weld procedure calling for a **60° included angle**, **1/8-inch root gap** (tolerance ±1/16 inch), and **maximum misalignment of 1/8 inch**.

You measure: angle **60°**, gap **1/4 inch**, misalignment **1/16 inch**.

Is it acceptable? *(No. The 1/4-inch gap exceeds the maximum of 3/16 inch. Re-fit the joint or use an approved procedure to correct it before welding.)*

</TryIt>
