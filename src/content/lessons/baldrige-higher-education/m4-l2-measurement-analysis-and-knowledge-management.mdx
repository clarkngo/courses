## The Foundation of the System

Category 4 (90 points) sits under everything else in the framework diagram. Without reliable measures, analysis, and knowledge, **management by fact** isn't possible.

- **4.1 Measurement, Analysis, Review, and Improvement of Organizational Performance (45):** which **measures** you track, where your **comparative data** comes from, how you keep measures **agile**, how leaders **review** performance, how you **project** future performance, and how reviews lead to **improvement**.
- **4.2 Information and Knowledge Management (45):** how you manage **knowledge** and share **best practices**, ensure **data quality, availability, and security** (including cybersecurity), and pursue **innovation**.

## Choosing Measures

STC tracks about 20 key measures. A few from its scorecard:

| Measure | Type | Frequency | Comparison | Linked to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Week-2 LMS login for new students | Leading | Weekly | Own trend | SO2 |
| Gateway course DFW rate | Leading | Quarterly | Peer institutions via a shared consortium | SO2 |
| First-year persistence, online | Lagging | Quarterly / annual | National Student Clearinghouse | SO2 |
| Six-year completion, part-time transfer students | Lagging | Annual | IPEDS Outcome Measures | 7.1 |
| Graduate employment in field within six months | Lagging | Annual | Labor-market data; peer programs | 7.1, SO1 |
| Adjunct onboarding completion | Leading | Quarterly | Own target | SO4 |
| Adjunct engagement index | Lagging | Annual | Pulse benchmark from survey vendor | SO4 |
| Cost per credit hour | Lagging | Quarterly | Other schools in the university; instructional-cost benchmarking studies | SO5 |

**Leading** measures give early warning that you can act on this quarter. **Lagging** measures confirm whether outcomes improved. A scorecard with only lagging measures tells you what went wrong a year too late.

**Measurement agility** means you can add or change measures quickly. When STC launched AI tutoring, it added "tutor sessions per student" and "help-desk tickets on AI tools" in the same quarter.

## Reviews That Lead to Action

4.1b asks how senior leaders **review** performance and capabilities, and **4.1c** asks how reviews lead to **improvement** and how you **project** future performance.

At STC's monthly leadership review, each action-plan owner reports **status against projection** in one slide: *on track*, *at risk*, or *off track*. Only at-risk and off-track items get discussion time. Each review ends with a list of **decisions**: what changes, who owns it, and when it'll be checked.

Projections connect measurement to strategy. In the quarterly review, the team compares actual persistence with the projection in the SO2 action plan. If the gap is growing, it adjusts the plan (2.2b) or reallocates resources.

## Managing Knowledge

Knowledge at a school is easily lost, because so much lives in individual instructors' heads and course sections. STC manages it deliberately:

- **Master course shells.** Every section is built from a shared master. Improvements made in one section are reviewed and added to the master, so the next 15 sections benefit.
- **Teaching circles.** Full-time and adjunct faculty meet each quarter by course cluster to share what worked.
- **Retirement handoffs.** Retiring faculty co-teach with their successor for a quarter and record walkthroughs of key labs.
- **Best-practice transfer.** When one program's early-alert follow-up rate hit 95%, its process was documented and adopted by the other programs.

## Data Quality, Security, and AI Tools

- **One definition per measure.** "Persistence" means the same thing in the dean's dashboard, the program review, and the NWCCU report. The definitions live in a shared data dictionary.
- **Access by role**, following FERPA: instructors see their own sections, advisors see their caseload, and leaders see aggregates.
- **Cybersecurity** of student and research data is managed with the university's IT security office and tested through regular phishing exercises and access reviews.
- **AI tools** are reviewed before adoption: what data they use, where it's stored, whether vendors can train on it, and whether outputs are checked for bias across student segments.

<Pitfall title="Dashboards Nobody Acts On">

**Common mistake:** STC builds an impressive dashboard with 70 charts. Leaders look at it in meetings and say "interesting," and nothing changes.

Measures earn their place by driving **decisions**. For each measure, ask: *Who reviews it, how often, against what target and comparison, and what do they do when it's off?* If no one can answer, drop the measure or fix the review.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

Category 4 is the **foundation**. Choose a small set of **leading and lagging** measures linked to objectives, each with a **comparison**. Review them on a **defined cadence** that ends in **decisions**, and **project** future performance. Manage **knowledge** through master shells, teaching circles, and handoffs. Keep data **accurate, consistently defined, secure, and governed**, including any AI tools that use student data.
