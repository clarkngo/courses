## What Category 1 Asks

Category 1 (115 points) has two items:

- **1.1 Senior Leadership (65):** how senior leaders set and deploy **vision and values**, promote **legal and ethical behavior**, **communicate** and engage people, and create an environment where the mission succeeds with a **focus on action**.
- **1.2 Governance and Societal Contributions (50):** how you make governance **accountable**, evaluate leaders and the governance system, meet **legal, regulatory, and accreditation** requirements, behave **ethically**, and contribute to **society**.

For a school, the "senior leaders" are usually the **dean and the school's leadership team**: associate dean, program directors, and the director of operations or student success. The questions don't change because you're one school inside a larger university. You describe *your* leadership system and how it connects to the university's.

| Step | What STC's leaders actually do |
|---|---|
| **1 Set direction** | Each summer the leadership team, with the faculty council, reviews mission, vision, and values against the Organizational Profile and sets the year's priorities. |
| **2 Communicate** | Monthly all-faculty meeting (recorded for adjuncts), a quarterly "dean's open hour" for students, a weekly adjunct newsletter, and **listening channels**: anonymous pulse surveys and a program-director check-in with every new adjunct. |
| **3 Build the environment** | Protected time for course redesign, a small innovation fund for teaching pilots, and an explicit statement that a failed, well-run pilot isn't a career risk. |
| **4 Focus on action** | Five strategic objectives, each with an owner and quarterly targets (Lesson 3.2), reviewed monthly by the leadership team. |
| **5 Review and learn** | An annual 360-degree review of the dean, a yearly faculty-council review of the leadership system, and changes based on what's learned. |

Notice step 2. Baldrige asks for **two-way** communication and for engaging **all** workforce segments. At STC, adjuncts teach most sections, so a leadership system that only reaches full-time faculty misses most of the people who face students.

## Governance at the School Level

**1.2a** asks how your governance system ensures **accountability** for leaders' actions, **fiscal** accountability, **transparency**, independent **audits**, and protection of **stakeholder interests**. It also asks how you **evaluate** senior leaders and the governance system.

At STC, the board of trustees governs the university, and the school's governance sits within that. Describe how it works:

- **Who holds the dean accountable**, and how: the Provost, through an annual review against school objectives
- **Shared governance**: what the faculty council decides (curriculum, academic standards) and what it advises on (budget, hiring)
- **Advisory, not governance**: the industry advisory board advises; it has no decision rights, and that should be clear
- **Fiscal accountability**: monthly budget review with the university's finance office

## Legal, Regulatory, Accreditation, and Ethical Behavior

STC keeps a **compliance calendar** tied to its P.1 regulatory table: NWCCU reporting dates, ABET evidence collection, Title IV and state authorization checks, accessibility audits of course shells, and FERPA training for every new instructor.

**1.2b** also asks how you **anticipate** public concerns and how you promote and monitor **ethical behavior**. In a computing school in 2026, two issues stand out:

- **AI and academic integrity.** STC publishes a school-wide AI-use framework. Each syllabus states what level of AI use is allowed for each assignment, and faculty get training on designing assessments that hold up.
- **Student data and learning analytics.** Early-alert tools use LMS data. STC states what's collected, who sees it, and how it's used, and a data-governance group reviews new tools before adoption.

Measures STC tracks for this item (reported in 7.4): accreditation and audit findings, accessibility-audit pass rate for course shells, FERPA-training completion, and the number and outcome of academic-integrity cases.

## Societal Contributions

**1.2c** asks how you consider **societal well-being** in strategy and daily operations, and how you **support your key communities**. For STC:

- A **community cyber clinic** where students run free security checkups for local nonprofits, supervised by faculty
- **Pathway partnerships** with community colleges and high schools to widen access to tech careers
- **Workforce diversity** goals for the regional tech pipeline, tracked by program
- **Environmental impact**: auto-shutdown of idle cloud lab resources, which cuts both cost and energy use

<Pitfall title="Broadcast Without Listening">

**Common mistake:** STC's leaders point to a monthly newsletter, a town hall, and an all-faculty email as their "communication and engagement" approach.

Those are all **one-way**. Baldrige asks how leaders *engage* the workforce and students, which means listening and responding. Ask: *What did leaders learn from faculty, staff, and students this year, and what did they change because of it?* If you can't name an example, especially one that came from adjuncts or online students, the approach isn't really two-way.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

Category 1 asks how senior leaders **set direction, communicate two-way, build an environment for success, and focus on action**, and how **governance** keeps leaders accountable. For a school inside a university, describe *your* leadership system and how it connects to the university. Cover **legal, regulatory, and accreditation** compliance, **ethics** (including AI use and student data), and **societal contributions**. Make sure the system reaches the people who teach most of your sections.
