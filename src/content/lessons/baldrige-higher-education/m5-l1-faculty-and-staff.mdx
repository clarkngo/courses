## What Category 5 Asks

Category 5 (85 points) is about the people who do the work. In the Education Criteria, that's **faculty and staff**, plus any student workers or volunteers who do core work.

- **5.1 Workforce Environment (40):** **capability and capacity** (do you have the right skills and enough people?), how you **recruit, hire, and onboard**, how you manage **change** in the workforce, and how you ensure **health, security, accessibility, benefits, and fair compensation**.
- **5.2 Workforce Engagement (45):** how you determine the **drivers of engagement** for each segment, assess engagement, build a culture of open communication and high performance, manage **performance**, and support **learning, development, and career progression**.

---

## Capability and Capacity

**Capability** is whether your people have the skills you need. **Capacity** is whether you have enough of them at the right times. STC checks both each spring, before it schedules the next year:

| Area | Capability question | Capacity question |
|---|---|---|
| **AI and machine learning** | How many faculty can teach applied AI and assess responsible AI use (SO1)? | Can we staff the new certificate's sections at four starts a year? |
| **Cybersecurity** | Do instructors hold current industry certifications? | Can the cyber range support enrollment growth? |
| **Advising and coaching** | Are coaches trained for transfer, military, and international students? | Is each coach's caseload at 150 or fewer (SO2)? |
| **Instructional design** | Can designers build accessible, AI-resilient assessments? | Can two designers support three gateway redesigns this year? |

The result is a **workforce plan** linked to Category 2's action plans: which roles to hire, which skills to develop, and which partners (such as employers who loan practitioner faculty) can fill gaps.

**Succession** is part of capability. Five of STC's 22 full-time faculty will retire within five years, including the only two who teach operating systems. The plan pairs each with a successor a year ahead (see the knowledge handoffs in Lesson 4.2).

Credentialing matters for accreditation too. NWCCU expects faculty to be qualified for what they teach, and ABET looks at faculty competence. Keep a record for each instructor of how their degree or professional experience qualifies them for each course.

## A Supportive Environment

5.1 also asks about the **workplace environment**: health, safety, security, accessibility, and **benefits and compensation**, including whether compensation is **fair**.

For STC, the honest issue is **adjunct pay and predictability**. Pay per course is set by the university, but STC controls *predictability*. It now offers adjuncts their teaching assignments two quarters ahead, and it guarantees a cancellation fee if a section is cut within two weeks of the start date. That change came straight from adjunct pulse-survey comments.

## Engagement Drivers by Segment

Baldrige asks you to **determine** what drives engagement for each segment, not assume it. STC used focus groups and pulse surveys, and found:

| Segment | Top engagement drivers found | What STC changed |
|---|---|---|
| **Full-time faculty** | Curriculum ownership; time for professional currency | Faculty-led course clusters; one professional-development day per quarter |
| **Adjunct faculty** | Predictable assignments; belonging; good course shells | Assignments two quarters ahead; adjuncts invited to teaching circles and paid to attend |
| **Advisors and coaches** | Manageable caseloads; seeing student impact | Caseload cap; quarterly "student stories" sharing |
| **Instructional designers** | Being involved early; respect as experts | Designers join course projects at kickoff, not at the end |

Then **assess** engagement with a measure for each segment, report it in **7.3 Workforce Results**, and look for **dissatisfaction** separately. Exit interviews with adjuncts who stopped teaching for STC turned out to be the best source.

## Performance, Learning, and Development

- **Performance management** supports high performance and engagement. Full-time faculty set annual goals tied to school objectives, and adjunct reviews draw on student feedback, peer observation, and outcome data (such as DFW rates compared with other sections).
- **Learning and development** cover both what the school needs and what people need for their careers. STC's 2026–27 priority is **teaching in the AI era**: designing assessments that hold up when students have AI, teaching responsible AI use, and using AI for feedback. Every instructor completes a short course, and faculty "AI fellows" coach colleagues.
- **Effectiveness** of development is evaluated, not assumed. STC checks whether trained instructors' courses show better assessment quality and student results.
- **Career progression** for staff includes a coach-to-senior-coach path. For faculty, it's a clear path from adjunct to lead adjunct to full-time openings.

<Pitfall title="Treating Adjuncts as Outside the System">

**Common mistake:** STC's workforce section describes hiring, engagement, and development for its 22 full-time faculty and 18 staff. Adjuncts get one sentence.

But adjuncts teach most of the sections students experience. If they're not in your workforce **segments**, **engagement measures**, and **development plans**, Category 5 misses most of the people who teach. Examiners will notice the gap between the Organizational Profile (110 adjuncts) and the workforce answers.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

Category 5 asks whether you have the **capability and capacity** you need, linked to strategy and succession. It also asks whether the **environment** is safe, accessible, and fair, whether you know each **segment's engagement drivers** and act on them, and whether **performance management and development** build the skills the school needs, now including AI-era teaching. In an adjunct-heavy school, the **adjunct pipeline** is a key workforce process with measures at every step.

<TryIt title="Find Your Engagement Drivers">

Pick your two largest workforce segments. For each:

1. Write down what you **think** drives their engagement.
2. Ask five people in that segment (short conversations or a two-question pulse): *What makes you want to do your best work here? What gets in the way?*
3. Compare. Where were you wrong?
4. Choose **one change** you could make this quarter, and one **measure** to see whether it worked.

</TryIt>
