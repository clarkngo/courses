## What Category 6 Asks

Category 6 (85 points) is about how the work gets done:

- **6.1 Work Processes (40):** how you determine **requirements** for your educational programs, services, and key work processes, how you **design** them, how you **manage** them day to day with in-process measures, how you manage **support processes**, and how you **improve** them.
- **6.2 Operational Effectiveness (45):** how you control **cost** and keep processes **efficient and effective**, manage the **supply network**, and ensure **safety, business continuity, and resilience**.

In 2023–2024, 6.2 gained points because it now covers the supply network, continuity, and risk. (Cybersecurity is handled in 4.2.)

## Requirements and In-Process Measures

For each key work process, define **requirements** (drawn from student, employer, accreditation, and regulatory needs) and the **in-process measures** that tell you during the term, not after it, whether you're meeting them.

| Key work process | Key requirements | In-process measures | Outcome measures (Cat. 7) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Design programs** | Employer-aligned outcomes; transfer-friendly; accessible; AI-resilient assessment | Advisory-board review completed; accessibility check passed before launch | Employer ratings of graduates; time to degree |
| **2 Deliver courses** | Consistent quality across sections; instructor presence; working labs | Instructor feedback within 72 hours; week-2 engagement; lab uptime | DFW rate; course satisfaction by segment |
| **3 Assess learning** | Valid, rubric-based assessment of program outcomes; usable for ABET and NWCCU | Rubric scoring calibration (inter-rater agreement); share of sections submitting data | Outcome attainment by program and segment |
| **4 Support student success** | Proactive, available outside 9-to-5; fits each segment | Alert follow-up within 48 hours; coach contact by week 3 | Persistence; completion |
| **5 Connect to careers** | Internships and capstones with real employers; career support for international students | Capstone sponsors secured by week 4 | Employment in field within six months |

**Design** is where AI shows up in operations. STC's course-design standard now requires each major assessment to state its AI-use level and to include at least one element AI can't do for the student: an oral defense, a live demonstration, or a reflection on the student's own process.

**Improvement** uses the PDSA cycle from P.2c. Gateway course redesign (SO2, action plan B) is an example: analyze DFW data by topic, redesign the hardest two weeks, run it across all sections, and compare.

## Operational Effectiveness (6.2)

### Cost control and efficiency

| Cost driver | STC's approach |
|---|---|
| **Cloud lab spend** | Per-course budgets, auto-shutdown of idle resources, monthly spend review by course; cost per student-lab-hour tracked |
| **Low-enrollment sections** | Decisions made six weeks before start (not one week), so students and adjuncts aren't disrupted; cross-listing graduate and undergraduate sections where outcomes allow |
| **Rework** | Master shells mean course fixes are made once, not 15 times |

### Supply-network management

STC sets **requirements** for key suppliers and checks performance: LMS uptime and support response, cloud-lab provider reliability and pricing, and transfer-credit turnaround from articulation partners. It gives suppliers **feedback** at an annual review. The cloud-lab provider contract includes an **exit plan**, so labs could move to another provider within one quarter if needed.

### Safety, business continuity, and resilience

STC's continuity plan covers the disruptions that are most likely or most damaging:

| Disruption | Plan |
|---|---|
| **Earthquake or building closure** (Seattle) | Every hybrid course can switch to fully online within 48 hours; course shells are built to support this from the start |
| **LMS outage during finals** | Published fallback: email submission and extended deadlines; vendor escalation path |
| **Cloud lab outage** | Downloadable local lab images for core courses |
| **Sudden loss of key faculty** | Every course has a designated backup instructor and a complete master shell |
| **Enrollment shock** (e.g., visa policy) | Triggers in 2.2b to re-plan; contingency budget reserve |

Each plan is **tested**. STC runs one tabletop exercise a year and does a live switch-to-online drill for one week each fall.

<Pitfall title="Listing Every Activity as a 'Key Process'">

**Common mistake:** STC's first process list had 46 "key work processes," from "order textbooks" to "approve travel."

Key work processes are the **few** (usually four to seven) that create value for students and other customers. Everything else is a support process. With 46 key processes, none get the design rigor, measures, and improvement attention the important ones need.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

Category 6 asks you to name your **few key work processes** and their **support processes**, define **requirements** from students, employers, and accreditors, manage with **in-process measures**, and improve systematically. Operational effectiveness covers **cost control** (cloud spend and section planning), **supply-network** requirements and exit plans, and tested plans for **safety, continuity, and resilience**.

<TryIt title="Define One Key Process Fully">

Pick one key work process at your school (course delivery is a good start). Write:

1. Its **three or four key requirements**, and where each came from (students, employers, accreditor, regulation)
2. **Two in-process measures** you can see during the term
3. **One outcome measure** you'd report in Category 7
4. Your **last improvement** to it: what changed, when, and how you know it helped

If you can't answer step 4, the process has no improvement cycle yet.

</TryIt>
