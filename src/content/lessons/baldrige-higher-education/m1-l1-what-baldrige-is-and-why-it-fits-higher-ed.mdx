## A Framework of Questions, Not a Checklist

The **Baldrige Excellence Framework** is a set of questions about how an organization is led and run, plus a way to score the answers. It doesn't tell a school *what* to do. It never says "use this LMS" or "run advising this way." It asks **how** you do the things that matter and **how you know** they work.

That makes it useful for a school of technology and computing. Most schools already have many good practices: an advisory board, program review, course evaluations, an assessment plan for accreditation. Usually these were built at different times by different people. Baldrige asks whether they work together as **one system** that delivers on the mission and keeps improving.

---

## Where It Came From

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| **1987** | Congress passes the Malcolm Baldrige National Quality Improvement Act, named for the late Secretary of Commerce, to strengthen U.S. competitiveness. |
| **1999** | Education and health care organizations become eligible for the award. |
| **2001** | First education recipients, including **University of Wisconsin–Stout**, the first higher education institution to win. |
| **2004** | **Kenneth W. Monfort College of Business** (University of Northern Colorado), a business school *inside* a university, wins. |
| **2005** | **Richland College** (now Dallas College Richland Campus) becomes the first community college to win. |
| **2018, 2024** | **Alamo Colleges District** (San Antonio) wins twice, the first higher education institution to do so. |
| **2026** | Most award operations move from NIST to the Baldrige Foundation and the Alliance for Performance Excellence. NIST and the Department of Commerce keep oversight. |

Monfort is worth noticing. It was a single **school within a university** that used Baldrige to run itself, which is exactly the situation of a school of technology and computing. You don't need the whole university on board to start.

---

## The Three Parts of the Framework

1. **The Criteria**: the questions, grouped into an **Organizational Profile** plus **seven categories**. The Education version speaks the language of schools: *students and other customers*, *educational programs and services*, *faculty and staff*.
2. **Core values and concepts**: eleven beliefs that the Criteria are built on (Lesson 1.2).
3. **Scoring guidelines**: how examiners judge the maturity of processes (**ADLI**) and the strength of results (**LeTCI**) (Lesson 6.1).

Read it from top to bottom:

- The **Organizational Profile** comes first. It describes who you are and what you face, so every later answer can be judged *in your context*. A small online computing school isn't held to the same approach as a large research university.
- The **leadership triad** (Leadership, Strategy, Customers) is about setting direction: leaders, the plan, and the students and stakeholders the plan serves.
- The **results triad** (Workforce, Operations, Results) is about getting the work done. Faculty and staff run the key processes, and those processes produce results.
- **Measurement, Analysis, and Knowledge Management** is the foundation. Without good data, nothing above it can be managed by fact.

## How the 1,000 Points Are Weighted

Points come from the 2023–2024 Education edition. The Organizational Profile isn't scored.

| Category | Items | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Leadership** | 1.1 Senior Leadership (65) · 1.2 Governance and Societal Contributions (50) | **115** |
| **2 Strategy** | 2.1 Strategy Development (45) · 2.2 Strategy Implementation (45) | **90** |
| **3 Customers** | 3.1 Customer Expectations (40) · 3.2 Customer Engagement (45) | **85** |
| **4 Measurement, Analysis, and Knowledge Management** | 4.1 Measurement, Analysis, Review, and Improvement of Organizational Performance (45) · 4.2 Information and Knowledge Management (45) | **90** |
| **5 Workforce** | 5.1 Workforce Environment (40) · 5.2 Workforce Engagement (45) | **85** |
| **6 Operations** | 6.1 Work Processes (40) · 6.2 Operational Effectiveness (45) | **85** |
| **7 Results** | 7.1 Student Learning and Process Results (120) · 7.2 Customer Results (80) · 7.3 Workforce Results (80) · 7.4 Leadership and Governance Results (80) · 7.5 Budgetary, Financial, Marketplace, and Strategy Results (90) | **450** |

Two things stand out. **Results carry 45% of the points.** Good processes only count if they produce results. And **7.1 Student Learning and Process Results** is the single largest item. In an education framework, learning is the main thing you're judged on.

## Baldrige and Accreditation Are Different Tools

| | Accreditation (e.g., NWCCU, ABET) | Baldrige |
|---|---|---|
| **Main question** | Do you meet the standard? | How mature and integrated is your system, and is it improving? |
| **Outcome** | Pass/fail, with recommendations | A score and a feedback report of strengths and opportunities for improvement (OFIs) |
| **Scope** | Defined by the accreditor's standards | Every part of the organization, including finances and workforce |
| **Who participates** | Required for federal financial aid (institutional) or for professional standing (programs) | Voluntary |
| **Comparison** | Mostly against the standard | Against peers, competitors, and best performers |

They work well together. Accreditation sets the floor, and Baldrige shows you how to rise above it. In Lesson 6.2 you'll see how a Baldrige self-assessment produces much of the evidence an NWCCU *Evaluation of Institutional Effectiveness* needs.

<Pitfall title="Treating Baldrige as Another Compliance Report">

**Common mistake:** the provost asks for "a Baldrige document." A staff member writes 50 pages describing everything the school does, files it, and nothing changes.

The value of Baldrige is the **feedback** and the **improvement it drives**, not the document. A three-page Organizational Profile that leads to two real improvement projects beats a polished 50-page application nobody acts on. Start small, act on what you find, and keep the document a working tool.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

Baldrige is a set of **"how" and "how do you know" questions**, not a prescription. It has three parts: **Criteria, core values, and scoring**. The Organizational Profile sets the context. The leadership triad sets direction, and the results triad does the work, all on a foundation of **measurement and knowledge management**. **Results are 450 of 1,000 points**, and student learning is the biggest item. Accreditation sets the floor; Baldrige helps you go past it. A single school within a university can use it, as Monfort College of Business showed.
