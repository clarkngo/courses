## What P.2 Asks

P.1 described *who you are*. **P.2 Organizational Situation** describes *what you're up against* and how you get better. It has three parts:

- **P.2a Competitive environment:** your position, the changes affecting it, and your sources of comparative data
- **P.2b Strategic context:** your key strategic challenges, threats, advantages, and opportunities
- **P.2c Performance improvement system:** your overall approach to improving, and the tools you use

---

## P.2a Competitive Environment

### (1) Competitive position

STC enrolls about **1,400 students** (60% undergraduate). Within the Puget Sound market for adult-serving computing degrees, it estimates its share at about 8%. Undergraduate enrollment is flat over three years. Graduate enrollment grew quickly but swings with international demand.

Its competitors fall into four groups:

| Competitor type | What they offer | How STC differentiates |
|---|---|---|
| **National online universities** | Low cost, self-paced, huge marketing budgets | Live practitioner faculty; local employer network |
| **Regional public universities** (online and evening programs) | Brand, lower in-state tuition | Four starts a year; transfer-friendly; adult-focused support |
| **Community college applied bachelor's degrees** | Very low cost, local | Graduate pathway; cloud and security depth |
| **Bootcamps and vendor certifications** | Short, cheap, job-focused | Accredited degree; broader foundations; certifications built into courses |

### (2) Changes affecting the competitive situation

- **Generative AI** is changing entry-level software and IT work, and with it what employers expect from new graduates.
- **International graduate demand** moves with visa policy, which makes graduate enrollment volatile.
- **Short-term credentials** are growing, and some employers now hire on skills rather than degrees.
- **Opportunities for collaboration:** employer-sponsored cohorts, and stackable certificates that count toward the degree.

### (3) Comparative data sources

Baldrige asks where you get **comparisons**, inside and outside education, and what limits them. This question trips up many schools.

| Source | What it gives you | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **IPEDS** (federal) | Retention, graduation, Outcome Measures for part-time and transfer students, completions by field | Institution-level, not school-level; slow (one to two years old) |
| **National Student Clearinghouse Research Center** | Persistence, transfer, and completion across institutions | Aggregate benchmarks; institution-specific data requires participation |
| **College Scorecard** | Earnings and debt by field of study | Only students who received federal aid; earnings lag several years |
| **NSSE** or an adult-student survey such as RNL's Adult Student Priorities Survey | Engagement and satisfaction benchmarks against peer groups | Survey cost; response rates for online adults can be low |
| **CUPA-HR** | Faculty and staff salary benchmarks | Few benchmarks for adjunct pay by discipline |
| **Labor-market data** (e.g., job-posting analytics) | Skill demand and wages by occupation | Shows demand, not graduate outcomes |
| **Outside education** | Customer-service and Net Promoter benchmarks; cybersecurity maturity benchmarks | Must be translated to a school context |

## P.2b Strategic Context

| | Key items for STC |
|---|---|
| **Strategic challenges and threats** | 1. Keeping curriculum current as AI changes entry-level work · 2. Retaining online working adults through their first year · 3. Volatile international graduate enrollment · 4. Adjunct engagement and consistency across ~110 instructors · 5. Price pressure from national online providers |
| **Strategic advantages** | 1. Practitioner faculty with current industry practice · 2. Four start dates and evening delivery · 3. A 14-employer advisory board and internship network |
| **Strategic opportunities** | 1. Employer-sponsored cohorts · 2. Certificates that stack into degrees · 3. AI-assisted tutoring and feedback at scale |

These lists matter later. In Category 2, examiners will check whether your **strategic objectives address these challenges** and **use these advantages**. If a challenge has no matching objective, that's a gap.

Other tools in the system: **program learning-outcome assessment** with rubrics, **advisory board** skill reviews, **course-shell standards**, and the evidence cycle for **NWCCU** and **ABET**.

<Pitfall title="'We Use Data' Is Not a System">

**Common mistake:** P.2c says, "STC is committed to continuous improvement and uses data to drive decisions."

That's a sentence, not a system. Name the **cycles**, their **timing**, the **tools**, and **how findings move between levels**. If course-level learning never reaches program review, and program review never reaches the budget, you have three separate activities, not a system.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

P.2 covers your **competitive position and the changes affecting it**, your **comparative data sources and their limits**, your **strategic challenges and advantages**, and your **performance improvement system**. Later categories are checked against it: strategy must address the challenges, and results must be compared using the sources you named. A real improvement system **links cycles at different speeds** so findings move up and priorities move down.
