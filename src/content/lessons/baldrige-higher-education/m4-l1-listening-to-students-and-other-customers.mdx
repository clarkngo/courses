## What Category 3 Asks

Category 3 (85 points) is about the people you serve:

- **3.1 Customer Expectations (40):** how you **listen** to current and potential students and other customers, how you **segment** them, and how you decide which **educational programs and services** to offer.
- **3.2 Customer Engagement (45):** how you build **relationships**, provide **access and support**, manage **complaints**, ensure **fair treatment**, and determine **satisfaction, dissatisfaction, and engagement**.

At STC, "other customers" are mainly **employers**: those who hire graduates and those who pay for employees to study.

---

## Listening Across the Lifecycle

A course evaluation at the end of the quarter is one listening post. Baldrige expects many, spread across the whole relationship, and including people who **aren't** your students yet or **no longer** are.

Three details make this a strong approach:

- **Potential customers** are included: inquiry questions and a short survey of admitted students who didn't enroll ("lost applicants") show why people choose a competitor.
- **Former customers** are included: stop-out interviews with students who left are one of the best sources of insight.
- **Listening varies by segment.** International graduate students get an in-person check-in, because online surveys under-represent them. Military-connected students get a benefits-specific check-in in their first term.

Also listen through **social media and review sites**. Tag comments by theme, the same way you tag complaints.

## Segmentation and Program Offerings (3.1b)

STC segments students by **program level**, **modality**, **entry path** (new, transfer, international), and **military affiliation**. Each segment's requirements, as written in P.1b(2), drive decisions about programs and services.

New offerings follow a defined path:

## Relationships, Support, and Complaints (3.2a)

| Requirement | STC's approach |
|---|---|
| **Relationship management** | A named success coach for every new student through the first year; a program director "welcome session" each quarter |
| **Access and support** | Evening and weekend virtual advising; a single help portal that routes to advising, IT, financial aid, or the registrar; service-level targets for each |
| **Complaint management** | One intake form for all complaints; acknowledgment within one business day; resolution target of five business days; quarterly **root-cause review** of all complaints by theme |
| **Fair treatment** | Results for every approach are reviewed **by segment**; course shells meet accessibility standards; international and online students get equal access to tutoring and career services |

The complaint process has two loops: one that resolves each case, and one that removes the cause:

Root-cause review is what turns complaints into improvement. When STC grouped a year of complaints, a third were about **transfer-credit evaluation taking too long**. The fix was a process change, not an apology: evaluations are now done before the first term starts.

## Satisfaction, Dissatisfaction, and Engagement (3.2b)

These are three different things, and Baldrige asks about each:

- **Satisfaction:** did we meet expectations? (course and service surveys)
- **Dissatisfaction:** what drives people away? It's **not** just low satisfaction scores. Determine it separately, through stop-out interviews, complaint themes, and lost-applicant surveys.
- **Engagement:** would they recommend us, come back for another credential, or hire our graduates again? (net-promoter-style questions, return rates, employer repeat hiring)

Also get satisfaction **relative to competitors**. STC uses an adult-student survey with peer benchmarks and asks lost applicants which school they chose and why.

<Pitfall title="Averages That Hide the Students You're Losing">

**Common mistake:** STC reports overall course satisfaction of 4.3 out of 5 and concludes students are happy.

Split by segment, online first-year students rate it 3.6, and they're also the group with the lowest persistence. The average was carried by graduate students in hybrid courses. And survey response rates are lowest among the students most likely to leave. Always **segment** results, check **who didn't respond**, and determine **dissatisfaction** directly rather than inferring it from averages.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

Category 3 asks how you **listen** to current, potential, and former students and to employers across the **whole lifecycle**, how **segmentation** drives which **programs and services** you offer, and how you build **relationships**, provide **support**, resolve **complaints** with root-cause analysis, and ensure **fair treatment**. Measure **satisfaction, dissatisfaction, and engagement** separately, by segment, and compared with competitors.
