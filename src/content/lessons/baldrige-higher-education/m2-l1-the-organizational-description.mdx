## Start Here, Always

The Organizational Profile is a **snapshot of your organization and its strategic environment**. It's the right first step for a self-assessment, for three reasons:

- **It's a self-assessment in itself.** If your team can't agree on who your key student segments are, or doesn't know your comparative data sources, you've found your first gaps.
- **It sets the context** for every later answer. Examiners judge your approaches against *your* situation.
- **It aligns people.** New faculty, staff, and even board members can read it to understand the school in ten minutes.

The profile has two items: **P.1 Organizational Description** (this lesson) and **P.2 Organizational Situation** (next lesson). It isn't scored, but it's still the most important document you'll write.

## P.1a Organizational Environment, for STC

Northbridge's **School of Technology and Computing (STC)** is the running case. Remember that it's a composite, and the numbers are invented.

### (1) Educational programs and services

| Offering | Share of STC tuition revenue | Modalities |
|---|---|---|
| **BS Information Technology** (cloud and cybersecurity concentrations) | 28% | Online; hybrid evenings in Seattle |
| **MS Computer Science** | 25% | Hybrid evenings; partner-site cohorts |
| **BS Computer Science** | 22% | Online; hybrid evenings |
| **MS Cybersecurity** | 15% | Online |
| **Certificates and corporate training** (cloud, data analytics) | 10% | Online; on-site at employers |

STC runs **10-week quarters** with four start dates a year. Online courses mix weekly asynchronous work with one live session, scheduled in the evening.

### (2) Mission, vision, values, and core competencies

- **Mission:** Prepare working adults to build and secure the technology their communities rely on.
- **Vision:** The most trusted path into a technology career for working adults in the Pacific Northwest.
- **Core competencies** (what STC does better than most, and what its mission depends on):
  1. **Practitioner faculty** who teach what they do at work
  2. **Flexible delivery** designed around adult schedules
  3. **Career-aligned curriculum** updated with employers

### (3) Workforce profile

Baldrige asks you to **segment** your workforce and name the **engagement drivers** for each group. A single "faculty satisfaction" number hides more than it shows.

| Segment | Size | Key engagement drivers |
|---|---|---|
| **Full-time faculty** | 22 | Curriculum ownership, manageable load, scholarship and professional time |
| **Adjunct (practitioner) faculty** | ~110 | Schedule predictability, good course shells, feeling part of the school, fair pay |
| **Professional staff** (advisors, coordinators, instructional designers) | 18 | Clear roles, workable caseloads, career growth |

**Changes underway:** more demand for AI and cybersecurity expertise, more adjuncts teaching from other states, and a wave of full-time faculty retirements within five years.

### (4) Assets

A cloud-based **virtual lab environment**, a **cyber range** for hands-on security exercises, downtown Seattle classrooms, and a library of **master course shells** that every section is built from.

### (5) Regulatory environment

| Requirement | Why it matters to STC |
|---|---|
| **NWCCU** institutional accreditation | Required for the university's federal financial aid eligibility; drives assessment and student-achievement reporting |
| **U.S. Department of Education** (Title IV) | Financial aid rules, satisfactory academic progress, return of funds |
| **State authorization** for online students in other states (e.g., through NC-SARA) | Determines where STC can enroll online students |
| **SEVP / F-1 rules** | Limits online coursework for international students; affects hybrid scheduling |
| **FERPA** | Protects student education records, including learning analytics data |
| **ADA / Section 504** and digital accessibility | Online courses and lab tools must be accessible |
| **Program accreditation** (STC is preparing an ABET application for the BS CS) | Requires student-outcome assessment and continuous improvement |

## P.1b Organizational Relationships

### (1) Structure and governance

The President and Provost lead the university, and the **Dean of STC** reports to the Provost. Program directors lead each degree. A **faculty council** reviews curriculum, and an **industry advisory board** of 14 employers advises on skills. The university's **board of trustees** holds final fiduciary authority.

For each group, name the **key requirements**. The differences between groups are what matter most:

| Group | Key requirements |
|---|---|
| **Working-adult undergraduates** | Flexible schedule, clear path to a job, credit for prior learning, affordable |
| **Transfer students** | Maximum credit transfer, clear degree map from day one |
| **International graduate students** | Visa-compliant schedule, career support for the U.S. market, community |
| **Military-connected students** | Benefit processing, credit for military training, deployment-friendly policies |
| **Employers** | Graduates with current, job-ready skills; reliable pipeline; sponsored-employee progress reports |
| **University leadership** | Enrollment and margin targets, accreditation standing, reputation |

### (3) Suppliers, partners, and collaborators

The **LMS vendor** and **cloud lab providers** deliver the learning environment. **Community colleges** supply transfer students through articulation agreements. **Employers** supply adjunct faculty, internships, and advisory board members. **University central services** (registrar, financial aid, IT) are internal suppliers, and STC depends on them but doesn't control them.

<Pitfall title="Describing the Org Chart Instead of the System">

**Common mistake:** P.1 turns into a list of departments, buildings, and degree names copied from the catalog.

Examiners need what makes your situation *different*: which student segments have different needs, which workforce groups have different engagement drivers, which partners you can't operate without. If two schools could swap their profiles and no one would notice, the profile isn't doing its job.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

P.1 describes your **environment** (programs and modalities, mission and core competencies, a **segmented** workforce, assets, and regulations) and your **relationships** (governance, **student segments and their differing requirements**, and key partners). Keep it specific and short. Its value is in the **differences** it shows and the **gaps** it reveals.

<TryIt title="Draft Your P.1 in One Page">

For your school, write one page with:

1. Your top programs, each with **share of revenue** and **modality**
2. Mission, vision, and **two or three core competencies**
3. A workforce table with **at least three segments** and their engagement drivers
4. Your **five most important** regulatory or accreditation requirements
5. **Three to five student or customer segments**, each with key requirements

Mark every cell where you had to guess with a "?". Those guesses are the start of your improvement list.
