## The Fastest-Growing Clinical AI

Ambient AI scribes listen to the clinical encounter (with consent), transcribe it, and generate a draft note. Many clinicians report less after-hours charting and more eye contact with patients. That's a real benefit. But the note you sign is a **legal and clinical record**, and you are its author.

| Stage | Typical errors |
|---|---|
| **Capture** | Missed speech (soft voices, masks, background noise), side conversations recorded, interpreter dialogue partially captured |
| **Transcribe** | Misheard drug names (sound-alikes), numbers, laterality ("left" vs. "right"), wrong speaker attribution (patient vs. family vs. clinician) |
| **Generate** | **Fabricated findings** (e.g., a full normal ROS or exam that wasn't done), omissions of important negatives, over-confident assessments, statements framed as patient-reported when the clinician said them |
| **Review** | Automation bias: skimming a well-formatted note and signing |

### The most dangerous error: plausible additions

The model was trained on huge numbers of notes in which certain exam elements usually appear together. It may "complete the pattern" by documenting a **normal exam element you didn't perform**, or a **review of systems you didn't ask about**. These errors look exactly like real documentation and can mislead the next clinician, affect billing compliance, and create medicolegal exposure.

## A 90-Second Review Routine

Before signing every AI-drafted note, check these in order:

1. **Exam and ROS.** Did I actually perform/ask everything documented? Delete anything I didn't.
2. **Medications and numbers.** Drug names, doses, frequencies, vitals, lab values, durations. Sound-alike drugs and transposed numbers are common.
3. **Laterality and anatomy.** Left/right, upper/lower, specific digits or levels.
4. **Attribution.** Is each statement attributed to the right person (patient, family, clinician)?
5. **Assessment and plan.** Does it reflect *my* reasoning and decisions, including uncertainty, and not a more confident or different version?
6. **Omissions.** Is anything important missing: a key negative, a safety-net instruction, a shared decision, a sensitive disclosure the patient asked you to document (or not to)?

<Pitfall title="Reviewing Only the Assessment and Plan">

**Common mistake:** trusting the HPI, ROS, and exam because "that's just the scribe's part" and focusing review on the A&P.

Fabricated exam findings and misattributed history are among the most common ambient-scribe errors, and they're in the sections clinicians skim. Review the **whole** note, with extra attention to anything you didn't personally say.

</Pitfall>

## Consent and Sensitive Encounters

- **Tell patients** that an AI tool is helping with documentation, and follow your organization's consent process.
- **Offer an opt-out**, and honor it without friction.
- **Pause or decline** ambient capture for sensitive conversations (intimate partner violence, substance use disclosures, mental health crises), or when a patient seems hesitant, as your policy allows.
- **Be careful with third parties.** Family members and interpreters are recorded too.

## Feedback Improves the System

Most enterprise scribe tools have a way to flag errors. Use it. Recurring error patterns (a misheard drug, a template that always inserts a normal exam) are often fixable at the configuration level, and your reports help colleagues.

<TryIt title="Audit Five of Your Own Notes">

If your organization uses an ambient scribe:

1. Pick five recent AI-drafted notes **before** your edits, if your system keeps a version history. Otherwise, review the next five as they arrive.
2. Using the six-step routine, count errors in each category.
3. Identify your top error category. Add a deliberate check for it to your routine.
4. Report any systematic pattern through your organization's feedback channel.

If you don't use a scribe yet, write a short fictional encounter dialogue, ask a general assistant to "write a SOAP note from this transcript," and audit the result for added findings.

</TryIt>
