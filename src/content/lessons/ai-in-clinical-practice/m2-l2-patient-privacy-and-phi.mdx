## The Fastest Way to Turn a Helpful Tool Into a Breach

A nurse pastes a patient's discharge summary into a free chatbot to produce a plain-language version. A physician photographs a rash and uploads it to a consumer app for a "second opinion." Both intentions are good, and both may be impermissible disclosures of protected health information.

This lesson uses **US HIPAA** as the reference point. Other jurisdictions have comparable or stricter rules (for example, GDPR in the EU and UK, PIPEDA and provincial laws in Canada). The principles transfer; the specifics belong to your privacy office.

---

## Key Concepts

### PHI

**Protected health information** is individually identifiable health information held by a covered entity or its business associate. Identifiability is broader than names: dates of service, MRNs, addresses, phone numbers, full-face photos, and rare-condition details can all identify a patient.

### Business Associate Agreement (BAA)

When a vendor creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on your organization's behalf, HIPAA generally requires a **BAA**: a contract obligating the vendor to safeguard the data and limiting how it can be used.

- **Enterprise AI tools** your organization has contracted for (ambient scribes, EHR-integrated assistants, enterprise LLM deployments) typically come with a BAA and configured data protections.
- **Consumer AI apps** (free or personal paid accounts) typically **do not**. Their terms may allow data retention, human review, or use for training.

**Whether a tool is covered is a contract question, not a features question.** A consumer product and an enterprise product from the same company can have entirely different terms.

### De-identification

HIPAA provides two pathways:

1. **Safe Harbor:** remove 18 specified identifier types (names, geographic units smaller than a state, all date elements except year, contact numbers, MRNs, device identifiers, full-face photos, and others) and have no actual knowledge that the remainder could identify the individual.
2. **Expert Determination:** a qualified expert certifies that re-identification risk is very small.

Removing the name alone is **not** de-identification. "68-year-old retired ferry captain from Port Townsend admitted March 4 with…" may identify someone in a small community.

### Safe habits

- **Abstract the question.** *"What's the renal dosing adjustment approach for [drug] at an eGFR of 25?"* needs no patient identifiers at all.
- **Apply minimum necessary** even in approved tools. Don't paste the whole chart when the task needs one lab trend.
- **Never use personal accounts** for patient work, even "just this once."
- **Images are high risk.** Photos can contain faces, tattoos, wristbands, room numbers, and metadata.
- **Know your organization's policy** and where to ask. Most health systems now have a published AI use policy and an approved-tools list.

<Pitfall title="Assuming 'Turn Off Training' Equals HIPAA Compliance">

**Common mistake:** believing that disabling model training in a consumer app makes it acceptable for PHI.

Turning off training may reduce one risk, but it doesn't create a BAA, guarantee retention limits, or restrict human review under HIPAA terms. Compliance depends on the contractual and technical arrangement your organization has with the vendor.

</Pitfall>

## Beyond Legal Compliance: Trust

Even where a use is technically permitted, consider how the patient would feel if they knew. Many health systems are adopting **transparency practices**, such as telling patients when ambient listening is used and offering an opt-out. Privacy is ultimately about preserving the trust that makes patients willing to tell you the truth.
