## Prompts Are Orders, Not Chit-Chat

You'd never write an order that says "give pain med." You specify drug, dose, route, frequency, indication, and hold parameters, because ambiguity causes harm. Clinical prompts deserve the same precision. A vague prompt yields generic output that's harder to verify and easier to misapply.

**Weak:** *"Write discharge instructions for CHF."*

**Strong:**

> **Role & audience:** You are helping a hospitalist write discharge instructions for a patient with limited health literacy, reading at about a 6th-grade level.
>
> **Context:** Adult patient admitted for acute decompensated heart failure (HFrEF), now euvolemic. Discharge medications include a loop diuretic (once daily), a beta-blocker, and an SGLT2 inhibitor. Follows a 2 g sodium diet. Has a home scale.
>
> **Task:** Draft discharge instructions covering daily weights, diet, medications, and when to seek care.
>
> **Constraints:** Do not include specific doses; I will add them. Use the threshold "call if weight increases more than 2 pounds in a day or 5 pounds in a week" exactly as written. If anything I've provided seems inconsistent with standard practice, flag it separately rather than changing it.
>
> **Format:** Short headings, bullets, under 300 words. A separate "Call 911 if…" box at the end.

The strong version is longer, but it produces a draft you can **review in a minute** rather than rewrite in ten.

## Prompt Patterns That Improve Safety

### Ask for uncertainty, explicitly

> *"For each recommendation, note your confidence and what would change it. If you're unsure, say so instead of guessing."*

### Ask for alternatives, not confirmation

| Leading (invites sycophancy) | Neutral (invites reasoning) |
|---|---|
| "Why is this most likely viral?" | "What's the differential, ranked, with features for and against each?" |
| "Confirm this dose is right." | "Calculate the dose, show the calculation, and list assumptions." |
| "This is fine to discharge, right?" | "What findings would argue against discharge?" |

### Ask what's missing

> *"What additional history, exam findings, or tests would most change the assessment?"*

This mirrors good clinical reasoning and helps surface gaps.

### Separate facts from inferences

> *"In the summary, mark anything stated in the source as [SOURCE] and anything you inferred as [INFERRED]."*

This makes fabrications much easier to spot during review.

### Constrain to provided material

> *"Use only the information in the text below. If something isn't in the text, write 'not documented.'"*

Useful for summarization, where you want the model to stay inside the record rather than "fill in" plausible details.

<Pitfall title="Prompting With Your Conclusion">

**Common mistake:** front-loading your working diagnosis ("62-year-old with a likely PE…") and asking AI to help with the plan.

The model will optimize for your stated diagnosis and rarely question it. If you want a second look, **describe the presentation without your conclusion first**, ask for the differential, and only then compare it with your own thinking.

</Pitfall>

## Iterate Like a Consult

Treat the first response as a first pass. Follow up as you would with a consultant: *"You didn't address renal function."* *"Simplify the language further."* *"Which of these recommendations is guideline-based versus expert opinion?"* Each turn should narrow toward something you can verify and use.

### Build reusable templates

If a prompt works well, save it (without patient data) as a template your team can reuse. Many enterprise tools support saved prompts or shared instructions. Consistent prompts also make output **consistently reviewable**.

<TryIt title="Rewrite a Weak Prompt">

Using a general AI assistant and **no PHI**:

1. Start with a weak prompt from your specialty, such as *"Patient handout on [condition]."* Save the output.
2. Rewrite it using all five elements, including a reading-level target, at least one explicit constraint, and a request to flag uncertainty.
3. Compare the two outputs. Which is faster to verify? Which contains more content you'd have to remove?
4. Save the improved prompt as a template.

</TryIt>
