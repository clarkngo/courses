## Why Printing on Corrugated Is Hard

A sheet of corrugated board isn't flat at a microscopic level. The liner is supported by flutes underneath and sags slightly between them. A printing plate pressing on that surface contacts the flute tips harder than the valleys, which can cause **fluting** (also called **washboard printing**) — faint stripes in solid areas that follow the flutes.

Every corrugated printing method is a different answer to that problem.

---

## The Four Main Methods

| Method | How it works | Graphics quality | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Post-print flexo** | Flexible rubber or photopolymer plates print directly on the finished board, usually in the FFG or RDC | Basic to good (1–4 colors typical) | Most shipping boxes: logos, handling marks, barcodes |
| **Preprint flexo** | Liner is printed on a wide web press **before** it goes to the corrugator | High | Long runs of high-graphics boxes (beverages, consumer goods) |
| **Litho-lamination** | Paper is printed on an offset litho press, then **laminated** onto single-face or board | Highest — photographic | Retail displays, premium consumer packaging |
| **Digital** | Inkjet heads print directly on board or on preprint liner | High; every box can differ | Short runs, versioning, prototypes, personalization |

### Post-Print Flexo Basics

In each flexo print unit:

1. An **ink fountain** supplies ink to an **anilox roll** — a ceramic roll engraved with millions of tiny cells.
2. A **doctor blade** wipes the surface, leaving a precise volume of ink in the cells.
3. The anilox transfers ink to the raised image on the **plate**.
4. The plate prints onto the board.

The **anilox line screen** (cells per inch) and **cell volume** control how much ink goes down. Coarser anilox = more ink for solid areas; finer anilox = less ink for fine detail. Corrugated inks are mostly **water-based**.

---

## Design Rules for Corrugated Graphics

Good box designers work **with** the substrate:

- **Avoid large solids** on post-print flexo — they show fluting. Use texture, reverse type, or break up solids.
- **Keep fine type and thin lines** above minimum sizes (plants publish their own specs).
- **Expect dot gain** — halftone dots spread on absorbent kraft liner, so images print darker than they look on screen.
- **Account for liner color:** brown kraft shifts colors, and white ink isn't always available. White-top or bleached liner costs more but prints cleaner.
- **Barcodes:** print them in the machine direction recommended by the plant, at an approved size, and verify scannability.
- **Keep graphics off scores and slots** — they crack and distort.

<Pitfall title="Designing a Box on Screen Like a Brochure">

**Common mistake:** sending a box plant artwork with full-bleed photos, small reversed type, and rich dark solids — intended for post-print flexo on brown kraft.

It will print muddy, fluted, and dark, and the brand team will reject it. Before artwork starts, ask the plant **which print method, liner, and number of colors** the job will use, and get their design guide. The right artwork for litho-lam is the wrong artwork for one-color post-print.

</Pitfall>

---

## Choosing a Method

Ask four questions:

1. **How many boxes?** Litho-lam and preprint have high setup; digital has none but higher unit cost.
2. **How good must it look?** Shipping-only box vs. retail shelf vs. premium unboxing.
3. **How many versions?** Many SKUs or seasonal changes favor digital.
4. **What's the timeline?** Plates and preprint rolls take lead time; digital can run tomorrow.

Increasingly, e-commerce brands use **digital or high-graphics flexo** on the box itself, because the shipping box *is* the first thing a customer sees.

---

## Key Takeaway

Corrugated's fluted surface makes printing harder than on flat paper. **Post-print flexo** covers most boxes; **preprint** and **litho-lam** deliver high graphics for long runs; **digital** wins for short runs and versioning. Design for the method, liner, and press — not for the screen.

<TryIt title="Spot the Method">

Look closely at three printed boxes: a plain shipping box, a beer or soda case, and a retail display or premium electronics box. Use a magnifying glass or phone camera zoom.

- Visible flute stripes in solids and soft edges → **post-print flexo**
- Crisp, glossy, photographic images on a paper layer you can peel → **litho-lam**
- Sharp graphics printed directly in the liner, no peelable layer → **preprint** or **digital**

</TryIt>
