## A Shared Language for Boxes

There are thousands of box designs, but most are variations on a few families. The **FEFCO/ESBO International Fibreboard Case Code** — maintained by the European corrugated and solid board associations and used worldwide — gives each standard style a four-digit number. A buyer in Ohio and a box plant in Vietnam can both say **"0201"** and mean exactly the same box.

| FEFCO series | Family |
|---|---|
| **02xx** | **Slotted boxes** — one piece, glued/taped/stitched joint, top and bottom flaps |
| **03xx** | **Telescope boxes** — separate lid and base that slide over each other |
| **04xx** | **Folder boxes and trays** — usually one piece, folded, often die-cut with locking tabs |
| **05xx** | **Slide boxes** — sleeves and inner trays |
| **06xx** | **Rigid boxes** — separate ends joined to a body |
| **07xx** | **Ready-glued cases** — ship flat, pop open with minimal assembly |
| **09xx** | **Interior fitments** — partitions, pads, liners |

## The Regular Slotted Container (RSC) — FEFCO 0201

The **RSC** is the most common box in the world, and the most efficient — it wastes almost no board.

- All four flaps on each end are the **same depth** — half the box width.
- The two **outer (major) flaps** meet in the center when closed.
- The two **inner (minor) flaps** don't meet unless the box is square.
- One **manufacturer's joint** (glued, taped, or stitched) closes the tube.

The RSC is cheap and versatile. Its weakness: the center seam on top, and a gap between inner flaps that leaves the product unsupported in the middle.

## Other Slotted Styles

| Style | FEFCO | Description | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Half slotted container (HSC)** | 0200 | RSC with only one set of flaps; open top | Used with a separate cover, or as a tray |
| **Overlap slotted container (OSC)** | 0202 | Outer flaps overlap by an inch or more | Long, narrow boxes where flaps wouldn't meet well |
| **Full overlap (FOL)** | 0203 | Outer flaps each span the full width | Heavy or rough handling; flaps double the top and bottom strength |
| **Center special slotted (CSSC)** | 0204 | Both inner and outer flaps meet in the center | Full, flat support on top and bottom |

## Telescope, Trays, and Die-Cuts

- **Telescope (03xx):** a base and a lid. **Full telescope** lids cover the whole depth (heavy-duty storage). **Partial telescope** lids cover part of it — like a shoebox or a document box (0301 style).
- **Trays (04xx and others):** shallow, open-top designs for produce and retail-ready packaging. Many are **die-cut** with locking tabs so they assemble without glue.
- **Roll-end tuck-top mailer (FEFCO 0427):** the classic e-commerce and subscription box — a die-cut, one-piece mailer with dust flaps and a tuck lid. Usually E or B flute, often printed.

Die-cut styles can do almost anything, but they cost more: they need a **die**, run slower, and generate more trim waste than an RSC.

<Pitfall title="Choosing a Style Without Checking Board Usage">

**Common mistake:** picking a FOL or a fancy die-cut mailer for its looks or strength, then being surprised by the price.

A FOL box uses noticeably more board than an RSC of the same size, and a die-cut adds tooling and trim. For a pallet-stacked shipper, a well-designed RSC with the right board grade is usually the cheapest way to hit the strength target. Use premium styles where they actually earn their cost — presentation, unboxing, or extreme handling.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

The **FEFCO code** is the international shorthand for box styles. The **RSC (0201)** is the most common and efficient box. **HSC, OSC, FOL, and CSSC** are slotted variations; **telescopes, trays, and die-cuts** cover lids, retail displays, and mailers — at higher cost.
