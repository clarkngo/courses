## The Corrugated Loop

Corrugated is one of the most recycled packaging materials. Used boxes (**OCC**) are baled at stores, warehouses, and recycling facilities, pulped at containerboard mills, and made into new linerboard and medium — often within weeks.

Fibers shorten each cycle, so the loop needs a steady stream of **virgin kraft** fiber too. That's why linerboard typically blends virgin and recycled fiber, and why many boxes carry both a recycled-content claim and a forest certification (FSC, SFI, or PEFC).

## Design Moves That Cut Material and Cost

The most sustainable box is usually also the **cheapest** one — because it uses less board and ships less air.

| Move | How it helps |
|---|---|
| **Right-size the box** | Less board, less void fill, more boxes per pallet and per truck. Carriers charge by **dimensional weight**, so shipping air costs money. |
| **Pick the right orientation** | Swapping which product dimension is the depth can change board area noticeably. |
| **Use the right grade, not the heaviest** | Use McKee and real-world factors to hit the needed BCT — no more. |
| **Let the product share the load** | Rigid products (cans, bottles in tight packs) can carry stacking load, allowing lighter board. |
| **Fix the pallet pattern** | Column stacking and no overhang can recover strength without adding fiber. |
| **Choose high-performance papers** | Lighter, stronger liners can hit the same ECT with less fiber. |
| **Design for recyclability** | Avoid plastic laminations, heavy wax, and mixed materials that complicate recycling. Use paper tape where possible. |

### Dimensional Weight

Parcel carriers bill by whichever is greater: actual weight or **dimensional (DIM) weight**:

> DIM weight = (L × W × H, in inches) ÷ DIM divisor

The divisor is set by the carrier (139 is a common value for U.S. commercial parcel rates). A light product in an oversized box can be billed at several times its actual weight — a powerful financial reason to right-size.

<Pitfall title="Lightweighting Without Retesting">

**Common mistake:** cutting from 44 ECT to 32 ECT to hit a sustainability goal, without re-running stacking calculations or transit tests.

If the box then fails in the warehouse, the damaged product — with all the energy, materials, and emissions it took to make — is a far bigger environmental cost than the fiber saved. Lightweight **with data**: recalculate BCT, check the pallet pattern, and retest.

</Pitfall>

## Box Plant Safety

> **Note:** This is an orientation only. Follow your plant's training, procedures, and lockout rules.

Box plants combine high-speed rotating equipment, sharp tooling, steam, and constant forklift traffic.

| Hazard | Where | Key controls |
|---|---|---|
| **In-running nips** | Corrugator rolls, FFG feed and pull rolls, die cutter cylinders | Guards, interlocks, never reach in while running |
| **Hazardous energy** | All equipment; stored energy in pneumatics, steam, and coasting rolls | Lockout/tagout with verification before clearing jams or changing tooling |
| **Cuts** | Slitter-scorer knives, cut-off knife, rule dies, strapping | Cut-resistant gloves where allowed, blade guards, safe die handling |
| **Steam and hot surfaces** | Preheaters, single facer, double backer hot plates | Insulation, guarding, treat all lines as live |
| **Starch kitchen chemicals** | Caustic soda, borax | Eye and skin protection, eyewash stations |
| **Forklifts and clamp trucks** | Roll handling, finished-goods loads | Pedestrian lanes, eye contact, horn at intersections |
| **Paper dust** | Dry end, converting, trim systems | Housekeeping, dust collection — dust is a fire hazard |
| **Noise** | Corrugator, trim systems, FFGs | Hearing protection |

### Jam Clearing: The Most Dangerous Routine Task

Many serious box plant injuries happen when a worker reaches into a machine to clear a **jam** — a misfed sheet or a wrapped roll — without locking it out. The machine is "stopped," but a sensor, an operator, or stored energy restarts it.

**Rule:** if your hand goes past a guard, the machine is locked out — no matter how quick the job seems.

## Key Takeaway

Corrugated is highly recycled but still needs virgin fiber. The best sustainable designs **right-size, use the right grade, fix pallet patterns, and stay recyclable** — and they save money, especially under **dimensional-weight** pricing. On the floor, **nips, stored energy, knives, steam, and forklifts** are the major hazards; jam clearing requires lockout every time.

<TryIt title="Right-Size a Shipment">

A 2-lb product ships in a 16 × 12 × 10 in box. With a DIM divisor of 139, DIM weight = 1,920 ÷ 139 ≈ **13.8 lb → billed as 14 lb**.

Redesign: the product fits in a 12 × 9 × 6 in box. DIM weight = 648 ÷ 139 ≈ **4.7 lb → billed as 5 lb**. Same product, roughly a third of the billable weight, less board, and less void fill.

Now try it with a product you recently received in a box that was too big.

</TryIt>
