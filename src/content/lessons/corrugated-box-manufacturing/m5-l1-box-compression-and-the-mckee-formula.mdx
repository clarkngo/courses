## What BCT Means

**Box Compression Test (BCT)** strength is the top-to-bottom load an empty box can carry before it buckles, measured in a compression tester under standard conditions (23 °C, 50% RH).

BCT is the number that matters on a pallet: the bottom box in a stack carries the weight of everything above it — sometimes for weeks.

Where does a box's strength come from? Mostly the **corners and the panels near them**. The center of each panel bulges and carries little load. That's why a box's **perimeter** and the board's **stiffness** matter so much.

---

## The McKee Formula

In 1963, McKee, Gander, and Wachuta published a simplified formula that's still used across the industry to estimate BCT for RSCs:

> **BCT ≈ 5.87 × ECT × √(caliper × perimeter)**

| Term | Meaning | Units (U.S.) |
|---|---|---|
| **BCT** | Box compression strength | lbf |
| **ECT** | Edge crush test value of the board | lb/in |
| **caliper** | Board thickness | in |
| **perimeter** | 2 × (L + W) of the box | in |

The constant 5.87 is for these U.S. units. The formula works best for RSCs of ordinary proportions.

### Worked Example

A **12 × 10 × 8 in** RSC made of **32 ECT C-flute** board, caliper **0.16 in**:

1. Perimeter = 2 × (12 + 10) = **44 in**
2. caliper × perimeter = 0.16 × 44 = 7.04
3. √7.04 ≈ **2.65**
4. BCT ≈ 5.87 × 32 × 2.65 ≈ **498 lbf**

So in the lab, this box should hold roughly **500 pounds** before it collapses.

### What the Formula Tells You

- **ECT is linear:** doubling ECT doubles BCT. Board grade is your biggest lever.
- **Caliper matters (square root):** crushed board loses BCT. Converting crush and flat crush both hurt.
- **Perimeter matters (square root):** bigger boxes are stronger — but carry heavier loads above them, too.
- **Depth isn't in the formula** for typical proportions — though very tall boxes do weaken.

## The Real World Is Not a Lab

That 498 lbf is under ideal conditions: a fresh box, 50% RH, a short test. In a real warehouse, the same box can lose more than half its strength. Packaging engineers apply **environmental factors** (multipliers) to find a safe working load.

Typical rule-of-thumb multipliers (actual values vary by source and design):

| Condition | Approximate multiplier |
|---|---|
| **Storage time:** 10 days / 30 days / 90 days | ~0.63 / ~0.60 / ~0.55 |
| **Humidity:** 50% / 70% / 80% / 90% RH | 1.00 / ~0.80 / ~0.68 / ~0.50 |
| **Column-stacked** (corners aligned) | ~1.00 |
| **Misaligned** column stacking | ~0.70–0.90 |
| **Interlocked** (brick) stacking | ~0.50–0.60 |
| **Pallet overhang** | ~0.70–0.80 |
| **Pallet deck gaps** under the boxes | ~0.75–0.90 |

### Example, Continued

Our 498 lbf box sits **30 days** in a **70% RH** warehouse, **interlocked** on the pallet, with some **overhang**:

498 × 0.60 (time) × 0.80 (humidity) × 0.55 (interlock) × 0.75 (overhang) ≈ **99 lbf**

The box lost about **80%** of its lab strength. If each box weighs 25 lb and the stack is 5 high, the bottom box carries 4 × 25 = **100 lb** — right at its limit. It will likely fail.

The fixes, in order of cost:
1. **Column-stack** instead of interlock, and **eliminate overhang** — free.
2. Control **warehouse humidity** or stack lower.
3. Use a **higher ECT** board, or redesign so the product shares the load.

<Pitfall title="Specifying Board Without Knowing the Pallet Pattern">

**Common mistake:** choosing 44 ECT "to be safe" without asking how the boxes will be stacked and stored.

An interlocked pattern with overhang can cost half the box's strength — more than any upgrade in board grade can buy back economically. Fixing the **pallet pattern** is usually the cheapest strength upgrade in packaging. Ask for the pallet configuration before choosing the board.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

**BCT** is a box's top-to-bottom crush strength. The **McKee formula** estimates it from **ECT, caliper, and perimeter**. Real-world factors — **time, humidity, stacking pattern, pallet overhang** — can remove most of that strength, so engineers derate BCT and compare it to the actual load with a safety margin.

<TryIt title="Run the Numbers">

Calculate McKee BCT for a **16 × 12 × 10 in** RSC made from **44 ECT** C-flute board, caliper **0.17 in**.

Perimeter = 2 × (16 + 12) = 56 in. caliper × perimeter = 9.52. √9.52 ≈ 3.09. BCT ≈ 5.87 × 44 × 3.09 ≈ **797 lbf**.

Now derate it for 90 days at 80% RH, column-stacked, no overhang: 797 × 0.55 × 0.68 ≈ **298 lbf**. How many 30-lb boxes can safely sit on top of it?
