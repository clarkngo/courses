| Construction | Layers | Typical uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Single face** | One liner + one fluted medium | Wrapping, cushioning rolls, bottle sleeves |
| **Single wall** | Medium between two liners | The vast majority of boxes |
| **Double wall** | Two mediums, three liners (e.g., **BC**, **EB**) | Heavy products, appliances, long stacking times |
| **Triple wall** | Three mediums, four liners (e.g., **AAC**) | Bulk bins, machinery, a substitute for wooden crates |

Double-wall flute combinations are named with the two flutes, such as **BC** (B flute on one side, C on the other) or **EB** for a thinner, printable double wall.

## Specifying the Paper

A board is fully described by the papers in it. A common shorthand lists **liner / medium / liner** basis weights in **lb/1,000 ft²**:

> **42 / 26 / 42 C** = 42-lb liner, 26-lb medium, 42-lb liner, C flute

Heavier and stronger papers mean stronger board. The box plant chooses papers to hit a **board grade** at the lowest cost.

---

## Two Ways to Rate Board: Mullen vs. ECT

In North America, board grades are usually stated one of two ways. You'll see one of them printed on the bottom flap of most boxes.

### Mullen (Burst Test)

The **Mullen test** pushes a rubber diaphragm against the board until it bursts. The result is reported in **pounds per square inch**, e.g., **200# test**.

- Measures resistance to **puncture and rough handling**
- Driven mostly by the **linerboard** strength
- The traditional rating, still preferred for boxes that face rough handling, heavy individual loads, or export

### Edge Crush Test (ECT)

The **ECT** stands a small sample of board on its edge — flutes vertical — and crushes it. The result is reported in **pounds per lineal inch**, e.g., **32 ECT**.

- Measures **stacking strength** — the most important property for most warehouse and pallet use
- Depends on the **compression strength of all components**, so a box plant can hit an ECT target with lighter, higher-performance papers
- Allows **lighter boxes** for the same stacking strength — less fiber, lower cost

### Common Equivalents

These are the commonly cited **typical** pairings, not exact conversions — they measure different things.

| Wall | Mullen | Typical ECT | Rule-of-thumb max box weight* |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single | 125# | 23 ECT | 20 lb |
| Single | 175# | 29 ECT | 50 lb |
| Single | **200#** | **32 ECT** | **65 lb** |
| Single | 275# | 44 ECT | 95 lb |
| Double | 200# | 42 ECT | 80 lb |
| Double | 275# | 48 ECT | 100 lb |
| Double | 350# | 51 ECT | 120 lb |
| Double | 400# | 61 ECT | 140 lb |

*Max gross weight per box from historical carrier packaging rules. These are guidance values — actual stacking performance depends on box size, environment, and handling, which you'll calculate in Module 5.

**200# / 32 ECT single wall C flute** is the workhorse shipping box of North America.

<Pitfall title="Converting Mullen to ECT Like Units">

**Common mistake:** treating "200# test" and "32 ECT" as the same box.

They're tested differently. A box rated by ECT may be made of lighter papers than a Mullen-rated box and therefore have **lower burst** strength. That's fine for stacking on a pallet — but if the product is a dense, heavy item that could punch through the side, or the box will be handled roughly in parcel networks, burst may matter. Specify the property that matches the risk.

</Pitfall>

## The Box Maker's Certificate

The round stamp on the bottom flap of many boxes is a **Box Maker's Certificate (BMC)**. It names the manufacturer and states the board's rating — either burst (Mullen) or ECT — along with size and gross weight limits. It originated in rail and motor carrier rules (Rule 41 and Item 222) that once required certified packaging for freight shipments.

---

## Key Takeaway

**Single wall** covers most boxes; **double** and **triple wall** add strength for heavy or long-term loads. Board is specified by its **papers and flute** and rated by **Mullen (burst)** or **ECT (edge crush)**. ECT predicts stacking strength and allows lighter boxes; Mullen protects against puncture and rough handling.
