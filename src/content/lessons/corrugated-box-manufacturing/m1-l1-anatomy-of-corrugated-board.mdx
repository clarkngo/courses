## It's Not Cardboard

People call it "cardboard," but in the industry, **cardboard** means a single stiff sheet like a cereal box (paperboard). A shipping box is **corrugated fiberboard** — and that difference is the whole point.

Corrugated board is a **sandwich**:

- **Linerboard** — the flat facings on the outside and inside
- **Corrugating medium** — the fluted (wavy) sheet in the middle
- **Starch adhesive** — glues the flute tips to the liners

### Why Arches Are Strong

The fluted medium works like a row of **arches** or a series of tiny **columns**. It:

- Holds the two liners apart, which makes the board **stiff** (an I-beam works the same way)
- Resists **crushing** when you press on the flat face
- Carries **vertical load** when the flutes stand upright in a box wall
- Adds **cushioning** against shock

That's why a box made from three thin sheets of paper can hold up hundreds of pounds when stacked.

## Flute Sizes

Flutes come in standard sizes, identified by letters. The letters were assigned in the order they were invented — **not** by size — which is why the list looks scrambled.

| Flute | Approx. height | Flutes per foot | Strengths | Typical uses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | ~4.8 mm (3/16") | ~33 | Best stacking and cushioning; bulky | Fragile goods; less common today |
| **C** | ~3.6–4 mm (5/32") | ~39 | Good all-round stacking and cushioning | The standard shipping box |
| **B** | ~2.5–3 mm (1/8") | ~47 | Good crush resistance; folds and die-cuts cleanly | Canned goods, die-cut displays, inner packs |
| **E** | ~1.1–1.6 mm (1/16") | ~90 | Thin, smooth print surface | Retail packaging, e-commerce mailers, pizza boxes |
| **F** | ~0.8 mm (1/32") | ~125 | Very thin and smooth | Small retail boxes, replacing folding cartons |

*Exact dimensions vary by manufacturer and corrugating roll design.*

The trade-off is simple: **bigger flutes = more stacking strength and cushioning; smaller flutes = flatter print surface, better crush resistance, and less space.**

### Take-Up Factor

Because the medium is wavy, a box plant uses more medium than liner. The **take-up factor** is the length of medium per unit length of board — roughly **1.4–1.5 for C flute** and lower for smaller flutes. Take-up matters for cost: it multiplies the medium weight in the board.

<Pitfall title="Assuming Flute Letters Mean Size">

**Common mistake:** assuming "A, B, C" goes from biggest to smallest — or smallest to biggest.

It doesn't. A is largest, then **C**, then B, then E and F. The letters reflect the order flutes were introduced. Always confirm by caliper or flutes-per-foot, especially when specifying a replacement box.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

Corrugated board is a sandwich of **liners**, a **fluted medium**, and **starch**. The arches give stiffness, compression strength, and cushioning far beyond the paper's weight. **Flute size** trades stacking strength and cushioning (A, C) against printability and thinness (B, E, F).
