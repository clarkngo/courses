## The Board's Job and Yours

Boards are responsible for overseeing strategy and risk. For AI, that means asking whether management has a credible strategy, whether it's delivering, and whether the risks are understood and controlled. Directors increasingly face questions from investors and regulators about AI oversight.

The CAIO's job is to make that oversight **possible**: clear, consistent, honest information, presented the same way each time so trends are visible.

---

## An AI Scorecard

A one-page scorecard with four sections, reported quarterly:

### 1. Value

- Realized value from production use cases, measured against baselines, with ranges where estimated.
- Pipeline value: what's expected from use cases in pilot, with confidence levels.
- Total AI spend, and spend against plan.

### 2. Adoption

- Depth of use in priority roles (not just logins).
- Number of workflows redesigned to include AI.
- Literacy program coverage, by role layer.

### 3. Risk

- AI inventory size, and count by risk tier.
- High-risk systems reviewed, and any overdue reviews.
- Incidents: count, severity, and status.
- Regulatory changes affecting the organization, and readiness.

### 4. Capability

- Platform maturity milestones.
- Key hires and skills gaps.
- Vendor concentration and lock-in exposure.

Use the same structure every quarter. Show trends, not just snapshots, and use traffic lights sparingly and honestly.

<Pitfall title="Only Good News">

**Common mistake:** presenting a highlight reel of successful pilots and impressive demos.

Boards that only hear good news either lose trust when problems surface or, worse, don't find out until a crisis. Report what's stalled, what you stopped and why, incidents, and where value fell short. Credibility built in a mixed quarter is what lets you ask for investment in a good one.

</Pitfall>

## Questions Boards Ask

Prepare clear answers to the questions directors commonly raise:

- **Strategy:** How does AI support our business strategy? What are competitors doing, and are we ahead, level, or behind?
- **Value:** What have we spent, and what have we got? How do we know?
- **Risk:** What AI do we use that could harm customers, employees, or the company? How do we know what's running?
- **Regulation:** Which laws apply, and are we ready for upcoming deadlines?
- **People:** What does AI mean for our workforce? What have we told employees?
- **Security:** How are we protecting data in AI tools, and against AI-enabled attacks?
- **Dependencies:** How dependent are we on a few AI vendors? What happens if one changes terms or fails?
- **Oversight:** Who's accountable for AI, and how does the board stay informed?

---

## Board AI Literacy

Directors need enough understanding to oversee AI, not to build it. Offer the board:

- A short **education session** each year on what AI can and can't do, using your organization's own examples.
- **Hands-on time** with the tools employees use.
- Clarity on **which committee** oversees AI risk (often audit or risk) and how often it hears from you.
- A **risk appetite statement** for AI that the board approves: what kinds of AI use the organization will and won't pursue.

A board that has agreed an AI risk appetite makes hard decisions easier, because the CAIO can point to it.

---

## Telling the Story

Numbers alone don't land. Pair the scorecard with:

- **One case study** per quarter: a use case's before and after, told through the people involved.
- **One lesson learned**: something that didn't work and what changed as a result.
- **One decision needed**: a clear ask of the board, if there is one.
