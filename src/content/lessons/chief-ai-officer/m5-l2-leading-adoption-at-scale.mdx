That's not a technology failure. It's a change-management one. Tools that require people to change habits need the same leadership as any other major change.

---

## Why Adoption Stalls

- **No clear use.** People don't know what to use AI for in *their* job.
- **Bad first experiences.** An early wrong answer convinces people the tool is useless.
- **Fear.** About job security, about being seen as lazy, or about breaking rules.
- **No time.** Learning new ways of working takes effort that nobody budgeted.
- **Workflow friction.** AI sits in a separate window instead of where work happens.
- **Mixed signals.** Leaders encourage AI use while policies, reviews, or managers discourage it.

Each cause has a different fix, so diagnose before you intervene. Short surveys and conversations with low-usage teams are often more useful than usage dashboards.

---

## Bringing Shadow AI into the Open

"Shadow AI" means employees using AI tools the organization hasn't approved, often personal accounts. Surveys consistently find it's widespread.

Shadow AI is a **risk** (data leakage, no oversight, no records) and a **signal** (people found value and the approved options didn't meet their needs). Respond to both:

1. **Provide a good sanctioned option**, with capabilities comparable to what people are using.
2. **Offer amnesty**: invite people to share what they use and for what, without penalty.
3. **Learn from it**: shadow use shows you where demand and value are.
4. **Then enforce**: once a good alternative exists, restrict unapproved tools through technical and policy controls.

<Pitfall title="Measuring Adoption by Logins">

**Common mistake:** reporting "80% of employees have used the AI assistant" as a success.

Trying something once isn't adoption. Track **depth** (how often, for which tasks) and **workflow change** (which processes now include AI by design). Those predict value; logins don't.

</Pitfall>

---

## From Tools to Workflows

The biggest gains come when AI is built into **how work is done**, not left as an optional extra:

- **Redesign the workflow**, with the team, starting from the outcome. Where does AI draft, check, route, or decide? Where does a person review?
- **Embed AI where work happens**: in the CRM, the ticketing system, the document editor, rather than a separate chat window.
- **Write down the new standard way of working**, including review steps.
- **Retire the old way**, or the new one becomes optional and fades.

---

## Incentives and Signals

People follow what's rewarded and what leaders do:

- **Leaders use AI visibly** and talk about what worked and what didn't.
- **Recognize good AI use**, including catching AI errors, not just speed.
- **Align performance goals** so that time saved can go to valued work, rather than simply raising quotas.
- **Make it safe to experiment** within guardrails.
