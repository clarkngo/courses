## Data Is Still the Bottleneck

Modern AI models arrive pre-trained, so it's tempting to think data matters less than it used to. It doesn't. The model brings general capability; **your data brings the value**: the policies a support assistant cites, the contracts a review tool reads, the history a forecasting model learns from.

Ask four questions about the data behind any priority use case:

1. **Does it exist?** Is the knowledge written down, or does it live in people's heads?
2. **Can we reach it?** Is it in systems AI can connect to, with usable access controls?
3. **Is it good enough?** Current, consistent, and correct, or full of outdated versions and contradictions?
4. **Are we allowed to use it?** Do privacy law, contracts, consent, or confidentiality limit this use?

For generative AI, "data readiness" often means **content readiness**: cleaning up document libraries, removing outdated policies, and fixing permissions so the AI doesn't surface what users shouldn't see. That last point is a common failure: an AI search tool that respects file permissions will still expose files that were shared too broadly years ago.

## A Shared AI Platform

Without a platform, every team builds its own connections to models, its own logging, and its own guardrails. A shared platform gives you consistency and lets you change things once.

Key components:

- **A model gateway.** All applications call models through one layer that handles authentication, routing, logging, cost tracking, and policy. It also lets you switch providers without rewriting every application.
- **Approved models.** A small menu of models for different needs (large and capable, small and cheap, specialized), reviewed for security and contract terms.
- **Retrieval and data access.** Standard, permission-aware ways to connect AI to enterprise content.
- **Guardrails.** Controls on sensitive data, harmful content, and, for agents, which actions they can take.
- **Evaluation.** Test sets and automated checks so teams can measure quality before release and detect regressions after model updates.
- **Observability.** Logs of inputs, outputs, and actions sufficient for debugging, audit, and incident investigation, retained according to policy.

## Avoiding Lock-In

The AI market changes every few months: new models, price cuts, new capabilities. Protect your ability to change:

- Route through a gateway rather than calling providers directly from every application.
- Keep **prompts, evaluation sets, and retrieval pipelines** as your own assets, portable across models.
- Prefer shorter commitments or flexible terms for fast-moving technology, and negotiate data-use, retention, and exit terms carefully.
- Re-evaluate model choices on a regular cadence, using your own evaluation sets rather than vendor benchmarks.

<Pitfall title="Every Team Picks Its Own Model and Vendor">

**Common mistake:** letting each business unit sign its own AI contracts and connect directly to providers.

The result is fragmented spend, inconsistent data protection terms, no shared logs, and painful migrations when a better or cheaper model appears. Central contracts and a common gateway don't slow teams down; they free them from repeating security and legal reviews.

</Pitfall>

## Managing AI Cost

AI spending behaves differently from traditional software:

- **Usage-based pricing** means costs scale with adoption, and a popular tool can blow through its budget.
- **Agents and long documents** can use far more tokens per task than simple chat.
- **Seat licenses** for AI assistants are often bought broadly and used narrowly.

Practical controls, borrowed from cloud FinOps:

- **Show cost per use case and per unit of work** (cost per resolved ticket, per document reviewed), not just a total bill.
- **Right-size models.** Use smaller, cheaper models where they meet quality needs; reserve the largest models for tasks that need them.
- **Cache and reuse** where possible.
- **Review seat utilization** quarterly and reallocate unused licenses.
- **Set budgets and alerts** at the gateway so surprises are caught in days, not at quarter end.
