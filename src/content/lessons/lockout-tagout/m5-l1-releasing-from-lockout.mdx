## Restart Is a Hazard Too

The job is done, everyone's ready to run, and production is waiting. That pressure is exactly when people get hurt: a guard left off, a wrench left on a shaft, a coworker still inside the machine.

OSHA requires a specific release procedure before locks come off.

## Step by Step

### 1. Inspect the Machine

- Remove **nonessential items**: tools, rags, parts, blocks, and shims.
- Make sure components are **operationally intact**, meaning bolts tight, belts on, couplings connected.
- Reinstall and secure **guards** and safety devices.
- Remove **blocks and pins** used for stored energy, in the correct order (only after it's safe to let the load be supported by the system again).

### 2. Account for Everyone

- Check that all employees are **safely positioned or removed** from the machine area.
- Use the **group lockbox sign-in board** or a headcount.
- Physically **look** inside enclosures, pits, and behind the machine.

### 3. Notify Affected Employees

After locks are removed and **before** the machine starts, affected employees must be told that the lockout devices have been removed.

### 4. Remove Your Own Lock

Each lockout or tagout device must be removed **by the authorized employee who applied it**. Nobody else removes your lock, and you never remove anyone else's.

### 5. Restore Energy Safely

- Return controls to **OFF** before restoring power (they should already be, from verification).
- Restore energy in a **safe order**. For example, restore air with a **soft-start**, and know whether any double-solenoid valves will move cylinders when air returns.
- Stand clear and watch the first cycle.

## When the Person Who Applied the Lock Is Gone

Sometimes a lock is left on and its owner has gone home, is on vacation, or has left the company. OSHA allows removal by someone else **only** under the employer's direction, using a **specific documented procedure** with **specific training**. At a minimum, the procedure must include:

1. **Verification** by the employer that the authorized employee who applied the device is **not at the facility**
2. **All reasonable efforts to contact** that employee to tell them their lock is being removed
3. Making sure the employee **knows the lock was removed before they resume work** at the facility

Most plants add more: a supervisor and a second authorized person **inspect the machine together**, confirm no one is inside, and document every step.

<Pitfall title="Bolt Cutters Without the Procedure">

**Common mistake:** a supervisor cuts a lock because "they must have forgotten it and gone home."

What if they didn't go home? What if they're inside the machine, or on a break and about to go back in? Locks are cut **only** after the full procedure is followed, starting with **confirming the person is not at the facility**. A forgotten lock costs some production time. A wrong assumption can cost a life.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

Before release: **inspect** the machine, reinstall **guards**, remove **blocks**, **account for everyone**, and **notify** affected employees. Each person removes **only their own lock**. An absent worker's lock comes off **only** through the documented procedure: confirm they're **not at the facility**, make **reasonable efforts to contact** them, and **inform them before they return**.
