## When You Need Energy Back, Briefly

Some jobs require power partway through: jogging a conveyor to line up a splice, bumping a motor to check rotation, or running a machine to test a repair. OSHA allows lockout devices to be **temporarily removed** for **testing or positioning**, but only in a specific sequence.

Key points:

- Treat the removal exactly like a **full release** (Lesson 5.1): inspect, notify, and account for everyone.
- When you're done testing, the machine goes back to a **fully locked and verified** state before anyone goes back in.
- **No one** works inside the danger zone while energy is restored.

<Pitfall title="Jogging While Someone Holds the Belt">

**Common mistake:** a mechanic stays inside to guide a belt while a coworker jogs the motor "just a bump."

That's servicing in the danger zone with energy restored, which is exactly what LOTO is meant to prevent. If positioning needs a hand inside, use a method that keeps hands out (hand-turning with locks on, a barring device, or a dedicated, safeguarded inching mode designed for this purpose under a documented procedure).

</Pitfall>

## Alternative Measures for Minor Servicing

Recall the minor servicing exception: routine, repetitive tasks **integral to production** don't require LOTO **if** effective alternative measures protect the worker. Examples of well-designed alternatives:

| Task | Alternative measure |
|---|---|
| Clearing a jam at a guarded infeed | **Interlocked guard** monitored by a safety-rated system that stops hazardous motion and prevents restart while open |
| Adding labels to a running labeler | **Tool or access port** that keeps hands outside the danger zone |
| Wiping a sensor lens | Sensor relocated or a cleaning port added outside the guard |
| Clearing a film wrap at a stretch wrapper | **Safety-rated stop** plus a **trapped-key** system for guard entry |

What makes them **effective**:

- Designed using **risk assessment** and machine safety standards (such as **ANSI B11** and **ISO 13849** for safety-rated controls)
- Protect against **all** hazards for that task, including stored energy
- **Documented**, with training, so the task is done the same way every time

If a task falls outside the defined alternative measure (a bigger jam, a different access point, a broken interlock), go back to **full lockout**.

## Energized Electrical Troubleshooting

Diagnosing electrical faults sometimes requires taking **voltage measurements on live circuits**. That isn't lockout; it's **energized work**, governed by electrical safe work practices such as OSHA **1910.333** and **NFPA 70E**. In general:

- Only **qualified persons** do it.
- Work on energized parts must be **justified**. De-energizing is the default.
- The hazards (shock and **arc flash**) are assessed, and appropriate **PPE**, tools, and boundaries are used.
- Many sites require an **energized electrical work permit** for anything beyond diagnostic testing.

Once troubleshooting finds the problem, the **repair** is done under **lockout**.

---

## Key Takeaway

You may temporarily remove locks to **test or position** only by following the sequence: **clear tools, clear people, remove locks per release, energize, then fully re-lock** before resuming. Minor servicing alternatives must be **engineered, documented, and effective** against all hazards. **Live electrical troubleshooting** is energized work under electrical safety rules, and repairs happen under lockout.
