## When One Lock Isn't Enough

Big jobs, such as a paper machine shutdown, a line rebuild, or a boiler outage, can involve **dozens of isolation points** and **many workers** from several crews. Every person still needs the same protection they'd get from their own personal lock.

---

## Group Lockout

OSHA 1910.147 requires group lockout procedures to give each authorized employee **a level of protection equivalent to a personal lockout device**.

### The Primary Authorized Employee

One authorized employee has **primary responsibility** for the group. They:

- Coordinate the lockout of all isolation points
- Make sure each group member has applied their personal lock
- Know the **status of everyone** on the job
- Ascertain the exposure status of each member before work and before release

When **multiple crews or departments** are involved, one authorized employee is designated to **coordinate** among the crews.

### Each Worker's Right to Verify

Each worker's personal lock on the box is their protection. Good programs also let each worker **see the verification**, or perform their own try-out, before going in. **Never** put your lock on a box for a job whose isolation you don't trust. Ask.

<Pitfall title="Locking the Box, Then Leaving the Plant">

**Common mistake:** a contractor finishes their part, forgets their lock is on the group lockbox, and leaves for the day.

Now nobody can complete the job without the special lock-removal procedure (Lesson 5.1). The fix is administrative: a **sign-in/sign-out board** at the lockbox, and a supervisor check before anyone leaves the job.

</Pitfall>

## Shift and Personnel Changes

Long jobs often span shifts. OSHA requires specific procedures to ensure **continuity of lockout protection** during shift or personnel changes, including the **orderly transfer** of devices between outgoing and incoming employees.

A safe handoff:

1. Incoming worker **applies their lock first**.
2. Outgoing worker **then removes theirs**.
3. Both review the **job status, energy status, and hazards**.
4. The handoff is **documented** (log or sign board).

At no moment should the equipment be **without** a lock from someone actively protected by it.

---

## Outside Contractors

When contractors service equipment, OSHA requires:

- The **host employer** and the **contractor** **inform each other** of their respective lockout/tagout procedures.
- The host employer ensures its employees **understand and comply** with the contractor's energy control program.

In practice, many sites require contractors to follow the **host's** procedures, use host-issued or approved locks, and attend a site orientation. What matters is that **everyone uses one agreed system** and understands it **before** work starts.

---

## Key Takeaway

**Group lockout** must protect each worker as well as a personal lock, usually with a **lockbox** where every worker adds their own lock. A **primary authorized employee** coordinates and tracks everyone. Shift changes use **lock-on-before-lock-off** handoffs. **Host and contractor** must exchange and agree on LOTO procedures before work starts.
