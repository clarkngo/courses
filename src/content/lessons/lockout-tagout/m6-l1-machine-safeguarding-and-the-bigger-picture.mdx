## LOTO Is One Layer

Lockout protects people **during servicing and maintenance**. During **normal operation**, other safeguards do the job. A strong safety system combines them.

---

Examples applied to machinery:

- **Elimination:** design so tasks never need someone in the danger zone (automatic lubrication, remote adjustments)
- **Substitution:** a lower-energy process or a slower, lower-force mechanism
- **Engineering:** guards, interlocks, light curtains, lockable isolation devices
- **Administrative:** procedures, training, signs, permits
- **PPE:** gloves, eye protection, arc-rated clothing

Lockout is strongest when machines are **designed for it**: clearly labeled, lockable isolation points that are easy to reach, and stored energy that dissipates automatically.

## Machine Guarding

OSHA **1910.212** requires guarding to protect operators and others from hazards such as **points of operation, in-running nip points, rotating parts, flying chips, and sparks**. Guards must not create new hazards and should be secured so they can't easily be removed.

| Safeguard | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Fixed guard** | Permanent barrier; removing it requires a tool |
| **Interlocked guard** | Opening it stops hazardous motion and prevents restart |
| **Adjustable / self-adjusting guard** | Adapts to different stock sizes |
| **Light curtain / presence-sensing device** | Detects a person entering and stops motion before they reach the hazard |
| **Two-hand control** | Keeps both hands on buttons away from the hazard |
| **Safety mat / area scanner** | Detects a person standing in a zone |

These devices must be **safety-rated** and correctly installed, including the **safety distance** that accounts for the machine's stopping time, according to standards such as **ANSI B11.19** and **ISO 13855**.

<Pitfall title="Treating a Light Curtain as Lockout">

**Common mistake:** a mechanic reaches past a light curtain to replace a part because "the curtain stops the press."

A light curtain is a **production safeguard**. It relies on control logic and a working brake and doesn't isolate energy. Its protection can fail. For **servicing**, lock out.

</Pitfall>

## Connections to Other Programs

| Program | How it connects to LOTO |
|---|---|
| **Permit-required confined spaces** (1910.146) | Entry requires isolating hazards such as agitators, steam lines, and process piping, often with LOTO, blinds, or double block and bleed |
| **Electrical safety** (1910.333, NFPA 70E) | Establishing an **electrically safe work condition** is lockout plus testing for absence of voltage |
| **Hot work** | Isolating fuel and process lines before welding or cutting |
| **Management of change** | New equipment and modifications must update LOTO procedures and training |
| **Contractor safety** | Coordinated energy control (Lesson 4.1) |

---

## Key Takeaway

LOTO protects during **servicing**; **guards and safety devices** protect during **operation**. Use the **hierarchy of controls**: eliminate and engineer first, with procedures and PPE as backup. Safety devices like **light curtains are not lockout**. LOTO ties into **confined space, electrical safety, hot work, and change management**.
