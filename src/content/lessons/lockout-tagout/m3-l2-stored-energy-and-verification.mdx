## Isolation Isn't Zero Energy

Opening a disconnect stops **new** energy from coming in. It does nothing about energy **already stored** in the machine. That's why stored energy and verification are separate, required steps.

---

## Stored Energy by Type

| Stored energy | Where it hides | How to control it |
|---|---|---|
| **Electrical (capacitors)** | VFD DC bus, power supplies, power-factor capacitors, servo drives | Wait the maker's discharge time (often several minutes or more, per the drive label); **measure** the DC bus before touching; ground where required |
| **Pneumatic** | Receivers, cylinders behind check valves, closed-center valves | 3/2 dump valve; bleed at the actuator; verify gauges at zero |
| **Hydraulic** | **Accumulators**, cylinders holding loads, trapped lines | Dump accumulators per procedure; lower or block loads; verify gauges at zero |
| **Gravity** | Raised rams, platens, forks, lift tables, counterweights | Lower to rest, or block with **rated** blocks, pins, or props |
| **Springs** | Return springs, tensioners, brakes | Release tension or block in place |
| **Kinetic** | Flywheels, rolls, fans, saw blades | Wait for **complete stop**; brake or block |
| **Thermal** | Steam lines, dryers, ovens, hot oil | Isolate, vent, cool; verify temperature |
| **Chemical / process** | Pipes, tanks, reactors | Isolate (double block and bleed or blind), drain, purge, vent; verify |
| **Tension in material** | Webs, coils, strapping, cables | Release or secure before cutting |

<Pitfall title="The Accumulator Nobody Listed">

**Common mistake:** a hydraulic press is locked out at the disconnect, the pump stops, and the gauge at the pump reads zero. A mechanic loosens a line to the clamp cylinder.

A **hydraulic accumulator** downstream still held 2,000 psi. Fluid sprays out at injection pressure, and the clamp moves. Every accumulator must be on the procedure with its **dump method** and a **gauge to verify** it's at zero.

</Pitfall>

## Verification: Prove It

Verification answers one question: **Can this machine hurt me right now?** Use more than one method.

### 1. The Try-Out

With everyone clear, operate the **normal start controls** (start buttons, jog, selector switches). Nothing should happen. Then **return the controls to OFF or neutral**.

Try-out proves the **machine** won't start, but **not** that every conductor is dead, which is why electrical testing is separate.

### 2. Gauges and Visual Checks

- Pneumatic and hydraulic **gauges at zero**, at the right locations
- Loads **resting or blocked**
- Rotating parts **stopped**
- Temperatures **safe**

### 3. Electrical Testing: Live-Dead-Live

Before touching electrical conductors, test for **absence of voltage** with a properly rated tester. Electrical safe work practices (such as **NFPA 70E** and OSHA's **1910.333**) describe this process. The core method is called **live-dead-live**:

Why test the meter **after**? If the tester failed during the dead test (a blown fuse or a broken lead), it would have shown "dead" on a live circuit. Re-testing on a known source proves your "dead" reading was real.

- Use a tester rated for the **voltage and category** (CAT rating) of the circuit.
- Wear the **PPE** required for the task until absence of voltage is confirmed.
- Only **qualified** persons perform electrical testing.

---

## Re-Accumulation

Some energy **comes back**: pressure can build from thermal expansion, capacitors can recover a small charge, and process lines can leak past a valve. If re-accumulation is possible, keep **monitoring** (gauges, retesting) for the duration of the job.

---

## Key Takeaway

Isolation stops **new** energy; you still have to **release or block stored energy**, including **capacitors, accumulators, gravity, springs, motion, heat, and process fluids**. Verify with a **try-out**, **gauges and visual checks**, and an electrical **live-dead-live** test. Keep checking if energy can **re-accumulate**.

<TryIt title="Find the Stored Energy">

A **hydraulic scissor lift table** with a **VFD-driven** conveyor on top needs a conveyor roller replaced. The table is raised.

List the stored energy and controls:

1. **Gravity:** raised table → lower fully, or install the maker's **maintenance props**
2. **Hydraulic:** pressure holding the table → after lowering or propping, bleed per procedure; verify the gauge
3. **Electrical:** VFD DC bus → wait the labeled discharge time; **measure** before touching drive terminals
4. **Kinetic:** conveyor rollers → wait for full stop
5. **Verification:** try-out of lift and conveyor controls, gauge at zero, live-dead-live on the motor leads

</TryIt>
