## The Annual Check-Up

OSHA requires employers to conduct a **periodic inspection** of each energy control procedure **at least annually** to make sure the procedure and the standard's requirements are being followed.

### Who Inspects

The inspection must be done by an **authorized employee other than the one(s) using the procedure** being inspected. In other words, you can't inspect your own lockout. Many plants use trained peers from another area, maintenance planners, or safety staff who are also authorized employees.

### What the Inspection Includes

- **Observe** the procedure being performed, not just read it. Is every step followed? Is every energy source isolated and verified?
- **Review responsibilities** with each authorized employee using the procedure (for lockout). Where **tagout** is used, the review covers each **authorized and affected** employee and includes the **limitations of tags**.
- **Correct** any deviations or inadequacies found.

### Certification

The employer must **certify** that periodic inspections were performed. The certification identifies:

- The **machine or equipment** the procedure was used on
- The **date** of the inspection
- The **employees** included in the inspection
- The **person** performing the inspection

Machines that share the **same procedure** can be grouped, but the inspection should still reflect real-world use.

## How LOTO Programs Fail

Lockout/tagout consistently appears on OSHA's list of the **most frequently cited** standards. The citations and incident investigations show the same patterns again and again:

| Failure | What it looks like on the floor |
|---|---|
| **No written procedures** or generic ones | A one-size "Turn off, lock, tag" sheet for every machine |
| **Procedures don't match the machine** | Missing energy sources, wrong disconnect numbers, no stored energy steps |
| **No verification** | Locks applied, nobody tries to start or tests for voltage |
| **No periodic inspections**, or "paper" inspections | Signed forms with nobody actually observed |
| **Inadequate training** | Operators don't know what affected means; new hires untrained |
| **Minor servicing misuse** | Routine jam clearing done with guards open and no effective alternative |
| **Shared or missing locks** | One lock for a crew; personal padlocks from home |
| **Unsafe lock removal** | Locks cut without the absent-employee procedure |
| **Contractor gaps** | Contractors using their own system with no coordination |

<Pitfall title="The Perfect Paperwork Program">

**Common mistake:** the program binder is flawless, with every machine documented and every inspection signed, but nobody has watched an actual lockout in years.

Auditors and investigators look at **what happens on the floor**. Real periodic inspections mean **watching** someone lock out, asking them to **explain** each step, and **fixing** what you find.

</Pitfall>

## Leading Indicators

Don't wait for an injury to measure your program. Track:

- Percentage of machines with **validated, machine-specific** procedures
- **Periodic inspections** completed on time, and number of deviations found and closed
- **Near misses** reported involving energy control
- **Training** completion by role
- Lock removal **exceptions** (each one should be reviewed)

---

## Key Takeaway

Inspect each procedure **at least annually**, done by an **authorized employee who isn't using it**, by **observing** real use and **reviewing responsibilities**. **Certify** with machine, date, employees, and inspector. Watch for the classic failures: **generic procedures, no verification, paper inspections, misuse of minor servicing, and unsafe lock removal**.

<TryIt title="Run a Mini-Inspection">

Ask a qualified coworker to perform a lockout on a machine (with real isolation). As the observer, check:

1. Did they **notify** affected employees?
2. Did they use a **normal stop** first?
3. Did they isolate **every** source listed on the procedure, and was anything missing from the procedure?
4. Did they handle **stored energy**?
5. Did they **verify** with a try-out and testing, then return controls to OFF?
6. Could they explain their **responsibilities**?

Record what you found and one improvement for the procedure.

</TryIt>
