## Lockout vs. Tagout

- **Lockout:** a **lock** holds the energy-isolating device in the safe position. It can't be removed without the key or excessive force.
- **Tagout:** a **tag** is attached to the isolating device as a **warning**. It does **not** physically stop anyone.

OSHA prefers lockout. If an isolating device **can** accept a lock, you must use lockout unless the employer can show that a tagout program gives **full employee protection**, meaning protection equivalent to lockout.

---

## Device Requirements

OSHA requires lockout and tagout devices to be:

| Requirement | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Provided by the employer** | Workers don't bring random padlocks from home |
| **Singularly identified** | Used **only** for energy control, never to lock toolboxes or lockers |
| **Durable** | Withstand the environment; tags stay legible in wet, hot, or chemical areas |
| **Standardized** | Consistent within the facility by **color, shape, or size**; tags use a consistent print and format |
| **Substantial** | Locks can't be removed without **excessive force** such as bolt cutters; tag attachments are **non-reusable, self-locking**, attachable by hand, with a minimum unlocking strength of **at least 50 pounds** (like a one-piece nylon cable tie) |
| **Identifiable** | Show **who** applied the device |

Tags must carry a clear warning such as **DO NOT START**, **DO NOT OPEN**, **DO NOT CLOSE**, **DO NOT ENERGIZE**, or **DO NOT OPERATE**.

### One Person, One Lock, One Key

Every authorized employee working on the equipment applies **their own personal lock**, and **only they hold the key**. No shared keys and no "master" lock that covers everyone without their own lock on it.

Many plants add a **photo, name, and department** label to each person's lock.

---

## The Limits of Tags

Training must make sure everyone understands that tags:

- Are **warnings**, not physical restraints
- Must **never be bypassed, ignored, or removed** without authorization
- Must be **legible and understandable** to everyone in the area
- Can be **torn off** or **deteriorate** in harsh environments
- May create a **false sense of security**

When tagout is used where a lock could fit, **additional measures** are needed for full protection, such as removing an isolating circuit element, blocking a controlling switch, opening an extra disconnect, or removing a valve handle.

## Common Lockout Hardware

| Device | Use |
|---|---|
| **Personal safety padlock** | The basic lock; non-conductive bodies are common for electrical work |
| **Hasp (scissor)** | Lets several people put locks on one point |
| **Circuit breaker lockout** | Clamps onto a breaker handle in OFF so it accepts a lock |
| **Plug lockout** | Encloses a plug so it can't be inserted |
| **Ball valve lockout** | Holds a quarter-turn handle closed |
| **Gate valve cover** | Encloses a handwheel so it can't turn |
| **Cable lockout** | Steel cable threads through several devices for one lock point |
| **Group lockbox** | Holds keys for many isolation points; each worker puts their own lock on the box |
| **Blocks, pins, and cribbing** | Physically restrain gravity loads and mechanisms |

<Pitfall title="The Lock That Isn't Really Holding Anything">

**Common mistake:** a breaker lockout device that fits loosely, or a valve lockout that lets the handle move a few degrees, or a lock through a disconnect whose handle can still be moved by forcing it.

Always **test** that the locked device truly can't be moved to the ON or OPEN position. If the hardware doesn't fit, get the right device. Don't improvise with zip ties and tape.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

**Lockout** physically prevents operation; **tagout** only warns. Devices must be **employer-provided, singularly identified, durable, standardized, substantial, and identifiable**. Tag ties need **50 lb** minimum strength. Follow **one person, one lock, one key**. Use the right hardware and **test** that it really holds.

<TryIt title="Audit Your Locks">

Look at your facility's LOTO station or your own kit:

1. Are locks a **standard** color or style used **only** for LOTO?
2. Is each lock **identified** with a name (and ideally a photo)?
3. Do tags have a clear **warning** and room for name, date, and reason?
4. Are tag ties **self-locking** and rated?
5. Do you have the right devices for every isolation point in your area (breakers, valves, plugs)?

</TryIt>
