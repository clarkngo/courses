## The Brief

A regional promoter sells concert tickets online. Today it runs on two servers in a colocation facility, and the site crashed during the last big on-sale. They want to move to AWS before the next season.

From your requirements interview:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Traffic** | About 200 requests/second normally; up to **50,000 requests/second** for the first 10 minutes of a big on-sale |
| **Correctness** | A seat must **never be sold twice** |
| **Holds** | A buyer gets **5 minutes** to pay for held seats; unpaid seats return to inventory |
| **Payments** | Through a third-party payment provider; the company wants its card-data (PCI) scope as small as possible |
| **Availability** | 99.9%; an outage during an on-sale is a headline |
| **Recovery** | RPO 1 minute; RTO 4 hours for a Regional disaster |
| **Users** | Mostly North America |
| **Team** | Six developers, no dedicated operations staff |
| **Cost** | Busy for a few hours a week; idle most of the time |

Before designing, notice what these requirements **imply**. Extreme, short spikes and a mostly idle week point to **serverless**. "Never sold twice" points to **conditional writes** or transactions. A small team points to **managed services**. A 4-hour RTO for a Regional disaster does **not** require active/active.

### Walking through it, domain by domain

**Performance (Domain 3).** The static site comes from **S3 through CloudFront**, so 50,000 people loading the page never touch the backend. A **virtual waiting room** (AWS publishes a reference solution) gives each visitor a place in line and admits them to the purchase API at a rate the backend can handle. **API Gateway** throttling is a second limit. **Lambda** scales out quickly, and **DynamoDB on-demand** absorbs bursts of writes.

**Correctness.** Holding a seat is one **conditional write** to the seat's item in DynamoDB: set `holder = me` and `holdExpires = now + 5 min` **only if** the seat is available or its hold has expired. If two buyers try at once, exactly one write succeeds. DynamoDB **TTL** isn't used to release holds, because TTL deletes can lag; the expiry timestamp in the condition is what makes a seat available again.

What happens when two buyers click the same seat at the same moment:

**Resilience (Domain 2).** Every service here is **Multi-AZ by default**. Checkout is a **Step Functions** workflow: call the payment provider with retries, confirm the order, and on failure **release the seat** (a compensating step). Order events go through **EventBridge** to an **SQS queue** for email, so a slow email service never blocks a sale. For Regional disaster, **DynamoDB point-in-time recovery** meets the 1-minute RPO within the Region, **AWS Backup** copies to a second Region, and **infrastructure as code** rebuilds the stack there within the 4-hour RTO. That's backup and restore, the cheapest strategy that meets the requirement.

**Security (Domain 1).** **AWS WAF** rate-limits by IP and filters known bots. **Cognito** handles sign-in. The browser sends card details **directly to the payment provider** (hosted fields or a redirect), so card data never touches the company's systems and PCI scope stays minimal. The provider's API key lives in **Secrets Manager**. Each Lambda function has its own least-privilege role.

**Cost (Domain 4).** Between on-sales, almost nothing runs: Lambda, API Gateway, DynamoDB on-demand, and Step Functions all charge for use. CloudFront reduces egress and origin load. No Savings Plan is needed for a workload that is idle most of the week.

## Trade-Offs to Write Down

| Decision | Chosen | Alternative | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seat inventory | DynamoDB conditional writes | Aurora with row locks | Handles extreme write bursts with no connection limits or capacity planning; Aurora would work at lower peaks and suits complex reporting |
| API compute | Lambda | ECS on Fargate | Scales to zero and absorbs bursts; Fargate is better for steady load or long-running connections |
| Surge control | Waiting room + throttling | Pre-scaling only | Protects the payment provider and DynamoDB from a thundering herd, and is fairer to buyers |
| DR | Backup and restore to a second Region | Warm standby | A 4-hour RTO allows it, at a fraction of the cost |

<Pitfall title="Load-Testing Only Your Own Side">

**Common mistake:** the team load-tests Lambda and DynamoDB at 50,000 requests per second and declares victory.

The **payment provider** has its own rate limits, and **service quotas** (Lambda concurrency, API Gateway throttles) have default values that may be too low. Find each downstream limit, set the waiting room's admission rate **below** the tightest one, and request quota increases weeks before the on-sale.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

Let the requirements drive the design: spikes and idle time point to **serverless**, correctness points to **conditional writes**, a small team points to **managed services**, and the RTO decides the **DR strategy**. Protect the backend with **caching, a waiting room, and throttling**, isolate slow work with **events and queues**, and write down every **trade-off**.

3. Traffic becomes **steady** at 5,000 requests per second all day, every day.
