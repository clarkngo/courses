## Availability Zones and Regions

A **Region** is a geographic area (such as `us-east-1`). Each Region has multiple **Availability Zones (AZs)**: one or more data centers with independent power, cooling, and networking, connected by low-latency links.

- **High availability (HA)** means the system keeps working when a component or an AZ fails. On AWS, that almost always means **Multi-AZ**.
- **Fault tolerance** goes a step further: the failure causes **no** user-visible interruption, usually because redundant capacity is already running.
- **Disaster recovery (DR)** is how you recover from a larger event, such as the loss of a whole Region, a bad deployment, or deleted data.

Read the question carefully. If it says "highly available," Multi-AZ is usually enough. Multi-Region is the answer only when the question mentions **Regional outages**, **global users**, or a very low RTO/RPO across Regions.

The pattern: spread every tier across **at least two AZs**, size each AZ so the others can carry the load if one fails, and use services that are already Multi-AZ (S3, DynamoDB, SQS, Lambda, EFS) wherever you can.

## Database Resilience Options

| Option | What it does | Readable? | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **RDS Multi-AZ (instance)** | Synchronous standby in another AZ; automatic failover, usually in a minute or two | No, standby only | High availability |
| **RDS Multi-AZ DB cluster** | One writer and **two readable standbys** in three AZs | Yes | HA plus read capacity, faster failover |
| **RDS read replicas** | **Asynchronous** copies, in-Region or **cross-Region**; can be promoted | Yes | Scaling reads; cross-Region DR |
| **Aurora** | Storage keeps **six copies across three AZs**; up to 15 low-lag replicas; fast failover | Yes | HA and read scaling with less to manage |
| **Aurora Global Database** | Replicates to other Regions, typically with under a second of lag | Yes, in secondary Regions | Low-RPO cross-Region DR and local global reads |
| **DynamoDB global tables** | Multi-Region, multi-active replication | Yes, read and write in every Region | Global apps that write in many Regions |

Remember the difference: **Multi-AZ is for availability**, and **read replicas are for read scaling** (plus cross-Region DR). Exam answers that use a read replica for automatic failover, or a classic Multi-AZ standby to serve reads, are wrong.

## Route 53: Routing and Failover

| Routing policy | Use it when |
|---|---|
| **Simple** | One resource, no health checks |
| **Failover** | Active-passive: send traffic to the secondary when the primary's **health check** fails |
| **Weighted** | Split traffic by percentage (canary or blue/green releases) |
| **Latency** | Send users to the Region with the lowest latency for them |
| **Geolocation** | Route by the user's country or continent (compliance, localized content) |
| **Geoproximity** | Route by distance, with a bias to shift traffic between Regions |
| **Multivalue answer** | Return several healthy IPs for simple client-side load spreading |
| **IP-based** | Route by the client's IP range |

**Alias records** point a domain name (including the zone apex, like `example.com`) at AWS resources such as an ALB, CloudFront distribution, or S3 website, at no query charge.

## Backups and the Four DR Strategies

**AWS Backup** centralizes backup policies across EBS, RDS, Aurora, DynamoDB, EFS, FSx, S3, and more, including **cross-Region and cross-account copies**. Copies in a separate account protect you from someone deleting both the data and its backups.

DR strategies, from cheapest and slowest to most expensive and fastest:

| Strategy | What runs in the DR Region | RTO / RPO |
|---|---|---|
| **Backup and restore** | Nothing but backups | Hours |
| **Pilot light** | Data is replicated and live; servers are off or minimal | Tens of minutes |
| **Warm standby** | A scaled-down but fully working copy of production | Minutes |
| **Multi-site active/active** | Full production in more than one Region, serving traffic | Near zero |

Choose the **cheapest strategy that meets the RTO and RPO** in the question.

<Pitfall title="Backups You Have Never Restored">

**Common mistake:** the design says "nightly snapshots" and stops there.

An untested backup is a hope, not a plan. Real DR plans include **restore tests**, documented runbooks, and the **service quotas** needed in the recovery Region. Exam questions also test the time dimension: a nightly backup can't meet a 15-minute RPO.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

**Multi-AZ** is the default for high availability; go **multi-Region** only when Regional failure or global users are part of the requirement. Know which database feature gives **failover** (Multi-AZ) and which gives **read scaling** (read replicas), how **Route 53 failover** works with health checks, and pick the **cheapest DR strategy** that meets the RTO and RPO.

<TryIt title="Design for the RTO and RPO">

For each scenario, pick a DR strategy and the main services. Then compare with the suggested answers.

1. An internal reporting app. RTO 24 hours, RPO 24 hours.
2. An e-commerce checkout. RTO 15 minutes, RPO 1 minute.
3. A global trading platform. RTO and RPO near zero.

Suggested answers: (1) backup and restore with AWS Backup cross-Region copies; (2) warm standby with Aurora Global Database and a scaled-down ASG plus Route 53 failover; (3) multi-site active/active with DynamoDB global tables or Aurora Global Database, latency-based routing, and full capacity in each Region.
