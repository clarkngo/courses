## Cost Is a Design Requirement

Cost isn't something you fix after launch. It's a requirement like availability. The cost-optimized answer on the exam is the **cheapest design that still meets every stated requirement**, not the cheapest design overall.

The big levers, in rough order of impact:

1. **Don't run what you don't need** (turn off dev environments at night, delete idle resources)
2. **Right-size** what you do run
3. **Pay less per unit** with commitments or Spot
4. **Use the right storage tier** for how often data is accessed
5. **Reduce data transfer** (covered in the next lesson)

## EC2 Purchase Options

| Option | Discount vs On-Demand | Commitment | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **On-Demand** | None | None | Short-term, unpredictable, or new workloads |
| **Compute Savings Plans** | Up to 66% | $/hour for 1 or 3 years | Steady usage that may change instance family, Region, or move to Fargate or Lambda |
| **EC2 Instance Savings Plans** | Up to 72% | $/hour for 1 or 3 years in one instance family and Region | Steady usage on a known family |
| **Reserved Instances** | Up to 72% | A specific instance configuration for 1 or 3 years | Steady usage; also available for RDS, ElastiCache, Redshift, OpenSearch |
| **Spot Instances** | Up to 90% | None, but AWS can reclaim them with a **2-minute warning** | Fault-tolerant, flexible work: batch, CI, rendering, stateless web tiers |
| **Dedicated Hosts** | — | Optional | Licensing tied to physical cores or sockets, compliance |

Discount figures are AWS's "up to" numbers. Actual savings depend on term, payment option, and instance.

### Right-sizing and Graviton

- **AWS Compute Optimizer** analyzes utilization and recommends smaller or different instance types, EBS volumes, and Lambda memory settings.
- **Graviton** (Arm) instances usually cost less than comparable x86 instances and often perform better, if your software runs on Arm.
- **Auto Scaling** removes capacity when it isn't needed. A fleet sized for peak all day wastes money all night.

### Serverless economics

Lambda and Fargate charge for what runs, so **idle costs nothing** (Lambda) or very little. For spiky or low-volume workloads, serverless is often far cheaper than an always-on instance. For very high, steady load, well-utilized instances with Savings Plans can win. Compute Savings Plans cover Lambda and Fargate too.

<Pitfall title="Spot for Work That Can't Be Interrupted">

**Common mistake:** a design runs a single-instance production database or a long, non-checkpointed job on Spot "to save 90%."

Spot capacity can be reclaimed with a **2-minute notice**. Use it only for work that is **stateless, checkpointed, or spread across many instances** that can absorb the loss of a few, and diversify across instance types and AZs.

</Pitfall>

## S3 Storage Classes

| Class | Access pattern | Retrieval | Minimum storage duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S3 Standard** | Frequent | Milliseconds | None |
| **S3 Intelligent-Tiering** | Unknown or changing | Milliseconds (optional archive tiers are slower) | None; small monitoring fee per object |
| **S3 Standard-IA** | Infrequent, but needs fast access | Milliseconds, per-GB retrieval fee | 30 days |
| **S3 One Zone-IA** | Infrequent, re-creatable data | Milliseconds; stored in **one AZ** | 30 days |
| **S3 Glacier Instant Retrieval** | Rare (about once a quarter), needs instant access | Milliseconds | 90 days |
| **S3 Glacier Flexible Retrieval** | Archive | Minutes to hours | 90 days |
| **S3 Glacier Deep Archive** | Long-term archive, rarely if ever read | Within 12 hours (standard) or 48 hours (bulk) | 180 days |

The classes trade storage price against access price:

Also add lifecycle rules that **delete old object versions** in versioned buckets and **abort incomplete multipart uploads**. Both quietly add up.

### EBS and file storage savings

- Move **gp2 to gp3**: usually about 20% cheaper per GB, with performance you can tune separately.
- Use **st1 or sc1** for large sequential data that doesn't need SSD.
- Delete **unattached volumes** and old **snapshots**; archive rarely needed snapshots with **EBS Snapshots Archive**.
- Turn on **EFS lifecycle management** to move cold files to the Infrequent Access or Archive class.

## Key Takeaway

Layer **Savings Plans or RIs** under the steady baseline, **On-Demand** for the predictable rise, and **Spot** for interruptible work. Right-size with **Compute Optimizer**, consider **Graviton**, and use **serverless** for spiky load. Match **S3 storage classes** to access patterns, automate moves with **lifecycle policies**, and move EBS to **gp3**.
