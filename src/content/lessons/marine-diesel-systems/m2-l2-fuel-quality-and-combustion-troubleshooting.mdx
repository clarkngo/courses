## The Three Enemies of Fuel

### 1. Water

Water gets in through condensation in partly empty tanks, leaking deck fills and vents, and contaminated bunkers. It:

- Damages injectors and pumps (water doesn't lubricate)
- Causes rough running and power loss
- Feeds microbial growth

**Drain** settling and service tanks and filter bowls regularly. Keep tanks **full** where practical to reduce condensation.

### 2. Microbial Growth ("Diesel Bug")

Bacteria and fungi live at the **fuel-water interface** and form dark, slimy sludge that clogs filters fast. Signs: rapid filter plugging, black or brown slime in filter bowls, a sulfur or rotten smell.

Control it by **removing water**, cleaning tanks, and using **biocides** only as directed.

### 3. Air

Air in the fuel lines stops a diesel. It enters after running a tank dry, changing filters, or through a loose fitting on the **suction side** (which leaks **air in**, not fuel out).

---

## Bleeding the Fuel System

After a filter change or running out of fuel, the system must be **bled** to remove air. The general approach (always follow the engine manual):

1. Fill new filters with **clean** fuel if the design allows.
2. Open the bleed screw on the **first filter**; pump the **hand primer** until fuel flows without bubbles; close the screw.
3. Repeat at the **secondary filter**, then at the **injection pump**.
4. If the engine still won't start, crack the **injector line nuts** (with eye protection), crank briefly until fuel appears, and retighten.
5. Fix the **cause** of the air leak.

## Reading the Smoke

Exhaust color is a fast, useful diagnostic.

| Smoke color | What it usually means | Common causes |
|---|---|---|
| **Black / dark gray** | Too much fuel for the available air (incomplete combustion) | Overload, dirty air filter, turbocharger problem, faulty injector, wrong timing, fouled hull or propeller |
| **Blue** | Burning **lube oil** | Worn rings or liners, worn valve guides, turbo seal leak, overfilled sump |
| **White** | Unburned fuel or water vapor | Cold engine, low compression, late timing, **water in fuel**, coolant leak into the cylinder |

A little dark smoke during **acceleration** or white smoke during a **cold start** can be normal. Persistent smoke at steady load is not.

## Exhaust Temperatures and Cylinder Balance

Many engines have a **pyrometer** (exhaust temperature sensor) on each cylinder. At a steady load, all cylinders should read **close to one another**.

| Pattern | Possible cause |
|---|---|
| **One cylinder much higher** | Dribbling or leaking injector, late injection, leaking exhaust valve |
| **One cylinder much lower** | Injector not firing or blocked, fuel pump problem, failed sensor |
| **All cylinders high** | Overload, restricted air intake, turbocharger fouling, high intake air temperature, fouled hull |
| **Rising trend over weeks** | Gradual turbo or charge air cooler fouling; dirty air filters |

Larger engines also measure **firing pressure (Pmax)** and **compression pressure** to diagnose combustion directly.

<Pitfall title="Chasing the Thermometer, Not the Trend">

**Common mistake:** looking only at today's exhaust temperatures with no history.

A cylinder 20 °C above the others might be normal for that engine, or it might be a new problem. Log temperatures at the same load every watch. **Changes from baseline** are what matter.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

Keep fuel **dry**, watch for **microbial sludge**, and **bleed** air after filter changes or running dry. **Black** smoke means too much fuel or too little air; **blue** means oil burning; **white** means unburned fuel or water. Use **cylinder exhaust temperature balance** and **trends** to spot combustion problems early.

<TryIt title="Diagnose the Engine">

A tug's main engine shows:

- **Black smoke** under load
- **All** cylinder exhaust temperatures about 40 °C higher than last month at the same rpm
- Turbo boost pressure **lower** than normal

What's the likely problem area? *(Air supply. Probably a fouled or damaged turbocharger, a dirty intake filter, or a fouled charge air cooler. Less air means too much fuel for the air available, so all cylinders run hot.)*

What might you check outside the engine? *(Hull and propeller fouling increase load at the same rpm.)*
