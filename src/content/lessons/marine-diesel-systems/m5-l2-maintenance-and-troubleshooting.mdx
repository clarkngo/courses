## Planned Maintenance Systems

Vessels run a **planned maintenance system (PMS)** that schedules tasks by **running hours** or **calendar time**, based on the maker's manuals, classification society requirements, and the operator's experience. The PMS tracks:

- Tasks due and overdue
- Work done, by whom, with what parts
- Spare parts inventory
- Running hours for each machine

Under the **ISM Code** (International Safety Management), ships covered by it must maintain equipment per documented procedures, and maintenance records are part of audits and inspections.

---

## Common Maintenance Tasks

| Task | Typical trigger | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Oil and filter change** | Running hours or oil analysis | Take an oil sample first; check old filter for metal |
| **Fuel filter change** | Hours or differential pressure | Bleed the system afterward |
| **Raw water impeller** | Hours or annually | Carry spares; check for missing vanes downstream |
| **Zincs / anodes** | Inspection; replace when about half consumed (or per maker) | Clean the contact surface |
| **Valve lash (clearance) adjustment** | Running hours | Engine **cold**, cylinder at **TDC on compression**, use feeler gauges per maker spec |
| **Injector testing / overhaul** | Hours or symptoms | Check opening (pop) pressure, spray pattern, leakage on a test bench |
| **Heat exchanger cleaning** | Hours or rising temperatures | Rod or chemically clean tubes; replace gaskets and zincs |
| **Belt inspection** | Regularly | Tension, cracks, glazing |
| **Turbo wash** | Per maker | Compressor and turbine side |
| **Air filter** | Differential pressure or hours | Never run without one |

### Valve Lash

Valves need a small clearance ("lash") between the rocker arm and valve stem so they **close fully** when the engine is hot.

- **Too little clearance:** valves may not seat when hot, causing burned valves, low compression, and hard starting.
- **Too much clearance:** noisy valve train, valves open late and close early, power loss, and wear.

## Troubleshooting the Classic Faults

### Won't Start (Cranks but Doesn't Fire)

| Check | Possible problem |
|---|---|
| Fuel reaching injectors? | Empty tank, closed valve, clogged filter, **air in lines**, fuel shutoff solenoid not energized |
| Cranking speed fast enough? | Weak batteries, low starting air pressure |
| Air intake clear? | Blocked filter, closed damper |
| Engine cold? | Use pre-heat or glow plugs if fitted |
| Compression OK? | Worn rings, burnt valves, wrong lash |

### Overheating

See Lesson 3.2: check **raw water flow first** (sea valve, strainer, impeller), then coolant level, heat exchanger, thermostat, and load.

### Low Oil Pressure

**Reduce load or stop.** Then check oil level, oil dilution (smell for fuel; check for milky coolant), filter condition, pressure sensor accuracy (compare to a mechanical gauge), and pump and bearing wear.

### Loss of Power

- **Fuel:** restricted filter, air in fuel, bad injector, contaminated fuel
- **Air:** dirty filter, turbo or cooler fouling
- **Exhaust:** high back pressure
- **External:** fouled hull or propeller, line or net in the prop, wrong propeller
- **Governor or controls** problem

### Vibration

- **Engine mounts** loose or failed
- **Shaft misalignment** between engine/gear and shaft (soft mounts and hull flex make alignment critical)
- **Damaged propeller** (bent blade, missing piece, fouling)
- A **misfiring cylinder**
- **Coupling** wear

<Pitfall title="Changing Parts at Sea Without Isolating">

**Common mistake:** changing a fuel filter on the running generator because "it's a duplex filter, it's fine," or working on the raw water strainer with the sea valve open.

Duplex filters and strainers can be changed in service **only** by following the correct changeover and isolation procedure. A sea strainer opened with the sea valve open will **flood the engine room**. Know which valves isolate what, and follow your vessel's lockout and isolation procedures (see the Lockout/Tagout course).

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

A **PMS** schedules work by **hours and calendar** and keeps records. Core tasks include **oil and filters, impellers, zincs, valve lash, injectors, and heat exchangers**. Troubleshoot systematically: **won't start** (fuel, cranking speed, air, heat, compression), **overheating** (raw water first), **low oil pressure** (stop or reduce load), **loss of power** (fuel, air, exhaust, hull and prop), and **vibration** (mounts, alignment, prop).
