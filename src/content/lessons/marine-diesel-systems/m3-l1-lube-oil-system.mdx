## What Lube Oil Does

Lube oil is the engine's lifeblood. It:

- **Lubricates** bearings, pistons, liners, and gears
- **Cools** pistons and bearings by carrying heat away
- **Cleans**, carrying soot and wear particles to the filters
- **Neutralizes acids** formed from sulfur in the fuel
- **Seals** between rings and liners
- **Protects** against corrosion

Low oil pressure is one of the few alarms that should make you **slow down or stop the engine immediately**.

### Wet Sump vs. Dry Sump

- **Wet sump:** oil lives in the engine's oil pan. Common on smaller engines.
- **Dry sump:** oil drains to a separate **sump tank** below the engine and is circulated by a separate pump. Common on larger engines, which also run oil through a **centrifugal separator** to remove water and soot.

### Pre-Lubrication

Many larger engines have an electric **pre-lube pump** that pressurizes the oil system **before starting**, so bearings aren't dry for the first seconds of rotation.

## Reading Oil Pressure and Temperature

| Reading | Possible causes |
|---|---|
| **Low oil pressure** | Low oil level, clogged filter or strainer, worn pump, worn bearings, oil diluted with fuel, oil too hot (thin) |
| **High oil pressure** | Cold oil, blocked passage, stuck relief valve |
| **High oil temperature** | Fouled or failed oil cooler, cooling water problem, overload |
| **Rising oil level** | **Fuel dilution** or **coolant leak** into the oil, which is serious |
| **Falling oil level** | Leaks, burning oil (blue smoke), worn rings |

Check level at the **same condition every time** (e.g., engine stopped for a set time, or on the dipstick's running mark if the maker specifies it).

## TBN and Cylinder Oil

Sulfur in fuel burns to form **sulfuric acid**, which corrodes liners and rings. Lube oil contains **alkaline additives** measured as **TBN (Total Base Number)** to neutralize it.

- Higher-sulfur fuels need **higher-TBN oils**.
- Large two-stroke engines use a **separate cylinder oil** injected directly onto the liners, with TBN matched to the fuel's sulfur content. Using a high-TBN oil with very low sulfur fuel can cause **deposits**, so engine makers specify combinations.

## Oil Analysis

Regular lab analysis spots problems early:

| Result | May indicate |
|---|---|
| **Iron, chromium** rising | Liner, ring, or crankshaft wear |
| **Copper, lead, tin** rising | Bearing wear |
| **Aluminum** | Piston or bearing wear (depends on engine design) |
| **Silicon** | Dirt entering through the air intake |
| **Sodium, potassium**, glycol | **Coolant** leak |
| **Low viscosity, low flash point** | **Fuel dilution** |
| **TBN falling** | Oil being depleted; change interval or oil grade may need review |
| **Water** | Condensation, cooler leak, or seawater entry |

## Crankcase Safety

Hot spots in the crankcase (a failing bearing, a seized piston) can create **oil mist** that can **ignite** and explode. Larger engines have:

- **Oil mist detectors** that alarm and slow the engine
- **Crankcase relief (explosion) doors** that vent pressure safely
- **Bearing temperature monitoring**

<Pitfall title="Opening the Crankcase Right After a Mist Alarm">

**Common mistake:** after an oil mist alarm, someone opens a crankcase door to look for the hot spot.

Fresh air rushing into hot oil mist can trigger an explosion. Follow the engine maker's procedure: **stop the engine**, keep people **away** from crankcase doors, and wait for the engine to **cool down** (often a significant time, as specified) before opening.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

Lube oil **lubricates, cools, cleans, neutralizes acid, and seals**. **Low oil pressure** demands immediate action. **Rising oil level** means fuel or coolant contamination. **TBN** neutralizes sulfur acids. Use **oil analysis** to spot wear early, and respect **crankcase mist** alarms.
