| Component | Job |
|---|---|
| **Main engine** | Produces power |
| **Reduction gear** | Lowers speed to the propeller's efficient rpm; often includes a **clutch** and **reverse** gearing on smaller vessels |
| **Thrust bearing** | Takes the propeller's **push** and transfers it to the hull. This is what actually moves the ship. |
| **Line shaft and bearings** | Carry the shaft through the hull |
| **Stern tube and bearings** | Support the shaft where it passes through the hull; lubricated by **water** or **oil** |
| **Shaft seal** | Keeps seawater out (or oil in); can be a traditional **packing gland** or a **mechanical face seal** |
| **Propeller** | Converts rotation into thrust |

A traditional packing gland is supposed to **drip slightly** to cool and lubricate the packing. Mechanical seals should be essentially dry. Know which your vessel has before you "fix" a drip.

## Propeller and Drive Types

| Type | How it works | Common on |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed-pitch propeller (FPP)** | Blades fixed; speed and direction controlled by engine rpm and reverse gear (or reversing the engine on large two-strokes) | Most fishing vessels, many cargo ships |
| **Controllable-pitch propeller (CPP)** | Hydraulics rotate the blades; engine can run at constant speed while pitch controls thrust and direction | Ferries, tugs, offshore vessels, ships with shaft generators |
| **Azimuth / Z-drive** | The propeller unit rotates 360° to steer and thrust in any direction | Tractor tugs, many ferries, offshore supply vessels |
| **Waterjet** | Pump draws water and jets it astern; steering by deflecting the jet | Fast ferries, patrol boats |
| **Diesel-electric** | Diesel generators power electric propulsion motors | Cruise ships, icebreakers, research vessels, some ferries |

## The Engine Room Systems Map

The main engine is the heart, but an engine room is full of **auxiliary systems** that keep it and the vessel running. This course follows them in order.

| System | Module |
|---|---|
| **Fuel** storage, transfer, purification, injection | Module 2 |
| **Lube oil** and **cooling water** | Module 3 |
| **Air intake, turbocharging, starting air, exhaust** | Module 4 |
| **Generators**, switchboard, and electrical power | Mentioned throughout |
| **Bilge**, ballast, and **oily water separator** | Module 6 |
| **Steering gear** | Hydraulic rams or rotary vane steering the rudder |
| **Compressed air**, fresh water making, sewage treatment, HVAC | Auxiliary services |

<Pitfall title="Tightening the Packing Gland Until It's Dry">

**Common mistake:** a new crew member sees seawater dripping from a traditional stuffing box and tightens the gland nuts until it stops.

Packing needs a slow drip (often described as a few drops per minute while the shaft turns, per the builder's guidance) to stay cool. Overtightened packing overheats, scores the shaft, and can fail badly. Adjust a little at a time, evenly, with the shaft turning, and check temperature.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

Power flows **engine → reduction gear → thrust bearing → shaft → stern tube → propeller**. The **thrust bearing** passes the propeller's push to the hull. Propulsion may be **fixed-pitch**, **controllable-pitch**, **azimuth**, **waterjet**, or **diesel-electric**. Around the main engine are the fuel, lube, cooling, air, and exhaust systems covered next.
