## Engine Room Fire: The Big One

Engine rooms combine **fuel, oil, hot surfaces, and electricity** in a tight space. Engine room fires are among the most dangerous events at sea.

### The Classic Cause

The most common source is **fuel or lube oil spraying or dripping onto a hot surface**, such as an exhaust manifold, turbocharger, or indicator cock. Prevention:

- Keep **insulation and shielding** intact on hot surfaces; replace any removed during maintenance.
- Maintain **jacketed high-pressure fuel lines** and leak alarms.
- Fix **leaks** immediately; keep **bilges and drip trays clean**.
- Store **oily rags** in closed metal containers.
- Don't use engine room surfaces as storage for flammables.

### Detection and Fixed Systems

Engine rooms on larger vessels have **fire detection** and **fixed fire-extinguishing systems**, commonly **CO₂**, **high-expansion foam**, or **water mist**. A CO₂ system:

- **Requires everyone to evacuate** before release. CO₂ at extinguishing concentration is **lethal**.
- Comes with **pre-discharge alarms** and procedures to **shut down ventilation and fuel** and close openings first.
- Works only if the space is **sealed**.

Know your vessel's **fire plan**, **emergency fuel shut-offs** (often remote, outside the engine room), **ventilation shut-downs**, and muster station.

<Pitfall title="Re-Entering Too Soon">

**Common mistake:** after a fixed system discharge, crew open the engine room to "check" a few minutes later.

Fresh air can **re-ignite** hot surfaces, and the atmosphere may still be lethal. Follow the vessel's procedures for **cooling time**, boundary cooling, and re-entry with breathing apparatus.

</Pitfall>

## Other Engine Room Hazards

| Hazard | Controls |
|---|---|
| **Rotating machinery** (shafts, couplings, belts) | Guards in place; no loose clothing or jewelry; never work on running shafts |
| **Noise** | Engine rooms often exceed safe levels; wear hearing protection |
| **Heat** | Hydration, rest breaks, ventilation; insulated surfaces |
| **Hot fluids** | Never open hot pressurized coolant caps; let systems cool |
| **Hazardous energy** | Lockout and isolation before maintenance (see the Lockout/Tagout course) |
| **Enclosed spaces** (tanks, voids, cofferdams) | Oxygen-deficient or toxic atmospheres; follow **enclosed space entry** permits, testing, ventilation, and a standby person |
| **Slips and falls** | Oily deck plates, ladders; clean spills, use handrails |
| **Batteries** | Hydrogen gas; ventilate; no sparks |

## MARPOL and the Engine Room

**MARPOL** is the international convention for preventing pollution from ships. Several annexes affect engineers.

### Annex I: Oil

- Oily bilge water may be discharged at sea **only** through an approved **oily water separator (OWS)** with an **oil content meter** that stops discharge above **15 ppm** oil, and only when other conditions are met (such as the ship being en route and outside special areas where stricter rules apply).
- Oily residues (sludge) go to a **sludge tank** and are landed ashore or incinerated as allowed.
- Every transfer, discharge, and sludge disposal is recorded in the **Oil Record Book**.

Bypassing the OWS (a so-called **"magic pipe"**) is a serious crime. In the U.S., it has led to large fines and prison sentences for companies and individual engineers.

### Annex VI: Air

Fuel sulfur and NOx limits (Lessons 2.1 and 4.2), plus rules on ozone-depleting substances and incinerators.

### Annex V: Garbage

Strict limits on discharging garbage; **plastics may never be discharged** at sea. Oily rags and used filters are garbage that goes ashore.

### Smaller Vessels

U.S. vessels are also governed by **U.S. Coast Guard** regulations and the **Clean Water Act**. Even small boats must not discharge oil or oily water that causes a sheen; report spills to the **National Response Center**.

## Key Takeaway

Fuel or oil on **hot surfaces** is the classic engine room fire. Keep **insulation**, **jacketed lines**, and **clean bilges**. Know your **fire plan**, **remote shut-offs**, and the rule that **everyone gets out before CO₂ discharge**. Follow **MARPOL**: the **OWS limit is 15 ppm**, record everything in the **Oil Record Book**, and never bypass the separator.
