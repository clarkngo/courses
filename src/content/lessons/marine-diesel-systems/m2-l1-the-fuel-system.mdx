## Clean Fuel, Reliable Engine

Injection systems have clearances measured in **microns**. Water, dirt, and microbial sludge in fuel cause worn injectors, stuck pumps, poor combustion, and engine stops at the worst moment. Most of the fuel system exists to make fuel **clean, dry, and at the right temperature and pressure**.

On smaller vessels, the path is shorter: tanks, a **primary filter/water separator** (often a spin-on or bowl type), the engine's lift pump, a **secondary filter**, and the injection system.

## Marine Fuel Grades

| Fuel | Description | Typical users |
|---|---|---|
| **MGO (marine gas oil)** | Distillate, similar to diesel; clean and easy to handle | Small and medium vessels; large ships in emission control areas |
| **MDO (marine diesel oil)** | Distillate with some heavier components | Medium-speed engines |
| **VLSFO** | Very low sulfur fuel oil (≤0.50% sulfur), often blended | Large ships since 2020 |
| **HFO (heavy fuel oil)** | Residual fuel, very viscous; must be **heated** to pump and inject | Large ships with exhaust gas scrubbers |
| **LNG, methanol, others** | Alternative fuels in dual-fuel engines | Growing in newer vessels |

### MARPOL Annex VI Sulfur Limits

International rules under **MARPOL Annex VI** cap fuel sulfur content:

- **0.50%** globally (since **January 1, 2020**)
- **0.10%** inside **Emission Control Areas (ECAs)**, which include the **North American ECA** along U.S. and Canadian coasts

Ships can comply by burning compliant fuel or by using an approved equivalent such as an **exhaust gas cleaning system (scrubber)** with higher-sulfur fuel, where allowed.

## Bunkering

Taking on fuel is a high-risk operation for spills and fires:

- Follow the vessel's **bunkering procedure and checklist**; agree on rates and signals with the supplier.
- Keep **scuppers plugged** and spill kits ready.
- Monitor **tank levels** continuously; never leave the manifold unattended.
- Take a **representative sample** (often by continuous drip sampler) for testing and records.
- Get the **bunker delivery note (BDN)**, which states quantity, density, and sulfur content.

---

## Purifiers (Centrifugal Separators)

Larger vessels use **centrifugal separators** that spin fuel at high speed. Heavier water and solids are flung outward and removed, while clean fuel flows from the center.

- A **purifier** removes water and solids, with a water seal and gravity disc sized for the fuel's density.
- A **clarifier** removes mainly solids.
- Heating fuel to the right temperature (as specified for the fuel and separator) lowers viscosity and improves separation.

## Injection Systems

### Mechanical (Jerk Pump) Injection

A camshaft-driven **injection pump** for each cylinder (or a single in-line or rotary pump) pressurizes fuel and sends it to the injector at the right moment. The injector's spring-loaded needle opens at a set **pop pressure** and sprays fuel into the cylinder.

### Common Rail

A high-pressure pump keeps fuel in a shared **rail** at very high pressure (often above **1,000 bar** on modern engines). **Electronically controlled injectors** open exactly when and as long as the engine controller commands. Benefits include cleaner combustion, quieter running, and flexible injection timing, but the system is **extremely sensitive to dirty fuel and water**.

### High-Pressure Fuel Safety

Fuel lines between the pump and injector carry fuel at very high pressure. International rules (SOLAS) require high-pressure fuel lines on many vessels to be **jacketed (double-walled)** with leak detection, so a cracked line can't spray fuel onto a hot exhaust manifold.

<Pitfall title="Feeling for a Leak">

**Common mistake:** running a hand along a high-pressure fuel line to find a leak.

A pinhole leak at injection pressure can inject fuel **through your skin** and into tissue. It's a medical emergency even if it looks minor. Find leaks with **cardboard or paper** held near the line, with the engine stopped whenever possible, and wear eye protection.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

Fuel flows **bunker → storage → settling → separator → service tank → filters → injection**. **MARPOL Annex VI** limits sulfur to **0.50%** globally and **0.10%** in ECAs. **Purifiers** spin out water and solids. **Mechanical** and **common-rail** systems inject at very high pressure, so keep fuel clean, use **jacketed lines**, and **never** feel for high-pressure leaks with your hand.
