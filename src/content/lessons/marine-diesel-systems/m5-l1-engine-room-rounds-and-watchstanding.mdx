## The Watch Keeps the Ship Running

Whether it's a licensed engineer on a cargo ship or a deckhand-engineer on a crabber, someone is responsible for the machinery. Good watchstanding finds small problems **before** they become breakdowns at sea.

---

## Pre-Start Checks

Before starting a main or generator engine (adapt to your vessel's checklist):

- **Fluid levels:** lube oil, coolant expansion tank, reduction gear oil, fuel day tank
- **Valves:** sea valves open, fuel valves open, exhaust and cooling valves set
- **Drains:** water drained from fuel filters, air receivers
- **Bilges:** dry and clean; check for new leaks
- **Belts and guards:** in place and tight
- **Turning gear:** **disengaged**
- **Pre-lube:** run the pre-lube pump if fitted
- **Indicator cocks** (on larger engines): blow the engine over on air with cocks open to expel any water or fuel from cylinders, then close them
- **Communication:** tell the bridge or operator you're starting

## Starting and Warm-Up

After starting, check immediately:

1. **Oil pressure** comes up within seconds
2. **Raw water** discharge is flowing
3. No unusual **noise, vibration, smoke, or leaks**

Let the engine **warm up** at light load before applying full power. Cold engines wear faster and can seize pistons if loaded hard.

## Making Rounds

Rounds are a planned walk through the machinery spaces at a regular interval (often hourly while underway).

### What to Check on Each Round

| System | Items |
|---|---|
| **Main engine** | Lube oil pressure and temperature, jacket water temperature, exhaust temperatures per cylinder, boost pressure, rpm, load |
| **Reduction gear** | Oil pressure and temperature, sounds |
| **Shafting** | Stern tube and line bearing temperatures, seal or packing leakage |
| **Generators** | Voltage, frequency, load (kW), temperatures |
| **Fuel** | Day tank level, filter differential pressures, separator operation, leaks |
| **Cooling** | Sea strainer differential or condition, expansion tank levels |
| **Bilges** | Levels and contents (oil? water? where from?) |
| **Steering gear** | Hydraulic oil level, leaks, noise |
| **Air** | Starting air receiver pressure, compressor operation |

## The Engine Log

The log is a **legal and technical record**. Record:

- Readings at set intervals
- **Engine starts, stops, and speed changes** (often ordered by the bridge)
- **Fuel and lube oil** soundings and transfers
- **Alarms** and actions taken
- **Maintenance** done during the watch
- Unusual events

Write clearly, in ink (or the electronic system), and never alter old entries. Corrections are lined through and initialed.

---

## Responding to Alarms

1. **Silence** the audible alarm and **acknowledge** it.
2. **Identify** what alarmed and check the actual condition (sensor fault or real problem?).
3. **Act**: reduce load, start a standby pump, switch filters, or stop the engine if needed.
4. **Inform** the chief engineer or the bridge as required.
5. **Log** it.

Some conditions cause **automatic shutdown or slowdown**, such as low lube oil pressure, overspeed, and high jacket water temperature. Never **override** a safety shutdown without authorization and a clear understanding of the risk.

<Pitfall title="Silencing Without Investigating">

**Common mistake:** an alarm goes off repeatedly for a "known" issue, so the watchkeeper silences it each time without looking.

That one time it's real, a bearing runs hot or the bilge floods. Every alarm gets **checked**. If an alarm is truly faulty, **repair it** or formally report it. Don't train yourself to ignore it.

</Pitfall>

---

## Handing Over the Watch

The relieving watchkeeper should not take over until they understand:

- Machinery running and on standby
- Any **abnormal conditions** or ongoing work
- **Orders** from the chief and bridge
- Tank levels and transfers in progress
- Any **lockouts** or isolations in place

---

## Key Takeaway

Do **pre-start checks**, confirm **oil pressure and raw water** immediately after starting, and **warm up** before loading. Make regular **rounds** using all your senses and compare to **normal**. Keep a clear **log**. **Investigate every alarm**, and hand over the watch completely.
