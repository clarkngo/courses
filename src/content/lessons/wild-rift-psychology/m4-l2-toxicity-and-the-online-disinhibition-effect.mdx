## Would You Say That Face to Face?

Picture saying "uninstall, you're worthless" to someone sitting next to you at a café after they missed a skillshot. Most people never would. Yet messages like that show up in online games every day. Why?

---

## The Online Disinhibition Effect

Psychologist John Suler described the **online disinhibition effect**: people say and do things online that they wouldn't in person. He identified several factors. Here's how they show up in Wild Rift:

| Factor | What it means | In game |
|---|---|---|
| **Dissociative anonymity** | "You don't know me" | You're a summoner name, not a person with a face |
| **Invisibility** | Nobody can see you | No eye contact, no seeing the hurt on their face |
| **Asynchronicity** | Not a real-time conversation | You type and move on; you don't watch the reaction land |
| **Solipsistic introjection** | Others feel like voices in your head | Teammates become characters in your internal story |
| **Dissociative imagination** | "It's just a game" | Online feels like a separate world with separate rules |
| **Minimized authority** | No one in charge is watching | No teacher, boss, or referee present in the moment |

Suler also noted **benign disinhibition**: the same effect lets shy people open up, make friends, and be kind online. Disinhibition isn't only bad. It removes brakes, and what happens next depends on the person and the moment.

## Who Is Toxic?

A common assumption is that a small group of "toxic players" causes most problems. Riot's player-behavior team reported in the early 2010s, from *League of Legends* data, that a large share of toxic incidents came from **otherwise ordinary players having an occasional bad moment**, not from a small group of permanently toxic players.

That finding matters:

- Toxicity is often a **state**, not a fixed **trait**. Almost anyone can slip into it when tilted.
- It means **you** are a possible source of toxicity on a bad night, not just a possible target.
- It suggests that prevention (helping people regulate in the moment) matters as much as punishment.

---

## Toxicity Is Contagious

Emotions spread between people, a process called **emotional contagion**. In a match, one flaming player raises everyone's arousal. Others flame back or go quiet and disengage. Coordination drops. The team loses. The loss confirms the flamer's belief that their team is bad.

Research on online-game toxicity, including studies of League of Legends' former Tribunal system, found toxic behavior is linked to **worse team performance**. Whether toxicity causes losses or losses cause toxicity, it's almost certainly both, which makes it a feedback loop.

---

## Deindividuation

**Deindividuation** is a loss of self-awareness in groups or anonymous settings, which weakens personal standards. If you notice yourself typing something you wouldn't want your name attached to, that's deindividuation at work.

One finding from deindividuation research is that **reminders of identity** reduce antisocial behavior. Simply imagining the other player as a real person, maybe a 15-year-old playing on the bus, can restore the brakes.

---

## What Systems Do

Game designers use psychology to reduce toxicity:

- **Honor and positive recognition.** Rewarding good behavior (Wild Rift has an honor system) taps into reinforcement for prosocial play.
- **Chat filters and mute options.** Situation modification at the system level.
- **Reporting and penalties.** Consequences restore some sense of authority.
- **Pings and quick-chat over free text.** Lower-emotion communication channels.

None of these remove toxicity entirely, because the underlying psychology (tilt, anonymity, attribution) is still there.

<Pitfall title="Fighting Fire in Chat">

**Common mistake:** arguing back to "defend yourself" or "teach them a lesson." It almost never changes the flamer's mind, raises your own arousal, splits your attention, and spreads the conflict to the rest of the team. **Mute, report after the game, and play.** That's not weakness. It's protecting your performance and your team's.

</Pitfall>

### If It Gets to You

Repeated harassment can genuinely affect mood. If online abuse follows you outside the game, includes threats, or targets your identity, use the in-game report tools, block the player, and talk to someone you trust. If it's affecting your sleep, school, or work, that's a reason to step back from the game for a while. You'll cover healthy play more in Module 5.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The **online disinhibition effect** comes from anonymity, invisibility, asynchronicity, and minimized authority.
- Disinhibition can be **toxic** or **benign**.
- Much toxicity comes from **ordinary players in a bad moment**, so it's a state anyone can slip into.
- Toxicity is **contagious** and tied to worse team performance.
- **Identity reminders** and prosocial systems (honor, mute, pings) reduce it.
- The best personal response is usually **mute, report, play**.
