## 1,000 Games and Still Gold?

Plenty of players have thousands of games and haven't improved much in years. Others climb steadily with far fewer. The difference usually isn't talent. It's **how** they play.

Psychologist K. Anders Ericsson studied experts in music, sports, and chess and found that what separated top performers wasn't simply time spent, but time spent in **deliberate practice**.

---

## What Deliberate Practice Is

Deliberate practice has a few key features:

1. **A specific goal.** Not "get better," but "land 60% of my Ahri charms in lane."
2. **Just beyond your current ability.** Challenging enough that you fail sometimes.
3. **Full concentration.** Not autopilot.
4. **Immediate, informative feedback.** You know right away whether you succeeded.
5. **Repetition with refinement.** You adjust and try again.

Compare that to how most ranked games are played: no specific goal, autopilot through laning, feedback that's just "win" or "loss," and no review. That's **naive practice**. It builds familiarity, but it plateaus.

### How Much Does Practice Explain?

Ericsson's claims were influential and also debated. A 2014 meta-analysis by Brooke Macnamara, David Hambrick, and Frederick Oswald found deliberate practice explained about **26% of the variation in performance in games** (like chess), less in music and sports. So practice matters a lot, but so do other factors: starting age, working memory, reaction speed, coaching, and luck.

The honest lesson: **you can't control all of those factors, but practice quality is the one you control most.**

## The Feedback Problem in MOBAs

Win/loss is terrible feedback for learning. A game result depends on ten players, so it tells you very little about any single decision you made. That's outcome bias (Module 3) at the level of practice.

Better feedback sources:

- **Specific in-game metrics.** CS per minute at 10 minutes, deaths before 8 minutes, vision score, skillshot hit rate.
- **Replay review.** Watching your own decisions with the full information you didn't have in the moment.
- **Custom games.** Practicing combos, last-hitting, or wave management with no pressure and instant feedback.
- **A coach or a better friend.** Someone who can spot the error you can't see.

## One Focus Per Session

Lesson 3.2 showed that piling on goals overloads attention. Deliberate practice applies the same principle: **one skill at a time**.

A sample four-week plan:

| Week | Focus | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Last-hitting | CS at 10 minutes, tracked every game |
| 2 | Minimap habit | Self-rated 1–5 after each game |
| 3 | Dying less early | Deaths before 8 minutes |
| 4 | Objective timing | Did I move to Dragon or Herald before it spawned? |

Each week, the previous focus should be closer to automatic, freeing attention for the new one.

---

## Spacing and Rest

Learning research shows that **spaced practice** (shorter sessions spread over days) usually beats **massed practice** (one long marathon) for long-term retention. Sleep also plays a role in consolidating motor skills.

For a MOBA player, that means three focused games a day for a week will usually teach you more than twenty games in one Saturday, especially since games 10–20 of a marathon tend to be tired, tilted, and on autopilot.

---

## How to Review a Replay

A focused replay review takes about 10 minutes:

1. **Pick one game** and one focus (for example, your deaths).
2. **Pause before each death.** Ask: what did I know? What could I have known (minimap, timers, enemy items)?
3. **Find the decision point.** It's usually 5–15 seconds before the death, not the moment of death.
4. **Write one lesson.** One sentence: "When their jungler is missing and I have no ward, I don't push past the river."
5. **Turn it into an if-then plan** for next game.

Review wins too. Wins hide mistakes that you got away with.

<Pitfall title="Measuring Progress by Rank Alone">

**Common mistake:** judging a week of practice by whether your rank went up. Rank is a noisy, lagging measure. Your skill can improve for weeks before rank catches up, especially if a new habit temporarily makes you play slower while it becomes automatic. Track the **specific metric** you're practicing.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaways

- **Deliberate practice** means a specific goal, just-beyond-ability difficulty, full focus, quick feedback, and refinement.
- Practice explains a meaningful share of skill (about 26% in game research), and it's the share **you control**.
- Win/loss is weak feedback; use **metrics, replays, custom games, and coaching**.
- Practice **one skill at a time** and spread sessions out.
- Review the **decision point** before each mistake, and turn lessons into **if-then plans**.
