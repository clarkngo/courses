## Connecting Parts to Shafts

Torque has to cross from a shaft into a hub (a sheave, sprocket, gear, or coupling) without slipping. The usual tools are **keys**, **set screws**, and **tapered bushings**.

### Keys

- **Square keys** are most common. As a rough guide, key width is about **one quarter** of the shaft diameter (standard tables set exact sizes).
- The key must fit **snugly on its sides**. A loose key rocks back and forth, hammers the keyway, and eventually shears or "wallows out" the slot.
- Keys are deliberately the **weak link**: better to shear a cheap key than crack a shaft.
- Use **key stock** of the right material. Don't grab any piece of steel bar.

### Set Screws

Set screws stop a hub from sliding along the shaft. A typical arrangement puts one screw **over the key** and a second at about 90°.

- Use **cup-point** screws; they bite into the shaft.
- Torque them to spec.
- Many plants use **thread locker** on set screws because vibration loosens them.

## Tapered Bushings

Many sheaves and sprockets mount with a split tapered bushing, such as **QD** or **Taper-Lock** styles. Tightening the bolts draws the tapered bushing into the hub, squeezing the bushing onto the shaft.

Advantages:

- One hub fits many shaft sizes (just change the bushing)
- Grips the whole shaft circumference, so there's less fretting
- Easy removal using the **jacking holes**

Installation tips:

- Clean the tapers; **don't lubricate** them unless the manufacturer says to, because oil changes the clamping force.
- Tighten bolts **evenly and alternately** to the specified torque.
- Don't close the gap between bushing and hub flange completely on QD-style bushings; a gap is normal.

## Fasteners and Torque

### Bolt Grades

| Marking | Grade | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| No radial lines | SAE Grade 2 | Light duty, non-critical |
| **3 radial lines** | **SAE Grade 5** | General machinery |
| **6 radial lines** | **SAE Grade 8** | High-strength, heavily loaded joints |
| **8.8** on head | Metric class 8.8 | Roughly comparable to Grade 5 |
| **10.9** on head | Metric class 10.9 | Roughly comparable to Grade 8 |

**Replace with the same grade or better.** A hardware-store Grade 2 bolt in a Grade 8 hole is a failure waiting to happen.

### Why Torque Matters

A bolt works like a **stiff spring**. Tightening stretches it slightly, creating **clamp force**. Too little clamp and the joint slips, loosens, and fatigues. Too much and the bolt yields or the threads strip.

- Use a **calibrated torque wrench** and the manufacturer's spec.
- Torque values assume a thread condition (dry, oiled, or with thread locker). **Lubricating threads raises clamp force** at the same wrench reading.
- Tighten bolt patterns in a **star (crisscross) sequence**, in steps.

### Thread Lockers

| Type | Strength | Removal |
|---|---|---|
| **Medium (often blue)** | Removable | Hand tools |
| **High (often red)** | Permanent | Needs heat to remove |
| **Wicking (often green)** | Penetrates pre-assembled fasteners | Varies |

Colors vary by brand, so always read the label.

## Reading a Broken Shaft

Most shaft failures are **fatigue**: a small crack grows a bit each revolution until the remaining metal can't hold.

- **Beach marks** (curved ripple lines) on the fracture face show where the crack grew slowly.
- The **rough, final fracture zone** shows where it finally snapped. A **small** final zone means low stress over a long time; a **large** one means high overload.
- Cracks usually start at **stress raisers**: keyway ends, sharp shoulders, and corrosion pits.

<Pitfall title="Shimming a Worn Keyway">

**Common mistake:** a keyway has worn oversized, so someone stuffs in shim stock or peens the key to make it tight.

It'll loosen again within weeks, and the hammering spreads the damage to the shaft. Repair properly: cut a new keyway at 90° (if design allows), use a **stepped key**, sleeve the hub, or replace the part.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

**Keys** carry torque and are the designed weak link. **Set screws** locate the hub but don't carry torque. **Tapered bushings** clamp evenly and remove easily. Match **bolt grade**, use a **torque wrench**, and tighten in a **star pattern**. Fatigue fractures start at stress raisers like **keyway ends**.

<TryIt title="Inspect a Hub">

Find a sheave or sprocket on a machine (locked out, guard removed by an authorized person). Check:

1. Is it keyed, bushed, or both?
2. Is the key snug, or can you see wear or fretting (reddish-brown rust powder)?
3. Are the set screws present and tight?
4. If it's a bushing, are the bolts tight and is the gap even?
5. What grade are the bolts?

</TryIt>
