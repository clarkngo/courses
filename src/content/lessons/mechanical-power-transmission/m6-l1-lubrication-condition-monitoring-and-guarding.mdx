## From Fixing to Preventing

Maintenance strategies range from reactive to proactive:

| Strategy | Approach | Downside |
|---|---|---|
| **Reactive (run to failure)** | Fix it when it breaks | Unplanned downtime, secondary damage |
| **Preventive (PM)** | Service on a fixed schedule | Can replace good parts or miss early failures |
| **Predictive (PdM)** | Monitor condition, act when it degrades | Needs tools, training, and data |
| **Proactive / precision** | Remove root causes: alignment, balance, clean lubrication | Takes discipline |

Most plants mix all four. Critical equipment gets predictive monitoring; cheap, non-critical items may run to failure.

The **P-F curve** shows that failures usually start long before a machine stops. Point **P** is when the developing failure first becomes **detectable**. Point **F** is **functional failure**. The earlier your detection method, the longer you have to plan a repair. By the time you can **hear** or **feel heat**, you're close to F.

The exact order and timing vary by failure mode, but the lesson holds: **detect early, plan the repair, avoid the breakdown.**

## Condition Monitoring Tools

### Vibration

Vibration analysis is the backbone of rotating-equipment monitoring. Even simple overall readings, trended over time, catch a lot. Some classic signatures (as a starting point; real diagnosis takes training):

| Clue | Often points to |
|---|---|
| Strong vibration at **1× running speed**, mostly radial | Imbalance |
| High **axial** vibration, often at **1× and 2×** running speed | Misalignment |
| Many harmonics of running speed | Mechanical looseness |
| Non-synchronous high-frequency peaks | Rolling bearing defects |
| Peaks at gear mesh frequency (teeth × rpm) | Gear wear or problems |

### Temperature

An **infrared thermometer** or **thermal camera** finds hot bearings, slipping belts, overloaded gearboxes, and misaligned couplings. Compare to the same point over time and to similar machines.

### Ultrasound

Ultrasonic listening detects early bearing friction and **lubrication need**. Some plants grease bearings **while listening**, stopping when the sound drops, which prevents both under- and over-greasing.

### Oil Analysis

Samples tell you about **wear metals** (iron, copper), **contamination** (water, silicon from dirt), and **lubricant condition** (viscosity, oxidation). Take samples from the **same point, the same way, at the same interval** so trends mean something.

## A Basic Lubrication Routine

- **Right lubricant:** match the spec. Label every point and container.
- **Right amount:** calibrate grease guns; use calculated quantities.
- **Right interval:** based on speed, temperature, and environment.
- **Right method:** wipe fittings before greasing; use clean, sealed containers and filtered transfer for oil.
- **Right person and record:** route-based tasks, and log what was done.

Clean lubricant is cheap insurance. Most contamination enters through **dirty fittings, open containers, and bad breathers**.

## Guarding: OSHA 1910.219

In the U.S., **29 CFR 1910.219** covers guarding of mechanical power-transmission apparatus: belts, pulleys, chains, sprockets, gears, shafts, couplings, and set screws. In general:

- Drive components **within 7 feet** of the floor or working platform must be guarded.
- **Projecting set screws** and keys on rotating parts are a classic hazard and must be guarded or eliminated.
- Guards must prevent contact, be secured, and not create new hazards.

Rotating shafts, even smooth ones, can catch loose clothing, hair, or gloves and wrap a person in seconds. **In-running nip points**, where a belt meets a sheave or a chain meets a sprocket, can pull in a hand instantly.

<Pitfall title="Running with the Guard Off to Watch">

**Common mistake:** taking the belt guard off to see whether the belt is slipping, then running the machine.

Observing a drive in motion should be done through **mesh or transparent guard panels**, with a **strobe light**, or with remote tools such as a **laser tachometer or thermal camera**. Guards come off only under **lockout**, and go back on before restart.

</Pitfall>

## Key Takeaway

Mix **preventive**, **predictive**, and **proactive** maintenance. The **P-F curve** says to detect early with **vibration, oil analysis, ultrasound, and temperature** so you can plan repairs. Lubricate with the **right product, amount, interval, and cleanliness**. Keep drives **guarded** (OSHA 1910.219) and never run with guards off to "watch."

<TryIt title="Start a Simple Route">

Pick **five** belt-, chain-, or gear-driven machines. Once a week, record:

1. Bearing and gearbox temperatures with an IR thermometer (mark the exact spot)
2. Any new noise
3. Belt or chain condition through the guard
4. Oil level and leaks

After a month, you'll have baselines. Any reading that jumps compared to its own history deserves a closer look.

</TryIt>
