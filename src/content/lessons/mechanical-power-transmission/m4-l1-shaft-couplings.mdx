## What a Coupling Does

A **coupling** connects two shafts end to end, usually a motor to a pump, fan, or gearbox. Its jobs:

1. **Transmit torque** from one shaft to the other
2. **Accommodate small misalignment** between the shafts (flexible types)
3. **Absorb shock and vibration** (elastomeric types)
4. Sometimes **protect the drive**, using a shear pin or torque limiter that slips or breaks on overload

---

## Rigid vs. Flexible

**Rigid couplings** (sleeve, clamp, flanged) lock two shafts together as if they were one. They tolerate **essentially no misalignment** and are used where shafts are supported to act as one, such as some vertical pumps and line shafts.

**Flexible couplings** allow small amounts of misalignment and end float. They're the norm for motor-to-machine connections.

---

## Common Flexible Couplings

| Type | How it flexes | Strengths | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jaw (spider)** | Elastomer "spider" between interlocking metal jaws | Cheap, simple, fail-safe (jaws still drive if spider fails) | Spider cracking, crumbling, or melting |
| **Tire** | Rubber or urethane tire bolted between flanges | High misalignment tolerance, absorbs shock | Tire cracks, ozone and oil attack |
| **Sleeve (elastomeric)** | Toothed rubber sleeve engages two flanges | Simple, quiet | Sleeve wear, sensitive to heat and oil |
| **Gear** | Crowned external teeth mesh with internal sleeve teeth | Very high torque in compact size | **Needs grease**; worn teeth from poor lubrication |
| **Grid** | Tapered steel grid spring in grooved hubs | High torque with shock absorption | **Needs grease**; grid fatigue or breakage |
| **Disc (diaphragm)** | Thin metal discs flex | No lubrication, no backlash, high speed | Disc cracking from excessive misalignment |
| **Universal joint** | Cross and yokes pivot | Large angles (e.g., drive shafts) | Speed fluctuation unless phased correctly; needle bearings need grease |

### Lubricated vs. Non-Lubricated

**Gear** and **grid** couplings have metal-on-metal contact and need regular **grease** (often coupling-specific grease, because ordinary grease can separate under centrifugal force). Elastomer and disc couplings need **no lubrication** but have elements that wear out.

## Installing a Coupling

1. **Lock out** both the driver and driven machine.
2. **Clean** shafts and hub bores. Check keys and keyways (Lesson 1.2).
3. Mount hubs. For **interference fits**, heat the hub evenly (never a torch on one spot).
4. Set the **gap (distance between shaft ends)** per the coupling maker's spec. This allows for thermal growth and end float.
5. **Align the shafts** (Module 5). The coupling's tolerance is **not** your alignment target.
6. Install the flexible element, grid, or discs; torque bolts to spec.
7. For lubricated types, fill with the right grease and install seals and gaskets.
8. Reinstall the **coupling guard** before restart.

<Pitfall title="The Coupling Can Handle It">

**Common mistake:** "It's a flexible coupling, it can take some misalignment," so alignment is eyeballed.

The coupling may survive, but the **bearings and seals** on both machines won't. A coupling's published misalignment capacity is the most it can take **without destroying itself**, not what the machines can tolerate. Align to proper machinery tolerances (Module 5), which are much tighter.

</Pitfall>

---

## Inspecting Couplings

Lock out and remove the guard, then check:

- **Elastomer elements:** cracks, crumbling, swelling, melting, or black dust (a sign of heat and wear)
- **Gear couplings:** grease condition, tooth wear patterns, leaking seals
- **Grid couplings:** broken grid segments, grease present
- **Disc couplings:** cracked or deformed discs, loose bolts
- **Hubs:** loose set screws, key wear, hubs moved along the shaft
- **Wear patterns** that are uneven around the circumference (a sign of misalignment)

Rubber dust under a jaw coupling, or a coupling that's hot to the touch after shutdown, are classic misalignment clues.

---

## Key Takeaway

Couplings **transmit torque** and **tolerate small misalignment**. Pick the type for the torque, speed, shock, and maintenance you can support. **Gear and grid** couplings need grease; **elastomer and disc** types don't. A coupling's misalignment rating is **not** an alignment spec, so always align properly.
