## Why Bearings Die Young

Bearing makers report that only a small fraction of bearings reach their calculated fatigue life. The rest fail early, and the usual causes are within a mechanic's control:

- **Poor lubrication** (too little, too much, wrong type, or contaminated)
- **Contamination** (dirt and water)
- **Improper mounting** (hammering, wrong fits, misalignment)
- **Overload** (over-tensioned belts, misalignment, imbalance)

---

## Installing a Bearing Correctly

### The Golden Rule: Never Push Through the Rolling Elements

When pressing a bearing onto a shaft, apply force **only to the ring being fitted**, which is usually the **inner ring**. Pushing on the outer ring forces the load through the balls or rollers and dents the raceways (called **brinelling**) before the bearing ever turns.

### Heating

For medium and large bearings with an interference fit, heat the bearing so it expands and slides on.

- Use an **induction heater** with automatic demagnetization, or an oven.
- Heating about **80–90 °C above shaft temperature** is usually enough. Standard bearings should **never exceed about 120 °C (250 °F)**, or the steel, seals, and grease can be damaged. Follow the maker's limit.
- **Never use a torch.** Uneven heat ruins the steel's hardness.
- Wear heat-resistant gloves, and push it home quickly before it cools and grabs.

### Pressing and Driving

For small bearings, use a **fitting tool kit** (impact sleeve and ring) or an arbor press so force goes evenly into the correct ring. **Never strike a bearing directly with a hammer.**

### Fits

The ring that rotates relative to the load usually gets an **interference (tight) fit**; the other ring gets a looser fit. For most shafts turning under a stationary load, that means the inner ring is tight on the shaft. Worn shafts or housings that let a ring **spin** cause fretting and rapid failure.

## Greasing Without Killing the Bearing

### How Much?

More grease is **not** better. A bearing packed full churns the grease, overheats, and can blow out seals. For many open bearings, a common guideline is to fill the bearing itself and leave the housing roughly **one-third to one-half** full. Relubrication quantity and interval should come from the maker's formula or a lubrication program.

### Compatibility

Greases use different **thickeners** (lithium complex, polyurea, calcium sulfonate, and others). Some don't mix well. Mixing incompatible greases can make the mixture soften and run out. Label grease points and grease guns, and use one product per point.

### Electric Motors

Many motor bearings are overgreased. Good practice: wipe the fitting, **remove the drain plug**, add grease slowly with the motor running if it's safe and the maker allows, and let excess purge before reinstalling the plug.

<Pitfall title="Pumping Until It Comes Out">

**Common mistake:** greasing a sealed housing "until grease comes out somewhere."

With no relief path, grease forces past the seals or collapses them, and the bearing overheats from churning. Know the correct **shot count** for each grease gun (weigh a few shots to calibrate) and the right volume for each bearing.

</Pitfall>

## Reading Failed Bearings

A failed bearing is evidence. Look before you throw it away.

| Failure mode | What it looks like | Common cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Fatigue spalling** | Flaking pits in the raceway | End of normal life, or overload |
| **True brinelling** | Evenly spaced dents matching the rolling elements | Impact or improper mounting force |
| **False brinelling** | Shiny or rusty marks at element spacing, without true dents | Vibration while **stationary** (shipping, standby equipment) |
| **Fluting** | Washboard-like stripes across the raceway | **Electric current** passing through, often from VFD-driven motors |
| **Contamination** | Dents, scratches, dull surfaces | Dirt in grease, failed seals, dirty handling |
| **Corrosion** | Rust staining, etched areas | Water, condensation, washdown |
| **Discoloration (blue/brown)** | Heat tint on rings or elements | Lubrication failure, overload, too-tight fit |
| **Off-center wear path** | Load zone near one edge of the raceway | Misalignment or thrust overload |
| **Spun ring / fretting** | Polished or rusty bore or OD | Loose fit, worn shaft or housing |

### VFDs and Fluting

Variable frequency drives can induce **shaft voltages** that discharge through motor bearings, causing **electrical erosion** and fluting. Solutions include **shaft grounding rings**, **insulated bearings** (ceramic-coated or hybrid ceramic-ball), and proper drive grounding.

---

## Key Takeaway

Mount bearings by **pushing only the ring being fitted**, using **controlled heat** (never a torch) or proper tools (never a bare hammer). Grease the right **amount** with a **compatible** product. Read failures: **spalling** is fatigue, **brinelling** is impact, **fluting** is electrical current, and an **off-center path** is misalignment.
