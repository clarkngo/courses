## A Tutor Who's Always Available

A good tutor doesn't do your homework. They explain things in a way that clicks for you, ask questions that make you think, notice what you're missing, and quiz you until you know it. AI can do a lot of this, if you **describe** what you want.

Here are four study techniques that learning research strongly supports, and how to do each with AI.

---

## 1. Explain It at My Level

When the textbook doesn't click, ask for a different explanation, and tell the AI where you are:

```text
I'm in 10th-grade chemistry. I understand what atoms are but I'm
confused about why some bonds are ionic and others covalent. Explain
it using an everyday analogy, then check whether I understood by
asking me one question.
```

The last line matters. It turns a passive explanation into an active check.

## 2. Quiz Me (Retrieval Practice)

Pulling information *out* of your memory strengthens it far more than rereading. This is called **retrieval practice**, and it's one of the most effective study methods there is.

```text
Here are my notes on the causes of World War I: [paste notes].
Quiz me one question at a time. Mix easy and hard questions. Don't
show the answer until I've tried. Keep track of what I get wrong
and quiz me on those again at the end.
```

## 3. Let Me Teach It Back

If you can explain something simply, you understand it. Ask AI to be your student:

```text
I'm going to explain photosynthesis to you as if you're a curious
12-year-old. Ask follow-up questions like a real kid would. Then
tell me what I explained well and what I got wrong or left out.
```

## 4. Spaced Review

Studying a little over several days beats cramming, because spacing out review forces you to keep retrieving things you've half-forgotten. AI can help plan it:

```text
My biology test is in 8 days. The topics are [list]. I have about
30 minutes a day. Make me a spaced review plan that revisits each
topic at least three times, with more time on [hardest topics].
```

## Discernment: Your Tutor Makes Mistakes

AI tutors can be wrong: a wrong step in a math solution, a mixed-up date, a definition that's close but not quite right. Protect yourself:

- **Check against your class materials.** Your textbook and notes are the source of truth for your course.
- **Watch for differences from how your teacher does it.** AI might use a different method or notation. That can confuse you on a test.
- **Be suspicious when AI is very confident and very specific.** Double-check names, dates, numbers, and formulas.
- **Ask your teacher when AI and your notes disagree.** It's a great question to bring to class.

<Pitfall title="Studying the AI Instead of the Material">

**Common mistake:** asking AI for a summary of every chapter and studying only the summaries.

Summaries skip details your teacher might test, may contain errors, and leave you with an illusion of competence. Use AI to *quiz* you on the real material, not to replace it.

</Pitfall>

<TryIt title="Run a Full Study Session With AI">

1. Pick a test or quiz you have coming up.
2. **Explain:** Ask for a different explanation of the topic you find hardest, at your level, ending with a check question.
3. **Quiz:** Paste your own notes and run a retrieval quiz of at least 10 questions. Track what you miss.
4. **Teach back:** Explain one concept to the AI as if it's a younger student, and ask for feedback on what you missed.
5. **Plan:** Ask for a spaced review plan up to your test date.
6. **Check:** Find one thing the AI said that you verified against your class materials. Was it right?

</TryIt>
