## Why Your Voice Matters

Writing assignments aren't really about producing text. They're about learning to **think clearly, build an argument, and say what you mean**. Those are some of the most valuable skills you'll ever have. AI can write fluent text in seconds, which is exactly why handing it the writing skips the skill.

AI-written text also tends to sound a certain way: smooth, balanced, a little generic, with no real point of view. Teachers who know your writing often notice when a submission doesn't sound like you.

### Brainstorming

```text
I'm writing an argumentative essay for 11th-grade English about
whether cities should ban cars from downtown. Ask me questions to
help me figure out what I actually believe. Then suggest three
angles I might not have considered. Don't write any of the essay.
```

### Outline Feedback

```text
Here's my outline: [paste]. Don't rewrite it. Tell me: is my thesis
clear and arguable? Is there a point that doesn't support it? What
counterargument should I address?
```

### Draft Feedback

```text
Here's my draft paragraph. Act as a tough but fair writing tutor.
Point out the two biggest problems, but don't fix them. I'll revise
it myself.
```

The key words are **"don't rewrite"** and **"don't fix."** They're a performance description that keeps AI in the role of coach.

## Keeping It Yours

- **Your ideas first.** Decide what you think before asking AI for angles, so you're choosing among options rather than adopting one.
- **Your words.** Don't paste AI sentences into your draft. If AI phrases something well, close the chat and write the idea in your own way.
- **Your decisions.** Feedback is advice. You decide what to change. Sometimes the AI is wrong, or would make your writing sound less like you.
- **Your evidence.** Every quote, fact, and source in your essay should come from something you actually read (see Lesson 2.2).

<Pitfall title="Death by a Thousand Edits">

**Common mistake:** asking AI to "improve" your draft over and over, accepting each version, until the final essay is mostly AI's writing.

Each change seems small, but together they replace your voice and thinking. If AI is rewriting rather than advising, you've crossed from feedback into authorship, which most course policies don't allow without disclosure.

</Pitfall>

<TryIt title="Coach, Don't Write">

*Use a real writing assignment that allows AI feedback, or a practice piece.*

1. Write one paragraph yourself, without AI.
2. Ask AI for feedback using the "point out, don't fix" prompt above.
3. Revise the paragraph yourself, using only the feedback you agree with.
4. Now, as an experiment, ask AI to rewrite your original paragraph. Read it, but don't use it.
5. Compare your revision and AI's rewrite. Which sounds more like you? Which says what *you* mean? Which would you rather defend in front of your teacher?

</TryIt>
