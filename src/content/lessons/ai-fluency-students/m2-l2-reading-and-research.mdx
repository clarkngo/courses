## Reading: AI as a Guide, Not a Replacement

Hard readings are where AI can help a lot, and where the shortcut is most tempting. "Summarize this article" saves twenty minutes, and costs you the understanding the reading was assigned to build. Instead, use AI **around** your reading:

| Before you read | While you read | After you read |
|---|---|---|
| "What background do I need to understand an article about [topic]?" | "What does this paragraph mean? [paste it]" | "Here's my summary. What did I miss or get wrong?" |
| "Define these five terms I'll see." | "Explain this term in simpler words." | "Ask me three questions to test whether I understood the main argument." |

The pattern: **you read, you summarize, then AI checks you.** Not the other way around.

## Research: AI Is Not a Source

When you research a topic, AI can help you get started:

- Brainstorm angles and research questions
- Suggest search terms and keywords
- Explain background so you understand what you find
- Help you organize notes and compare what sources say

But **AI chat responses are not sources you can cite**, and AI can **invent sources** that look completely real: plausible titles, real-sounding authors, believable journal names, and links that go nowhere. This is called **hallucination**, and citations are one of the places it happens most.

### If AI Gives You a Source

1. **Search for it** by exact title in a search engine or your library database.
2. **Open it.** Confirm the author, date, and publication are real.
3. **Find the claim.** Make sure the source actually says what the AI said it does. Real sources are often misquoted.
4. **Cite only what you've read yourself.**

Some AI tools search the web and show real links. That helps, but you still need to open the link and check the claim.

<Pitfall title="The Fake Citation">

**Common mistake:** copying an AI-generated reference list into your bibliography.

If even one source doesn't exist, your teacher will notice, and fabricated citations are usually treated as a serious integrity problem, even if the AI made them up and you didn't know. You are responsible for every source you cite.

</Pitfall>

<TryIt title="Source Check Challenge">

1. Ask an AI assistant:
   ```text
   Give me five scholarly sources about [a topic you're studying],
   with authors, titles, years, and where they were published.
   ```
2. For each source, try to find it using a search engine or your school library's database.
3. Mark each: ✅ exists and matches, ⚠️ exists but details are wrong, ❌ can't find it.
4. For any that exist, check whether the source is actually about what the AI said.
5. Now find two real sources yourself using your library database. Compare the time and effort, and the confidence you have in each.

</TryIt>
