## Every Class Can Have Different Rules

You might have one teacher who bans AI completely, another who encourages it, and a third whose syllabus just says "use AI responsibly." That's confusing, but it usually isn't random. Teachers set AI rules based on **what each assignment is meant to build**.

Many courses now label assignments with levels like these:

| Level | What it usually means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **No AI** | The skill is being practiced or tested on its own. | In-class essay, foundational quiz |
| **AI for preparation** | Use AI to study, brainstorm, or get explanations. The work you submit is yours. | Book report |
| **AI as collaborator** | AI can help draft or give feedback. Disclose how you used it. | Research proposal |
| **AI integrated** | Using AI well is part of the assignment. | Analyzing AI output, AI-assisted projects |

If your course doesn't use levels, look for the same information in the syllabus, the assignment instructions, or the course website.

## When the Rules Aren't Clear, Ask

If you can't tell whether something is allowed, **ask before you do it**. A quick message works:

```text
Hi [Teacher], for the [assignment], is it okay to use AI to
[specific use, e.g. "quiz myself on the reading" or "get feedback
on my outline"]? I want to make sure I'm following the course
policy. Thanks!
```

Asking shows you take integrity seriously. Guessing wrong, even honestly, can lead to an awkward conversation or a penalty.

Some rules of thumb when you can't get an answer in time:

- **The rule is the strictest one that might apply.** If the course says no AI and the assignment says nothing, assume no AI.
- **"Allowed in my other class" doesn't count.** Rules don't carry over between courses.
- **When in doubt, use AI only for preparation** (studying, explanations) and do all the submitted work yourself.

<Pitfall title="Assuming Silence Means Yes">

**Common mistake:** "The assignment didn't say I couldn't, so I used AI to write it."

Most schools treat submitting work you didn't produce as an integrity violation unless it's explicitly allowed. Silence isn't permission. Ask.

</Pitfall>

## Protect Your Privacy, and Other People's

AI tools may store what you type, and some use it to improve their models. Before pasting anything, think about who it belongs to:

- **Your own sensitive information:** full name with address, student ID, passwords, health information, financial details. Leave these out.
- **Other people's information:** classmates' names, their work, group chat messages, or personal stories they told you. That's theirs to share, not yours.
- **Your school's tools:** many schools provide an approved AI tool with better privacy protection. Find out whether yours does, and use it when you can.
- **Age limits:** many AI tools require users to be at least 13 or 18, or to have parental permission. Check the terms of the tools you use.
