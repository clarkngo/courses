## Ten Fluent People Aren't a Fluent Team

Picture a team where everyone uses AI, and uses it well, each in their own way. One person drafts client emails with a personal chatbot. Another summarizes meeting recordings with a browser extension. A third built a clever spreadsheet automation nobody else knows about.

Individually, they're productive. As a team, there are problems:

- **Nobody knows what data has gone where.** Client details may be sitting in personal accounts with unknown data terms.
- **Quality is inconsistent.** One person checks AI output carefully; another doesn't check at all. Customers can't tell the difference until something goes wrong.
- **Good practices don't spread.** The best prompt or workflow stays in one person's head.
- **Accountability is fuzzy.** When AI-assisted work has an error, nobody is sure what the review process was supposed to be.

This is often called **shadow AI**: AI use that happens outside any shared visibility or rules. It usually isn't anyone being reckless. It's what happens when people find useful tools and nobody has set shared expectations.

## The 4Ds at Team Level

The 4D AI Fluency Framework applies to teams as well as individuals, but the questions change:

| Competency | Individual question | Team question |
|---|---|---|
| **Delegation** | Should I use AI for this task? | Where does AI fit in our workflows, and where do we require human judgment? |
| **Description** | How do I explain what I need? | What shared context, instructions, and standards do we give AI? |
| **Discernment** | Is this output good? | Who reviews what, to what standard, and how do we learn from mistakes? |
| **Diligence** | Am I using AI responsibly? | What are our rules for tools, data, disclosure, and accountability? |

## The Leader's Role

You don't need to be the team's best AI user. Your job is different:

1. **Make it safe to talk about.** People hide AI use when they fear judgment or think it's against the rules. You can't improve what you can't see.
2. **Set direction.** Decide, with the team, where AI belongs and where it doesn't.
3. **Create shared resources.** Approved tools, shared instructions, review standards.
4. **Model it.** Use AI openly and well, including showing when you checked it, corrected it, or decided not to use it.
5. **Own the outcomes.** AI-assisted work from your team is your team's work.

<Pitfall title="Leading by Ban or by Hype">

**Common mistake:** either banning AI outright ("too risky") or pushing everyone to "use AI for everything" to hit a productivity target.

A ban drives use into the shadows, where risk is highest. Hype pushes AI into tasks where it degrades quality or bypasses judgment. Both skip the real work: deciding, together, where AI genuinely helps.

</Pitfall>

<TryIt title="Run an AI Use Snapshot">

1. Send your team a short, **anonymous** survey (or hold an open conversation, if trust is high). Ask:
   - Which AI tools do you use for work, if any?
   - What do you use them for?
   - What kind of information do you put into them?
   - Where has AI helped most? Where has it caused problems?
   - What would make you more confident using AI at work?
2. Make clear that the goal is to learn, not to punish.
3. Summarize the results: tools, uses, data types, wins, and concerns.
4. Mark where your team sits on the path above: Scattered, Visible, Shared, or Fluent.

</TryIt>
