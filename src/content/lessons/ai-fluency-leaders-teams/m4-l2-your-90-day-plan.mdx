## Three Phases

You don't need to do everything at once. A 90-day plan in three phases moves a team from scattered to shared practice without overwhelming anyone.

### Days 1 to 30: See

- Run the **AI use snapshot** (Lesson 1.1) and share the summary with the team.
- Gather existing **organizational policies, legal requirements, and contracts** (Lesson 3.1).
- **Map two or three key workflows** with AI fit, checkpoints, and skill-building steps (Lesson 1.2).
- Identify **AI champions**.
- Address urgent risks right away. If confidential data is going into unapproved tools, fix that now rather than waiting for the policy.

### Days 31 to 60: Share

- Draft, review, and roll out the **one-page team AI policy** (Lesson 3.1).
- Build the first **shared instructions and definitions of done** (Lesson 2.1).
- Set **review tiers** for your team's AI-assisted work (Lesson 2.2).
- Run a **fairness check** on any AI use that touches decisions about people (Lesson 3.2).
- Start **one pilot**, with baseline measures (Lesson 4.1).

### Days 61 to 90: Scale

- **Review the pilot** against its measures, and decide: scale, adjust, or stop.
- Run **team training** using real workflows and the four competencies.
- Hold a **blameless review** of any AI incidents or near-misses.
- Set a **standing rhythm**: quarterly review of policy and shared instructions, and a regular agenda slot for AI wins and problems.
- Choose the **next workflow** to pilot.

## Keep It Going

After 90 days, AI fluency becomes part of how the team operates:

- **Regular check-ins:** a few minutes in team meetings for what's working, what isn't, and what changed.
- **Living resources:** shared instructions and the policy updated on schedule, with named owners.
- **Skill development:** new team members trained on the team's practices, and junior staff given real opportunities to build the expertise that review depends on.
- **Your own modeling:** keep using AI visibly and well, including showing what you checked and when you chose not to use it.

<Pitfall title="The Plan That Stays a Document">

**Common mistake:** writing a thorough 90-day plan, sharing it once, and then letting day-to-day work take over.

Put the plan's milestones in your calendar and your team's meeting agenda. Give each item an owner and a date. A short plan that actually happens beats a perfect one that doesn't.

</Pitfall>

<TryIt title="Write Your 90-Day Plan">

1. Using the three phases, write your team's plan. For each item, add an **owner** and a **target date**.
2. Adjust it to your situation: skip what you've already done, and add anything specific to your team or industry.
3. Identify the **single biggest risk** to the plan (time, buy-in, unclear rules, tools) and one way to reduce it.
4. Ask an AI assistant (approved tool, no confidential details) to critique it:
   ```text
   Here's my 90-day plan to build AI fluency on a team of [size] in
   [field]. As an experienced change-management coach, point out
   what's unrealistic for the timeframe, what's missing, and which
   item I should do first if I can only do one.
   ```
5. Revise, then share the plan with your team and ask for their input.

</TryIt>
