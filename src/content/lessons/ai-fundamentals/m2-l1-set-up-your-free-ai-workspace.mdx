## You Don't Need to Pay to Learn This

Every exercise in this course works in the **free tier** of a mainstream AI assistant. Paid plans usually add higher usage limits, faster or more capable models, and extra features — nice to have, not required. The skills you'll learn (framing a request, iterating, verifying) transfer across every tool, because they're about *you*, not the product.

Free-tier features and limits change often, so check the provider's current plan page if something in this course isn't available to you. If your employer provides an approved AI tool, **use that one for anything work-related** — it's likely covered by agreements that protect company data.

**Recommendation:** pick one and stick with it for the whole course. Switching tools every lesson makes it harder to tell whether a better result came from your prompt or from the tool. Once you're comfortable, try the same prompt in a second assistant — comparing them is a great lesson on its own.

## Three Settings to Check Before You Start

Take five minutes to look through your assistant's settings. Names and locations vary by product and change over time, but look for:

1. **Training on your data.** Many free consumer tools may use your conversations to improve their models unless you opt out. Look for a setting like "Improve the model for everyone," "Activity," or "Help improve." Decide deliberately rather than by default.
2. **Chat history and memory.** Some assistants remember facts about you across conversations. Useful, but know it's on — and know how to view and delete what it remembers.
3. **Temporary or private chats.** Most assistants offer a mode that doesn't save the conversation. Handy for anything you'd rather not keep.

<Pitfall title="Pasting Sensitive Information Into a Consumer Tool">

**Common mistake:** pasting customer lists, employee records, health information, passwords, unreleased financials, or confidential contracts into a personal, free AI account to "just summarize it quickly."

Once it's submitted, you've shared it with a third party — possibly one your organization has no agreement with. The rule of thumb for this whole course: **if you wouldn't paste it into an email to a stranger, don't paste it into a consumer AI tool.** Remove names and identifying details, or use your organization's approved tool.

</Pitfall>

## How the "Try It" Exercises Work

Each Try It box gives you a prompt or a short scenario to run in your assistant. A few conventions:

- **Text in brackets**, like `[your job title]`, is a placeholder. Replace it with your own details.
- **Your output will differ from anyone else's** — and from your own output if you run the same prompt twice. AI responses vary by design. That's normal.
- **Start a new chat** for each exercise unless it says to continue, so earlier conversations don't influence the result.
- **There's no answer key.** Success means you got a response that's genuinely useful to you, and you can explain *why* it's better than your first attempt.

<TryIt title="Get to Know Your Assistant">

Start a new chat and send:

```text
I'm new to using AI assistants and I work as a [your job title]
in [your industry]. Suggest 5 specific, low-risk ways I could use
you this week to save time. For each, give an example prompt I
could copy.
```

Then follow up with:

```text
Which of those 5 would be the riskiest if I didn't double-check
your output, and why?
```

Save one of the example prompts somewhere — a note, a doc — as the first entry in your personal **prompt library**. You'll add to it throughout the course.

</TryIt>
