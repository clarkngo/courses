## Why One Giant Prompt Falls Short

Ask an AI to "research, outline, write, and polish a 10-slide presentation on our customer retention problem" in one prompt, and you'll get something — but it will be shallow everywhere. The AI has to make dozens of decisions at once, and you have no chance to steer any of them.

**Prompt chaining** is the fix: split a big task into a series of smaller prompts, where **the output of one step becomes the input to the next**, and you review each link before moving on.

```text
Step 1: Understand   →   Step 2: Outline   →   Step 3: Draft   →   Step 4: Refine
   (you review)            (you review)          (you review)        (you review)
```

It's how a skilled professional works anyway: nobody writes a report by typing the final version from top to bottom. Chaining just makes those stages explicit.

---

## Three Chaining Patterns

### Pattern 1: Sequential Build

Each step builds directly on the last. Great for documents, presentations, and plans.

1. "Here are my notes on our customer retention data: [notes]. What are the 3–5 main themes?"
2. "Using those themes, propose an outline for a 10-slide presentation to our leadership team. One line per slide."
3. *(After you adjust the outline)* "Write speaker notes for slides 1–3 using the revised outline."
4. "Review all the speaker notes for consistency and cut any repetition."

### Pattern 2: Generate, Then Filter

Produce many options, then evaluate them in a separate step. You used a version of this in the Module 2 brainstorm.

1. "Generate 15 possible names for our internal mentoring program."
2. "Rate each name from 1–5 on: easy to remember, inclusive, not already a common brand. Show a table."
3. "Take the top 3 and write a one-sentence tagline for each."

### Pattern 3: Draft, Critique, Revise

Use separate steps for creating and judging, because AI (like people) evaluates better when it isn't also trying to create.

1. "Draft a response to this customer complaint: [complaint]."
2. "Now act as the customer who wrote the complaint. How would you feel reading that response? What's missing?"
3. "Revise the response to address those gaps."

## Tips for Better Chains

- **Plan the chain first.** You can even ask the AI: "I need to produce X. Break this into 4–6 steps I should do one at a time. Don't start the work yet."
- **Review at every link.** A mistake in step 1 carries through every later step. Catching it early is the whole point.
- **Edit the output before passing it on.** You don't have to accept an outline as-is — change it, then say "Use this revised version."
- **Keep one chain in one chat** so the AI can see the earlier steps. For very long chains, see the next lesson on carrying context.

<TryIt title="Chain Your Way to a Finished Deliverable">

Choose a deliverable you actually need, or use this scenario: *"Create a one-page onboarding guide for new volunteers at a community food bank."*

1. **Plan:** `I need to create [deliverable] for [audience]. Break this into 4-5 steps I should complete one at a time. Don't do the work yet.`
2. **Step through it:** complete each step in the same chat. After each response, review it and make at least one change or correction before saying "Next step."
3. **Critique:** at the end, ask the AI to review the whole thing from the audience's point of view and list what's confusing or missing.
4. **Revise and verify:** apply the fixes you agree with, then check every fact yourself.

Reflect: at which step did your review make the biggest difference?

</TryIt>

<Pitfall title="Rubber-Stamping Each Step">

**Common mistake:** running a chain but typing "looks good, next" at every step without actually reading.

That turns a chain back into one giant prompt with extra clicks. The value of chaining comes from the checkpoints. If you're not going to review a step, you've lost your chance to steer it — and any error compounds through everything that follows.

</Pitfall>
