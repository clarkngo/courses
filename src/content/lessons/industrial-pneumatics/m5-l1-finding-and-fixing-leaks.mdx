## The Silent Money Drain

Leaks are usually the **single largest waste** in a compressed air system. U.S. Department of Energy guidance notes that a typical plant that hasn't been well maintained can lose **20–30% of its compressor output** to leaks. A well-managed system can keep leakage closer to **10% or less**.

Leaks also cause:

- **Pressure drop** at machines, so someone raises the header pressure (more waste)
- **Extra compressor run time** and earlier overhauls
- **Added compressors** the plant didn't really need

---

## How Much Does a Leak Cost?

Leak flow depends on hole size and pressure. Approximate flows at **100 psig** through a well-rounded orifice (sharp-edged holes leak somewhat less):

| Hole diameter | Approx. leak flow |
|---|---|
| 1/32 in | ~1.6 scfm |
| 1/16 in | ~6.5 scfm |
| 1/8 in | ~26 scfm |
| 1/4 in | ~104 scfm |

Notice the pattern: **double the hole diameter, and flow goes up about four times**, because flow scales with area.

### Estimating Annual Cost

A rough way to estimate:

**Annual cost ≈ leak scfm × kW per scfm × hours per year × $ per kWh**

Many rotary screw systems use roughly **0.2 kW per scfm** delivered (check your own system's specific power).

A single **1/8-inch** leak running all year at **$0.10/kWh**:

26 scfm × 0.2 kW × 8,760 h × $0.10 ≈ **$4,500 per year**

One leak the size of a pencil lead, costing as much as a used car over a few years.

## Measuring Total Leakage: The Load/Unload Test

You can measure plant-wide leakage when production is **off** (all air-using equipment idle, but air still supplied):

1. Run the compressors with production stopped.
2. Time several cycles of **loaded time (T)** and **unloaded time (t)**.
3. Calculate: **Leakage % = T ÷ (T + t) × 100**

If the compressor runs loaded 40 seconds and unloaded 60 seconds, leakage ≈ 40 ÷ 100 = **40% of that compressor's capacity**, all going to leaks.

For VSD or multiple-compressor systems, a **bleed-down test** on a known receiver volume or a flow meter gives a similar answer.

## Where Leaks Hide

The most common leak points are at the **demand side**, at the machines:

- **Quick-disconnect couplings** and worn plugs
- **Push-to-connect fittings** with cut or poorly inserted tubing
- **FRLs**: bowl seals, drains stuck open, regulator vents
- **Hoses** cracked from age, heat, or abrasion
- **Valve and cylinder seals**, especially exhaust ports that hiss when the valve is idle
- **Thread sealant** that failed on pipe joints
- **Condensate drains** that are timed or stuck open
- **Open blow-offs** and abandoned equipment still connected to air

## Finding Leaks

| Method | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Listening** | Only large leaks, and only in quiet areas |
| **Soapy water** | Pinpointing a suspected joint |
| **Ultrasonic leak detector** | Fast surveys in a running, noisy plant; the turbulent flow emits high-frequency sound that the detector converts to audible tones |
| **Acoustic imaging camera** | Large surveys; shows leaks overlaid on a picture |

## A Leak Program That Sticks

Finding leaks is the easy part. The hard part is fixing them **and keeping them fixed**.

<Pitfall title="Tagging Without Repairing">

**Common mistake:** a contractor tags 300 leaks, hands over a report, and the tags fade on the machines for a year.

The savings only come from **repairs**. Assign repairs as work orders with parts pre-staged, fix many leaks during scheduled downtime, and report the results (scfm and dollars saved) so the program keeps its funding.

</Pitfall>

## Other Easy Efficiency Wins

- **Lower the header pressure** to the lowest the plant actually needs
- **Shut off air** to idle machines and lines with zone valves
- **Replace open blow-offs** with engineered air nozzles or blowers
- **Replace timed drains** with zero-loss drains
- **Stop inappropriate uses**, such as cooling people, sweeping floors, or open-tube sparging

---

## Key Takeaway

Leaks commonly waste **20–30%** of output. Doubling a hole's diameter roughly **quadruples** the leak. Measure leakage with a **load/unload** test, find leaks with **ultrasound**, and drive a **tag → repair → verify** loop. Repairs, not tags, save money.

<TryIt title="Price Your Leaks">

A survey finds **12 leaks** totaling an estimated **48 scfm**. The plant runs 6,000 hours per year, the compressor uses **0.21 kW per scfm**, and power costs **$0.12/kWh**.

Annual cost ≈ 48 × 0.21 × 6,000 × 0.12 ≈ **$7,260 per year**.

If the parts and labor to fix them cost $1,200, the payback is about **2 months**.

</TryIt>
