## The Teaching Work Inventory

Teaching involves a lot of different kinds of work. Some of it is mechanical, some depends on expertise, and some only works because a student knows *you* did it. Delegation means sorting those.

| Task | Typical call | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Reformatting a worksheet, generating answer keys you check | **Automate** | Well defined and quick to check. |
| Drafting practice questions at several difficulty levels | **Augment** | AI drafts quickly; you check accuracy and alignment. |
| Adapting a reading for different reading levels | **Augment** | Useful, but meaning can drift. Check against the original. |
| Brainstorming hooks, examples, or analogies for a hard concept | **Augment** | A thinking partner, with your judgment choosing. |
| Writing a newsletter or routine family email | **Augment** | Draft with AI, then edit into your own voice. |
| Deciding a student's grade | **Keep** | Your professional judgment and accountability. |
| Feedback on a student's personal writing or struggles | **Keep**, or augment with care | The relationship is part of the feedback. See Module 2. |
| Recommendation letters | **Keep** | The value depends on it being your genuine assessment. |
| Conversations about behavior, wellbeing, or family situations | **Keep** | Relationship and confidentiality come first. |

The pattern: **AI is strongest on materials and weakest on relationships and judgment.** The more a task depends on knowing a particular student, the more it should stay with you.

## Student Privacy Comes First

Before any delegation decision, there's a gate: **what student information would this task put into an AI tool?**

- **Know your rules.** Many places have student privacy laws (for example, FERPA in the United States) plus district or institutional policies about which tools are approved. Personal AI accounts are usually *not* approved for student records.
- **Default to de-identified.** Remove names, ID numbers, and details that could identify a student. "A ninth grader who struggles with fractions and loves basketball" is usually all the AI needs.
- **Be extra careful with sensitive categories:** disability and accommodation information, health, discipline records, family circumstances, and grades.
- **Student work counts too.** Pasting a student's essay into an unapproved tool shares their work, and possibly personal details in it, with a third party.

<Pitfall title="The Helpful Shortcut That Leaks Data">

**Common mistake:** pasting a class roster with accommodation notes into a personal AI account to "quickly make seating charts and grouping suggestions."

It saves ten minutes and may violate privacy law and school policy. Use an approved tool, or describe the needs without names ("three students need front-row seating, two need to be separated").

</Pitfall>

## The Delegation Check, for Teachers

From Foundations, the five-question delegation check (fit, checkability, stakes, ownership, permission) works well in teaching. Add one question specific to education:

> **Would a student or family feel let down if they knew AI did this part?**

If the answer is yes (for example, personal comments on a report card), keep it, or use AI only to help you organize thoughts you then write yourself.

<TryIt title="Sort Your Week">

1. List every task from a typical teaching week, aiming for at least twelve. Include the small ones.
2. Mark each **Automate**, **Augment**, or **Keep**.
3. For every **Automate** or **Augment** task, note what student data it would involve and whether your approved tool can handle it. If you don't know which tools are approved, write down who to ask.
4. Pick the single task that would save you the most time. Try it with AI this week, using de-identified details only.
5. Note how long the checking took. Was it still worth it?

</TryIt>
