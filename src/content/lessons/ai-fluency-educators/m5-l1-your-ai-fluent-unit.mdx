## Bringing Both Jobs Together

This lesson combines everything from the course into one planning tool. A unit plan that's fluent with AI answers questions on both sides:

| | As a fluent practitioner | As a fluency teacher |
|---|---|---|
| **Delegation** | Which prep tasks will AI support? What stays with me? | What's each assignment's purpose, and what AI level does it get? |
| **Description** | What will I tell AI about my learners and objectives? | Which description skill will students practice, such as the coaching prompt? |
| **Discernment** | How will I check materials before students see them? | Which activity builds discernment: an error hunt, a source check? |
| **Diligence** | Is student data protected? Am I telling students how I used AI? | How will students disclose? Is the integrity approach fair? |

## Model It Out Loud

Students learn AI fluency most powerfully by **watching you practice it**. Short, honest demonstrations help a lot:

- **Think aloud while delegating:** "I'm going to use AI to draft practice questions, but I'm writing the essay prompt myself, because I need it tied to our discussion."
- **Show a failed response:** "Look, it got this date wrong. That's why we check."
- **Show iteration:** start with a vague request, improve it in front of the class, and compare the results.
- **Disclose your own use:** "These review questions were drafted with AI and checked by me."

Five minutes of modeling often teaches more than a full lesson about AI.

<Pitfall title="Fluency as a One-Off Lesson">

**Common mistake:** teaching a single "AI literacy" lesson in September and never mentioning it again.

Fluency is built through repeated practice inside real subject work. A few minutes in many units, such as one error hunt, one AI-level discussion, and one reflection, does far more than a standalone lesson.

</Pitfall>

<TryIt title="Plan One Unit">

1. Choose a real unit you'll teach in the next two months.
2. Fill in the table above for that unit, with at least one specific entry per cell.
3. For each major assignment in the unit, write its **purpose**, its **AI level** (0 to 3), and a one-line **reason**.
4. Choose one **discernment activity** and one **description activity** from Module 3 and place them in the unit calendar.
5. Plan **two modeling moments** where you'll think aloud about your own AI use.
6. Ask an AI assistant to critique your plan:
   ```text
   Here's my unit plan, including where AI is used and how students
   learn to use it. As an experienced instructional coach, identify
   (1) any place where AI might replace the learning I'm aiming for,
   (2) any privacy risks, and (3) one way to make the fluency
   activities better connected to the subject content.
   ```
7. Revise using the suggestions you agree with.

</TryIt>
