## More Than a Prompt

Description is how you communicate with AI so it can actually help. Most prompting advice covers only one part of it: describing the output you want. The framework identifies **three things** worth describing:

| Type | Question it answers | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Product description** | *What* should the result be? | "A one-page memo for the operations director, under 400 words, with a recommendation in the first paragraph." |
| **Process description** | *How* should AI approach the work? | "First list the three options with pros and cons. Then pick one and justify it. Flag any assumptions." |
| **Performance description** | *How* should AI behave while working with me? | "Be direct and skeptical. Push back if my reasoning is weak. Ask before making assumptions about our budget." |

Most disappointing AI results come from a missing type, not a badly worded sentence.

---

## Product Description

Product description is the most familiar. It covers what the finished thing should be:

- **Purpose and audience:** who it's for and what they need to do with it.
- **Format and length:** memo, table, bullet list, script; word count or page count.
- **Content and constraints:** what it must include, what it must avoid, and which sources to rely on.
- **Style and tone:** plain, formal, warm, technical; reading level.
- **Examples:** "Here's a past memo that hit the right tone." One good example often beats a paragraph of adjectives.

If you've learned the **CRAFT** method in AI Fundamentals, its Context, Ask, and Format parts are mostly product description.

## Process Description

Process description tells AI **how to get there**. It matters most for complex or high-stakes work, where *how* the answer is reached affects whether you can trust it:

- "Work step by step and show your reasoning before the final answer."
- "Only use information from the attached policy document. If the answer isn't there, say so."
- "Before drafting, ask me up to five clarifying questions."
- "Give me three different approaches first. I'll pick one before you write anything."

A good process description also makes **discernment** easier later, because you can see how the AI reached its answer instead of just the answer.

## Performance Description

Performance description sets how AI should **behave as a collaborator**. It's the least-used and often the most powerful:

- "Act as a tough reviewer. Don't soften criticism."
- "I'm learning this topic. Give hints, not answers."
- "Keep responses short. I'll ask for detail if I need it."
- "If you're not confident about a fact, tell me instead of guessing."

Performance description becomes essential in **agency** mode. When you configure an assistant that others will use, its instructions are mostly performance description: how to respond, what to refuse, when to hand off to a human.

<Pitfall title="Describing Only the Product">

**Common mistake:** writing a detailed description of the output and saying nothing about process or performance, then being surprised when the AI fills gaps with confident guesses or agrees with every idea you have.

If you need sources respected, reasoning shown, or honest pushback, say so explicitly. AI assistants tend to be agreeable and to keep going rather than stop and ask, unless you describe a different behavior.

</Pitfall>

<TryIt title="Add the Missing Two Types">

1. Pick a real request you'd normally make in one sentence, for example "help me write a proposal for a new mentoring program."
2. Expand it into a **product description**: audience, purpose, format, length, must-includes.
3. Add a **process description** of two or three sentences about how the AI should work: steps, sources, whether to ask questions first.
4. Add a **performance description** of one or two sentences about how it should behave with you.
5. Send the full version in a new chat. Then send only the one-sentence version in a separate new chat. Compare:
   - Which result needs less editing?
   - Which one asked you useful questions or flagged assumptions?
   - Which part of your description made the biggest difference?

</TryIt>
