## The 4D Routine

Fluency becomes real when it's a habit, not a checklist you dig out for special occasions. Here's a routine that fits the four competencies into everyday work. For a two-minute task, it takes seconds. For high-stakes work, slow down at each step.

| When | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| **Before** | What's my real goal? Which pieces should AI do: automate, augment, or none? Is this tool approved for this data? |
| **During** | Have I described the product, process, and behavior I need? Is each response accurate, sound, and on track? What's my next specific piece of feedback? |
| **After** | Have I verified what matters? Who needs to know AI was involved? Am I ready to put my name on this? |

## Fluency Grows With Practice

A few habits accelerate fluency over time:

- **Keep a prompt and description library.** Save descriptions that worked, along with notes on why.
- **Notice your failure patterns.** If discernment keeps catching the same problem, fix it in your default description.
- **Revisit delegation regularly.** Tools improve. Something that failed six months ago may work now, and something you automate may deserve more attention than you're giving it.
- **Talk about it.** Share what works with colleagues or classmates. Team norms around AI make diligence easier for everyone.

<Pitfall title="Fluency as a One-Time Achievement">

**Common mistake:** finishing a course, feeling fluent, and never adjusting how you work again.

AI tools, workplace policies, and your own role all keep changing. Treat fluency like fitness: something you maintain through regular practice and occasional honest self-assessment, not a certificate you earn once.

</Pitfall>

## Write Your Personal AI Use Statement

A **personal AI use statement** is a short, plain-language description of how you use AI. It clarifies your own boundaries and makes expectations clear to your manager, team, instructor, or clients. It's diligence made visible.

A strong statement covers:

1. **Where I use AI**, with examples: tasks you automate and tasks you augment.
2. **Where I don't**, and why: tasks you keep for yourself.
3. **How I check AI work** before relying on or sharing it.
4. **How I handle data**: what you never put into AI tools.
5. **How I disclose AI use**, and when.

<TryIt title="Draft Your Statement With AI as an Editor">

1. Write a rough draft of your statement yourself, five to ten sentences covering the five points above. Do this part on your own: it's the part that matters.
2. Then ask an AI assistant to help refine it:
   ```text
   Here's my personal statement about how I use AI at work. Don't
   rewrite it in your own voice. Instead: (1) point out anything
   vague or missing, (2) flag anything that might conflict with
   common workplace AI policies, and (3) suggest one concrete example
   I could add to make it clearer.

   [paste your draft]
   ```
3. Apply discernment: accept only the suggestions you agree with.
4. Revisit the self-check from Lesson 1.1. Is the competency you circled then reflected in your statement now?
5. Save the final version. Consider sharing it with your manager, team, or instructor.

</TryIt>
