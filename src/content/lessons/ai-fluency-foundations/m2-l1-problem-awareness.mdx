## Delegation Starts Before AI

When a manager delegates badly, it's rarely because the employee was the wrong person. Usually the manager didn't know what they wanted, or handed off the part of the job they needed to own. Delegating to AI fails the same way.

## Three Questions Before You Open a Chat

**Problem awareness** means understanding the work well enough to make smart decisions about it. Before reaching for AI, answer three questions:

1. **What's the real goal?** Not the task, the goal. "Write a project update" is a task. "Get the steering committee to approve two more weeks without losing confidence in the team" is a goal. The goal changes what a good update looks like.
2. **What does success look like?** How will you know the result is good? Who will judge it, and by what standard?
3. **Which parts of this work matter most, and why?** Some parts are mechanical. Some require your judgment, relationships, or expertise. Some are the whole point, like an essay assignment designed to build *your* thinking.

<Pitfall title="Delegating the Point of the Work">

**Common mistake:** handing AI the part of a task that was the reason for doing it.

A student who asks AI to write a reflective essay gets an essay but skips the reflection the assignment was designed to produce. A manager who has AI write performance reviews from bullet points gets reviews, but not the careful thinking about each person that reviews exist to force. Ask: *if AI does this part, what do I lose?*

</Pitfall>

## Break the Work Into Pieces

Most real tasks aren't one thing. Breaking them down shows which parts suit AI. Take "prepare a presentation for a new client":

| Piece | Needs your judgment? | Easy to check? | Good AI candidate? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research the client's industry | Partly | Hard: facts need verification | **Augment**: AI suggests, you verify |
| Decide the key message | Yes, entirely | — | **You.** This is the point. |
| Draft an outline | Somewhat | Yes | **Augment**: draft together |
| Write speaker notes | Somewhat | Yes | **Automate**, then edit in your voice |
| Format slides consistently | No | Yes | **Automate** |
| Rehearse and get feedback | Yes | — | **Augment**: AI as a practice audience |

Notice that the decision isn't "use AI or not." It's piece by piece. Some pieces are pure automation, some are partnership, and one stays entirely with you.

<TryIt title="Map a Real Task Before Delegating">

1. Pick a task you'll actually do this week that's bigger than a single email.
2. Write one sentence for the **real goal** and one sentence for **what success looks like**.
3. Break the task into four to seven pieces, using a table like the one above.
4. Mark each piece **You**, **Augment**, or **Automate**, with a short reason.
5. Optional: share your breakdown with an AI assistant and ask:
   ```text
   Here's a task I've broken into pieces, with my plan for which
   pieces AI should help with. Challenge my plan: where am I handing
   off something I should own, or doing by hand something AI could
   speed up? Don't do any of the work yet.
   ```
   Decide for yourself whether its pushback is right.

</TryIt>
