## Fluent Isn't the Same as Right

AI output almost always *sounds* good: well organized, confident, grammatically clean. That's exactly why discernment matters. Polish is not evidence of accuracy. Discernment is the skill of assessing AI's work **as carefully as you would a new colleague's**, and it mirrors the three types of description:

| Type | Question | Red flags |
|---|---|---|
| **Product discernment** | Is the output accurate, complete, and fit for its purpose? | Unverifiable specifics, missing pieces, wrong audience, generic filler |
| **Process discernment** | Was the reasoning sound? | Logical leaps, ignored sources, unstated assumptions, steps that don't connect |
| **Performance discernment** | Did the AI behave the way I needed? | Agreeing with everything, not asking when it should have, overconfidence, drifting from instructions |

---

## Product Discernment: Check the Output

Run through these checks, scaled to the stakes:

1. **Accuracy.** Are the facts, figures, names, dates, quotes, and citations real? Verify specifics against a source you trust: the original document, an official site, or an expert. Be most suspicious of precise-looking details such as exact percentages, page numbers, and quotations.
2. **Completeness.** Did it cover everything you asked for? AI often quietly drops a requirement, especially from long instructions.
3. **Fit.** Is it right for *this* audience and purpose? A technically correct answer at the wrong reading level is still the wrong answer.
4. **Substance.** Is there real content, or just confident-sounding generalities? Try summarizing the key point in one sentence. If you can't, there may not be one.

## Process Discernment: Check the Reasoning

When AI shows its work, or when you ask it to, look at *how* it got there:

- Does each step actually follow from the previous one?
- Did it use the sources you specified, or drift to general knowledge?
- What did it assume? Are those assumptions true for your situation?
- If it compared options, did it apply the same criteria to each?

A right answer reached by bad reasoning is a warning sign. It won't stay right when the situation changes slightly.

## Performance Discernment: Check the Behavior

Step back from the content and ask how the collaboration went:

- **Too agreeable?** AI assistants tend to go along with your framing. If it praised every idea you had, ask it directly for the weaknesses.
- **Asked when it should have?** If key information was missing, did it flag the gap or quietly fill it?
- **Honest about uncertainty?** Did it signal low confidence where appropriate, or present guesses as facts?
- **Followed your performance description?** If you asked for hints and got answers, it drifted.

<Pitfall title="Asking the Same AI to Verify Itself">

**Common mistake:** asking "Are you sure?" or "Is that accurate?" and treating "Yes" as verification.

AI may confidently confirm an error, or abandon a correct answer just because you questioned it. Neither tells you the truth. Asking for sources, reasoning, or counter-arguments can be useful **input** to your judgment, but real verification means checking against something outside the conversation.

</Pitfall>

<TryIt title="Audit an AI Answer on All Three Levels">

1. Ask an AI assistant a question in an area you know well, ideally one with specifics, for example:
   ```text
   Explain the main rules for [a process you know well at work or
   in a hobby], including any key numbers, deadlines, or names.
   ```
2. **Product:** Mark every specific claim as ✅ correct, ❌ wrong, or ❓ unsure. Count each.
3. **Process:** Ask *"Walk me through how you arrived at that, and what you assumed."* Does the reasoning hold up?
4. **Performance:** Push back on a claim you *know* is correct: *"I think that's wrong."* Does the AI hold its ground with reasoning, or cave immediately?
5. Write two sentences on what this tells you about trusting this tool in an area you *don't* know well.

</TryIt>
