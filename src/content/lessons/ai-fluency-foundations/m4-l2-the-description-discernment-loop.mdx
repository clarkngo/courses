## Two Skills, One Loop

Description and discernment aren't separate steps. They feed each other. Each time you judge a response, you learn something about what your description was missing. Each better description gives you something more useful to judge.

The step most people skip is **Diagnose**. "This isn't good, try again" gives the AI nothing to work with. Asking *why* it isn't good usually points to a specific gap in your description.

---

## From Reaction to Diagnosis

| What you notice (discern) | Likely cause (diagnose) | Better feedback (describe) |
|---|---|---|
| "Too generic." | Missing context about audience or situation. | "Readers are night-shift nurses with ten minutes on break. Rewrite for them." |
| "Wrong tone." | Tone wasn't specified, or the adjective was vague. | "Less formal, closer to this example: [paste]." |
| "It made things up." | No source boundary; gaps were filled with guesses. | "Use only the attached notes. Put [CHECK] where you'd need information you don't have." |
| "It missed half my requirements." | Instructions were long and unordered. | "Let's do this in parts. First, only section 1, covering points A and B." |
| "It agreed with my bad idea." | No performance description inviting pushback. | "Critique this plan as a skeptical finance director. Name the weakest assumption." |

**Specific feedback beats starting over.** In the same conversation, the AI can see both its draft and your critique, so revisions are targeted.

## Iterate, Restart, or Stop?

The loop isn't infinite. Know when to change course:

- **Keep iterating** when each round gets closer and the problems are specific and fixable.
- **Restart in a new chat** when the conversation has gone off track, filled up with failed attempts, or anchored on a bad early direction. Bring a better description built from what you learned.
- **Stop and rethink delegation** when several rounds aren't converging, or when you realize the problem is the task, not the wording. Maybe this part should be yours, or needs a different tool. That's discernment feeding back into **delegation**.

<Pitfall title="Polishing the Wrong Thing">

**Common mistake:** spending ten rounds perfecting the wording of a draft whose core idea is wrong.

Discern from the top down: first ask whether the overall approach and key claims are right, then the structure, and only then the sentence-level polish. Fixing commas in a flawed argument wastes your time and hides the real problem.

</Pitfall>

<TryIt title="Run the Loop and Build Your Checklist">

1. Ask an AI for something moderately complex you actually need, such as a plan, a draft, or an explanation.
2. Run the loop at least **three times**. Each time, write down:
   - **Discern:** the single biggest problem with the current version.
   - **Diagnose:** the gap in your description that caused it.
   - **Describe:** the exact feedback you sent.
3. Decide at the end: was this an **iterate**, **restart**, or **rethink delegation** situation?
4. From what you noticed, write a **personal discernment checklist** of five to seven questions you'll ask about any important AI output. Start with these and adapt them:
   - Are the specific facts verified against a real source?
   - Did it do everything I asked?
   - Is it right for the audience?
   - What did it assume?
   - Would I sign my name to this?
5. Save the checklist somewhere you'll see it. You'll use it in the course challenge.

</TryIt>
