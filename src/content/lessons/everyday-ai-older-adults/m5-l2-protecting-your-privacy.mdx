## Think "Postcard," Not "Sealed Letter"

A helpful way to think about AI chats: **write as if you're writing on a postcard.** Most of the time nobody else will read it. But it passes through other hands, it may be stored, and it isn't the place for secrets.

AI companies have privacy policies and security protections, and the major ones are careful. Still, your conversations are typically stored on the company's computers, may be reviewed to improve safety, and, depending on your settings, may be used to train future AI. So it's wise to be choosy.

---

## A Simple Traffic Light

### 🟢 Generally fine to share

- General questions and everyday tasks
- Your general situation: "I'm 70, retired, live alone, and have arthritis"
- Recipes, travel ideas, hobbies, writing drafts
- Documents with personal details **covered or removed**

### 🟡 Think twice

- Specific health conditions tied to your name
- Details about family members, especially children
- Exact home address or daily routine ("I'm away every Tuesday 9 to 5")
- Photos of other people

### 🔴 Never share

- **Social Security, Medicare, or passport numbers**
- **Bank account, credit card, or PIN numbers**
- **Passwords** of any kind
- **One-time codes** sent to your phone ("Your verification code is…")
- Scans of your driver's license or ID

No legitimate AI assistant will ever ask you for your password, a verification code, or your Social Security number. If one does, or something that looks like one does, stop. It's a scam.

<Pitfall title="Sharing a Verification Code">

**Common mistake:** reading a six-digit code from a text message to someone, or typing it into a chat, because they said they needed it to "verify your account" or "fix a problem."

Those codes are the keys to your accounts. **Never share them with anyone**, including someone claiming to be from your bank, a tech company, or the government. Real companies will never ask for them.

</Pitfall>

## Housekeeping: Deleting Chats

You can usually delete individual conversations in your AI app:

- Look for a list of past chats (often under a **☰ menu** or a **clock** icon).
- Press and hold (on a phone) or hover (on a computer) over a chat, then choose **Delete**.

If you ever accidentally share something sensitive, delete that chat. If it was a password or account number, **change the password** or **call your bank** too, since deleting a chat doesn't undo every copy.

You can also revisit the settings from Module 2 at any time to turn off chat history or training.

## Protecting Your Family's Voices and Faces

Remember from the last lesson that scammers can clone a voice from a short video. A few gentle habits help the whole family:

- **Think before posting videos with voices** publicly on Facebook or other social media. Consider sharing family videos only with "Friends" or in private groups.
- **Avoid posting travel plans in real time.** Share the vacation photos after you're home.
- **Be careful with "fun" online quizzes** that ask your first pet's name, your mother's maiden name, or your first car. Those are common security questions.

## Using AI Built Into Other Apps

AI features are appearing inside email, photo, and word-processing programs. When an app asks permission to let AI "read your email" or "access your photos," it's okay to say **"Not now"** until you understand what it does. You can ask your AI assistant to explain: *"My email app is asking to turn on AI summaries. What does that mean for my privacy?"*, then decide.
