## "What Is This Letter Actually Saying?"

Insurance notices, Medicare statements, utility bills, lease renewals, warranty terms: much of the paperwork that arrives in the mail seems written to confuse. An AI assistant can act as a patient translator, turning dense language into plain English and pointing out what you need to *do*.

---

## Three Ways to Show AI a Document

1. **Type or paste the confusing part.** If it came by email or website, highlight the text, copy it, and paste it into the chat.
2. **Take a photo.** Most AI apps have a **camera** 📷 or **paperclip** 📎 button in the chat box. Snap a picture of the page and ask your question.
3. **Upload a file.** On a computer, you can usually attach a PDF the same way.

The AI doesn't need your name or account number to explain what "explanation of benefits" means. Fold the top of the letter over, put a sticky note over numbers, or use your phone's **crop** tool after taking the photo.

## Good Questions to Ask About Paperwork

| You want to know… | Ask… |
|---|---|
| The big picture | *"Explain this letter in plain English in three sentences."* |
| What to do | *"Is there anything I need to do, and by when?"* |
| What a term means | *"What do 'deductible,' 'copay,' and 'coinsurance' mean? Use a simple example."* |
| Whether something is normal | *"Is it common for a bill to say 'This is not a bill'?"* |
| What to ask | *"What questions should I ask when I call the company about this?"* |
| How to compare | *"Here are two phone plans. Which is cheaper for someone who mostly calls and rarely uses data?"* |

That last row is a great use of AI: **comparing options** side by side and spelling out the trade-offs.

<Pitfall title="Letting AI Make the Final Decision">

**Common mistake:** acting on AI's interpretation of an important document without confirming it.

AI can misread a photo, miss a line, or misunderstand a rule that's specific to your plan or state. Use it to **understand** the paperwork and **prepare questions**. Then, before you pay, cancel, sign, or ignore something important, confirm with the company, your plan's customer service, or a trusted advisor. For Medicare questions in the US, your **State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP)** offers free one-on-one counseling.

</Pitfall>

## Spotting Scam Letters

AI can also be a helpful second opinion on suspicious mail and email. Copy the text (without any personal details) and ask:

> *"Does this look like a scam? What warning signs do you see?"*

AI is good at noticing classic red flags: urgent deadlines, threats, requests for gift cards or wire transfers, and pressure to act before talking to anyone. But it **cannot** check whether a specific company really sent the message. To be sure, contact the company using a phone number you find yourself, from your card, a past statement, or the official website. You'll learn much more about scams in Module 5.

<TryIt title="Translate One Piece of Paperwork">

1. Find a letter, bill, or statement you've found confusing (or a sample from a website, if you don't have one handy).
2. Cover your name, address, and any account or ID numbers.
3. Take a photo or paste the text into your AI assistant.
4. Ask: *"Explain this in plain English. Is there anything I need to do, and by when?"*
5. Follow up with: *"What questions should I ask if I call about this?"* Write the questions down for next time.

</TryIt>
