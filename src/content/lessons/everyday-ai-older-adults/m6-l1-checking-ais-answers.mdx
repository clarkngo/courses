## Not Everything Needs Checking, But Some Things Must

If AI suggests a dinner recipe and it turns out a bit bland, no harm done. If AI gives you the wrong deadline for a Medicare enrollment, that could cost you. The trick is knowing **how much checking** each answer deserves.

---

## The Three-Question Routine

Before acting on an AI answer, ask yourself:

- **Low stakes, general advice, matches your experience?** Go ahead and use it.
- **High stakes, specific facts, or something feels off?** Double-check before you act.

| Usually fine as-is | Always double-check |
|---|---|
| Recipe ideas | Medication and health decisions |
| Gift suggestions | Deadlines (taxes, Medicare, benefits) |
| Drafts of cards and emails | Phone numbers, addresses, websites |
| Explaining a general idea | Laws and rules in your state |
| Trivia for fun | Prices, hours, and availability |
| Brainstorming hobbies | Anything involving money |

## Easy Ways to Double-Check

### Ask the AI where it got the information

> *"How confident are you in that? Where could I confirm it?"*

Some AI assistants can search the web and will show **links to sources**. Click a link and see whether the page really says what the AI claimed. (Make sure the website is the real, official one.)

### Go to the official source

- **Government benefits:** the official agency site, such as **ssa.gov** or **medicare.gov** in the US. Look for **.gov** at the end of the address.
- **Companies:** the phone number on your card or bill, or the company's official website.
- **Health:** your doctor, nurse line, or pharmacist.

### Ask a person you trust

A family member, a librarian, a senior center volunteer, or a friend who knows the subject. Librarians in particular are wonderful at helping people check information, and it's free.

<Pitfall title="Asking the AI 'Are You Sure?' and Stopping There">

**Common mistake:** asking the AI to double-check itself and accepting whatever it says.

Sometimes the AI will change a correct answer to a wrong one just because you asked "Are you sure?", and sometimes it will repeat a mistake confidently. Asking is fine, but for anything important, **confirm with a source outside the AI**.

</Pitfall>

## When AI Says "I Can't Help With That"

Sometimes an AI assistant will decline a request or add a lot of cautious warnings, especially about health, legal, or money questions. That's usually because it's designed to be careful in those areas. Try:

- Rephrasing to ask for **general information** rather than personal advice: *"What are the general factors people consider when deciding whether to downsize their home?"*
- Asking **what questions to bring** to a professional.

And if it truly can't help, that's a sign this question belongs with a real expert.

<TryIt title="Catch an AI Mistake">

1. Ask your AI assistant a factual question you already know the answer to, about your hometown, your career, or a hobby you know well. Ask for specific details: dates, names, or numbers.
2. Read the answer carefully. Is everything right?
3. Now ask it something you *don't* know, like *"When is the deadline to sign up for Medicare Part B if I'm turning 65?"*, and confirm the answer on the official website.
4. Notice: even when AI is mostly right, small details can be off.

</TryIt>
