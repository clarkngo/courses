## Look How Far You've Come

At the start of this course, AI may have felt mysterious or even a little threatening. Now you know:

- **What AI is**: a fluent predictor of words that can be helpful *and* confidently wrong.
- **How to set it up** safely and comfortably.
- **How to ask good questions** and keep the conversation going.
- **How to use it** for writing, paperwork, health preparation, learning, and fun.
- **How to stay safe** from scams and protect your privacy.
- **How to check** the answers that matter.

That's real AI fluency, and more than many people half your age have.

## Keep Learning, Gently

AI tools change often. New buttons appear, apps get updates, and new features arrive. You don't need to keep up with everything. A few habits help:

- **When something looks different, ask the AI itself:** *"The app looks different today. Where did the microphone button go?"*
- **Try one new thing a month.** Maybe voice conversation, or showing it a photo of a plant you can't identify.
- **Check your local library or senior center.** Many now offer free AI and technology classes.

<Pitfall title="Letting AI Replace the People in Your Life">

**Common mistake:** asking AI for everything because it's always available and never busy.

AI is a wonderful tool, but some questions are better asked of people: the grandchild who'd love to show you how something works, the neighbor who knows the best local plumber, the friend who'd enjoy a phone call. Use AI to make room for more connection, not less.

</Pitfall>

## Help Someone Else Get Started

One of the best ways to cement what you've learned is to teach it. If a friend is curious about AI, you're now well-placed to help. A few tips:

- **Start with something they care about**, like a recipe, a card for a grandchild, or a question about their garden, not with technology.
- **Let them do the typing or talking.** Resist the urge to take over.
- **Share the safety basics first:** no private numbers, check important facts, and the family safe word.
- **Be patient.** You remember what it was like to start.
