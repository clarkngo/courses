## A Blank Page Is the Hardest Part

Most of us know *what* we want to say. Getting started is the hard part: the right opening line, the right tone, remembering to include everything. This is where an AI assistant shines. It gives you a **first draft** in seconds, and you shape it into your own words.

---

## Four Everyday Writing Jobs

### 1. Cards and personal messages

> *"Help me write a birthday message for my granddaughter Emma, who's turning 16. She loves horses and just started driving lessons. I want it warm and a little funny, about four sentences. Sign it 'Love, Grandpa Joe.'"*

### 2. Thank-you notes

> *"Write a short thank-you note to my neighbor Linda, who brought me soup every day for a week after my surgery. I want her to know it meant a lot. Keep it simple and heartfelt."*

### 3. Everyday emails

> *"Write a brief email to the leader of my book club saying I can't host next month because of a family visit, but I'd be happy to host in June instead."*

### 4. Firm complaints and requests

AI is especially good at letters you feel nervous writing, because it keeps the tone **calm, clear, and polite** even when you're frustrated.

> *"I was charged twice for my internet bill this month, $64.99 each time on March 3. Please write a polite but firm letter to the company asking them to refund the duplicate charge within 10 business days. Include a spot for my account number."*

Notice that last one says "include a **spot** for my account number." That's on purpose.

<Pitfall title="Typing Private Numbers Into the Chat">

**Common mistake:** giving the AI your full account number, Social Security number, Medicare number, or bank details so it can "fill everything in."

You don't need to. Ask the AI to leave a blank, like **[ACCOUNT NUMBER]**, and type the real number yourself after you copy the letter into your email or document. Keep private numbers out of AI chats entirely.

</Pitfall>

---

## Make It Sound Like You

AI drafts can sound a bit stiff or overly cheerful. A few ways to fix that:

- **Tell it your style.** *"I'm usually short and to the point. Make it sound less formal."*
- **Give it an example.** Paste a message you wrote in the past and say: *"Write in a similar style to this."*
- **Ask for options.** *"Give me three different opening lines to choose from."*
- **Edit it yourself.** Change words that you'd never use. Add a memory only you would know.

Always **read the whole draft** before you send it. Make sure the names, dates, and amounts are right, and that it says what you mean.

---

## Getting the Text Where It Needs to Go

Once you're happy with the draft:

1. **Copy it.** On a phone, most AI apps show a **copy** button (two overlapping squares ⧉) under the answer. On a computer, you can also highlight the text with your mouse and press **Ctrl+C** (**Cmd+C** on a Mac).
2. **Paste it** into your email, text message, or document: press and hold, then tap **Paste** on a phone, or press **Ctrl+V** (**Cmd+V** on a Mac).
3. **Fill in any blanks** like [ACCOUNT NUMBER] yourself.

For a handwritten card, simply copy the words out by hand, which also makes them feel more personal.

---

## Should You Tell People AI Helped?

For a personal message, use your judgment. Many people see AI as just another writing aid, like a greeting card's printed verse or a thesaurus. What matters is that **the feelings are genuinely yours** and you've made the words your own. For anything official or important, you are responsible for what it says, whoever drafted it.

<TryIt title="Write Something Real">

1. Pick a message you've been meaning to write: a thank-you, a birthday card, or an email you've been putting off.
2. Ask your AI assistant for a draft, including names, the occasion, the tone you want, and the length.
3. Ask for one change, such as *"Make it a little less formal"* or *"Add something about her new puppy."*
4. Copy it into an email or card, put it in your own words where needed, and send it.

</TryIt>
