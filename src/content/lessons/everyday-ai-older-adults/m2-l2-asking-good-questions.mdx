## Talk to It Like a Helpful Stranger

Remember the well-read helper from Lesson 1? They know a lot about the world but **nothing about you**. The more you tell them about your situation, the better their help will be.

Compare these two requests:

- ❌ *"Exercises."*
- ✅ *"I'm 74 and I had a knee replacement last year. My physical therapist cleared me for gentle exercise. Can you suggest five easy exercises I can do sitting in a chair, with simple instructions?"*

The first gets a generic list that might not suit you at all. The second gets something you can actually use.

## A Simple Four-Part Recipe

You don't need special words or "prompts." Just try to include these four things:

**Example:**

> "I'm a retired teacher and I volunteer at the library. **[who you are]** Please write a short notice **[what you want]** announcing a free tech help session on Tuesdays from 10 to noon in the community room, for people who want help with phones and tablets. **[the details]** Keep it friendly and under 60 words, and suggest a title in big letters. **[how you'd like it]**"

You won't always need all four parts. A quick question like *"How long do I boil an egg for a soft yolk?"* is fine as it is.

## The Real Secret: Keep the Conversation Going

The first answer is just a starting point. You can **reply** as if you're talking to a person, and the AI remembers what you said earlier in the same conversation. Some useful follow-ups:

| If the answer is… | Try saying… |
|---|---|
| Too complicated | *"Can you explain that more simply, like you would to a friend?"* |
| Too long | *"Please shorten that to three sentences."* |
| Not quite right | *"That's close, but I don't have an oven. Can you change it for a stovetop?"* |
| Missing something | *"You didn't mention cost. Roughly how much would that be?"* |
| Hard to follow | *"Please give me numbered steps, one action per step."* |
| Confusing a word | *"What does 'deductible' mean?"* |

There's no limit to how many times you can ask it to try again, and it will never get impatient with you.

<Pitfall title="Starting Over Instead of Following Up">

**Common mistake:** closing the app and starting a brand-new chat every time an answer isn't right.

When you start a new chat, the AI forgets everything you told it. It's usually better to stay in the same conversation and say what you'd like changed. Start a **new chat** only when you switch to a completely different topic. That keeps things tidy and stops old details from getting mixed in.

</Pitfall>

## It's Okay to Be Polite (and Okay Not To)

Many people say "please" and "thank you" to AI. That's perfectly fine and can make the conversation feel more natural. It's also fine not to. The AI doesn't have feelings to hurt. What matters most is being **clear**.

<TryIt title="Practice the Recipe">

1. Think of something real you'd like help with this week: a meal plan, a gift idea, how to use a feature on your phone, or a question you've been curious about.
2. Write your question using as many of the four parts as make sense.
3. Read the answer, then send at least **two follow-ups** from the table above, for example, *"Explain that more simply"* and *"Please make it shorter."*
4. Notice how much better the answer gets after a couple of rounds.

</TryIt>
