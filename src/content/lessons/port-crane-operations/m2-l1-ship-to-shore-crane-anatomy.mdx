## The Giants on the Wharf

A ship-to-shore (STS) container crane is a huge steel **gantry** that travels along rails on the wharf and reaches out over the ship to lift containers in and out of the cells and off the deck.

| Part | Job |
|---|---|
| **Portal (legs and sill beams)** | Structure that straddles the apron lanes |
| **Gantry travel system** | Wheels on rails let the crane move along the wharf to line up with each ship bay |
| **Boom** | Extends over the ship; on many cranes it **hinges up** to clear the ship's superstructure when the vessel arrives and departs |
| **Backreach girder** | Extends landside for handling hatch covers and buffer space |
| **Trolley** | Runs along the boom and girder, carrying the load (and often the operator cab) |
| **Hoist** | Raises and lowers the load with wire ropes |
| **Headblock** | Connects the hoist ropes to the spreader |
| **Spreader** | Locks onto the container's corner castings |
| **Machinery house** | Holds hoist and trolley drives and electrical equipment |
| **Operator cab** | Usually rides with the trolley, with a downward view through the floor |

## The Four Motions

| Motion | What moves | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **Hoist** | The spreader and load | Up and down |
| **Trolley** | The trolley along the boom | Waterside and landside |
| **Gantry** | The entire crane along the rails | Along the wharf |
| **Boom hoist** | The boom raises or lowers | Usually only when the crane is not working cargo |

A skilled operator blends **hoist and trolley** motions smoothly while controlling **sway**, the swinging of the load on the ropes. Modern cranes have **anti-sway** systems and automation aids, but operator skill still matters.

---

## Size: Outreach and Lift Height

- **Outreach** is how far the crane reaches over the water from the waterside rail. It determines how many **containers across** the crane can work. The largest ships today are **more than 20 containers wide**.
- **Lift height** is the height under the spreader above the rail. It must clear containers stacked high on deck.

As ships grow, ports raise existing cranes or buy larger ones.

---

## Wind and Storms

Cranes act like sails. They're designed to operate only up to a set **wind speed limit** (from the maker and terminal rules), and to survive much higher wind when properly **secured**.

- **Anemometers** on the crane trigger warnings and stop work.
- **Rail clamps** and **brakes** resist being pushed along the rails.
- **Stowage pins** and **tie-downs** anchor the crane to the wharf in storm conditions.
- Cranes have been blown along their rails and toppled when not secured in time.

<Pitfall title="Working Through a Gusty Afternoon">

**Common mistake:** wind speed hovers near the limit, and the gang keeps working to finish the bay.

Gusts can drive the crane or swing a container into the ship's structure or a stack. Follow the terminal's **wind procedure**. Stop at the limit, park the spreader, and secure the crane as specified, early rather than late.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

An STS crane is a rail-mounted **gantry** with a **boom**, **trolley**, **hoist**, **headblock**, and **spreader**. Its motions are **hoist, trolley, gantry, and boom hoist**. **Outreach** and **lift height** define the ships it can work. Respect **wind limits** and secure the crane with **clamps, pins, and tie-downs**.
