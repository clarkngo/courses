## Rigging Connects the Crane to the Load

Containers lift with spreaders, but **breakbulk**, **project cargo**, **hatch covers**, **equipment**, and **maintenance lifts** all need **rigging**: slings, shackles, hooks, and spreader bars. Rigging that fails drops the load, no matter how good the crane is.

Key U.S. standards include **ASME B30.9** (slings), **B30.26** (rigging hardware), and **B30.10** (hooks), plus OSHA rules for marine terminals and longshoring.

---

## Working Load Limit (WLL)

Every piece of rigging has a **Working Load Limit (WLL)**, also called rated capacity, marked on its **tag or body**. It's the maximum load the manufacturer allows in normal service, with a built-in **design factor** (for example, around 5:1 for many wire rope and synthetic slings and alloy chain at 4:1, as set by the applicable standard and manufacturer).

**Never exceed the WLL.** The design factor is for wear, dynamic loading, and uncertainty, not extra capacity.

**No tag, no lift.** A sling with a missing or unreadable ID tag must be removed from service.

## Sling Types

| Sling | Strengths | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Wire rope** | Strong, durable, heat-tolerant | Broken wires, kinks, crushing, birdcaging, corrosion |
| **Alloy chain** (Grade 80 or 100) | Rugged, heat- and abrasion-resistant, adjustable | Stretch, cracks, nicks, gouges, bent links. **Only alloy chain** is used for overhead lifting, never transport or proof-coil chain. |
| **Synthetic web** | Light, flexible, protects finished surfaces | Cuts, burns, chemical damage, worn stitching; needs **edge protection** |
| **Synthetic round sling** | Very flexible, light | Cuts; exposed core yarns; many have **warning yarns** that show when the cover is damaged |

### Wire Rope Basics

Wire rope is made of **wires** twisted into **strands**, laid around a **core**. A common designation like **6×19 IWRC** means 6 strands of about 19 wires each, around an **independent wire rope core** (stronger and more crush-resistant than a fiber core).

## Hitches

| Hitch | Description | Capacity vs. vertical (typical) |
|---|---|---|
| **Vertical** | Single leg straight up | 100% of the vertical rating |
| **Choker** | Sling wraps the load and passes through its own eye | **Less**: often around 75–80% for wire rope at standard choke angles; check the tag |
| **Basket** | Sling cradles the load, both eyes on the hook | Up to **200%** when legs are vertical; less as legs angle out |

The sling's **tag** lists capacities for each hitch type. Angles reduce capacity further (Lesson 3.2).

## Shackles and Hooks

### Shackles

- **Screw pin** shackles for temporary lifts; **bolt-type (safety)** shackles with a nut and cotter pin for long-term or rotating loads.
- WLL is marked on the body. Use only **load-rated** shackles.
- Load the shackle **in line**; **side loading** reduces capacity per the maker's chart.
- Never replace a pin with a bolt.

### Hooks

- Should have a working **safety latch** (where required).
- Load sits in the **bowl** (saddle), never on the **tip**.
- Remove from service if the **throat opening** has increased or the hook is **twisted** beyond limits (ASME B30.10 and the maker give criteria), or if there are cracks.

## Inspection

| Inspection | When | Who |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-use / frequent** | Before each use or each shift | Competent person (often the rigger) |
| **Periodic** | At set intervals (often at least annually; more often in severe service) | Qualified/competent person; **documented** |

### Removal Criteria (Examples from ASME B30.9 for Slings)

**Wire rope slings** (single-part): remove for **10 randomly distributed broken wires in one rope lay**, or **5 broken wires in one strand in one rope lay**; **kinking, crushing, birdcaging**, or other distortion; **heat damage**; severe **corrosion**; damaged **end fittings**; missing or unreadable **ID tag**.

**Synthetic slings**: remove for **cuts, tears, holes, burns, melting, charring**, chemical damage, broken stitching in load-bearing splices, exposed **warning yarns** or core, knots, or a missing tag.

**Alloy chain**: remove for **cracks**, **stretch** beyond the maker's limit, **nicks and gouges**, excessive **wear**, **bent or twisted links**, or a missing tag.

Damaged gear gets **tagged out** and removed from the work area so it can't be grabbed by mistake.

<Pitfall title="Tying a Knot in a Sling to Shorten It">

**Common mistake:** a web sling is too long, so someone ties a knot in it.

Knots can cut sling strength drastically and damage fibers. Use the **correct length** sling, an **adjustable** chain sling, or a **proper shortening** device rated for the purpose.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

Every piece of rigging has a **WLL**. **No tag, no lift.** Choose **wire rope, alloy chain, or synthetic** slings for the job, and protect synthetics from edges. **Chokers** reduce capacity; **baskets** can double it with vertical legs. Load **shackles** in line and **hooks** in the bowl. Inspect **before each use** and **periodically**, and remove damaged gear using **ASME B30** criteria.
