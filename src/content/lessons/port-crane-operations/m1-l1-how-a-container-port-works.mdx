## The Box That Changed the World

Before standardized shipping containers, cargo was loaded piece by piece (**breakbulk**), which took days or weeks per ship. The **intermodal container** changed that. A sealed steel box moves from factory to ship to train to truck without its contents being handled.

Today, much of the world's general cargo moves in containers, and a modern terminal can move thousands of boxes a day.

### Container Units

| Unit | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **TEU** | Twenty-foot equivalent unit: one 20-ft container |
| **FEU** | Forty-foot equivalent unit: one 40-ft container (= 2 TEU) |
| **High cube** | Taller container (9 ft 6 in instead of 8 ft 6 in) |
| **Reefer** | Refrigerated container that needs power at the terminal and aboard |

Ship capacity and port volume are counted in **TEUs**.

| Area | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Berth / wharf** | Ships tie up; ship-to-shore cranes work the vessel |
| **Apron** | Space under and behind the cranes where boxes are handed off to transport |
| **Container yard (CY)** | Rows of stacked containers, organized by ship, destination, weight, and type |
| **Reefer racks** | Power outlets and monitoring for refrigerated containers |
| **Gate** | Truck check-in and check-out; inspections and paperwork |
| **Rail yard** | Building and unloading intermodal trains |
| **Maintenance and repair (M&R)** | Repair of equipment and containers |

### The Terminal Operating System

A **terminal operating system (TOS)** tracks every container's location and plans every move: which crane, which truck, which stack slot. Equipment operators get work instructions on in-cab screens.

### Dwell Time

**Dwell time** is how long a container sits in the yard. Long dwell clogs the yard and slows everything, which is why terminals track it closely.

## The Pacific Northwest Gateway

The ports of **Seattle and Tacoma** manage their container business jointly through the **Northwest Seaport Alliance**, formed in **2015**, making the region one of the largest container gateways in North America. Cargo moves inland largely by **rail**, connecting Asia trade to the Midwest. The region also handles **grain** exports (especially on the Columbia River), **autos**, **breakbulk**, and **Alaska** trade.

## Key Players

| Player | Role |
|---|---|
| **Port authority** | Owns land and infrastructure; leases terminals |
| **Terminal operator** | Runs daily operations under the lease |
| **Shipping line** | Owns or charters ships; books cargo |
| **Longshore workers** | Load and unload ships and operate terminal equipment; on the U.S. West Coast, represented by the **ILWU** |
| **Truckers, railroads** | Move containers inland |
| **U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)** | Clears imports; screens cargo |
| **U.S. Coast Guard** | Port safety and security |

<Pitfall title="Treating the Terminal Like a Parking Lot">

**Common mistake:** a new worker walks across a container yard to save time.

Terminals are busy industrial sites with **large machines that have big blind spots**, moving fast and constantly. Stay in **designated pedestrian routes**, wear **high-visibility gear**, and never enter operating areas without authorization and communication.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

Containers are counted in **TEUs**. A terminal moves boxes **berth → apron → yard → gate or rail** under a **terminal operating system**. Seattle and Tacoma operate as the **Northwest Seaport Alliance**. Ports involve **port authorities, terminal operators, shipping lines, longshore workers, carriers, CBP, and the Coast Guard**.

<TryIt title="Count the TEUs">

A ship discharges **1,200 forty-foot containers** and **600 twenty-foot containers**. How many TEUs?

1,200 × 2 + 600 × 1 = **3,000 TEU**.

If the terminal's cranes average **30 moves per hour** each (a move is one container), and four cranes work the ship, how long does discharge take? 1,800 moves ÷ (4 × 30) = **15 hours**.

</TryIt>
