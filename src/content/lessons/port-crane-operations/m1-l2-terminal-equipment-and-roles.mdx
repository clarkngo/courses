## Container Handling Equipment

| Equipment | What it does | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ship-to-shore (STS) crane** | Lifts containers between ship and wharf | The largest machines on the terminal; covered in Module 2 |
| **Yard tractor (UTR, hostler)** | Pulls containers on chassis or bomb carts between crane and yard | Fast, numerous, and a major source of traffic hazards |
| **Straddle carrier** | Straddles a container, lifts it, and carries it; can stack 1-over-2 or more | Combines transport and stacking |
| **Rubber-tired gantry (RTG)** | Spans several rows of containers in the yard and stacks them | Moves between blocks on tires; many now electric or hybrid |
| **Rail-mounted gantry (RMG) / automated stacking crane (ASC)** | Like an RTG but on rails; often automated | Common in rail yards and automated terminals |
| **Top handler (top pick)** | Large forklift with a spreader that lifts loaded containers from the top | Flexible; used in many smaller terminals |
| **Reach stacker** | Telescoping boom with a spreader; can reach second and third rows | Versatile; common in rail and smaller yards |
| **Empty handler (side pick)** | Lifts empty containers by their side corner castings | Lighter-duty, for stacking empties high |
| **Mobile harbor crane** | Wheeled crane on the wharf | Handles containers, breakbulk, and bulk where STS cranes aren't installed |

### The Spreader

Most container equipment lifts with a **spreader**: a frame that lowers onto the container's **top corner castings** and locks into them with **twistlocks**. Spreaders are often **telescopic** (adjusting for 20-, 40-, and 45-foot boxes), and some can lift **two 20-foot containers** at once (twin-lift).

**Indicator lights** in the cab show when the spreader is **landed** and when twistlocks are **locked** or **unlocked**. Never hoist without "locked" confirmation.

## Jobs on the Waterfront

| Role | What they do |
|---|---|
| **Crane operator** | Runs the STS or yard crane from the cab or a remote station |
| **Signal person / hatch tender** | Guides the crane operator when the load or landing spot is out of view |
| **Lasher** | Secures and releases container lashings aboard ship (Module 5) |
| **Equipment operator** | Drives yard tractors, straddle carriers, top handlers, and RTGs |
| **Clerk / checker** | Verifies container numbers, seals, damage, and moves in the TOS |
| **Foreman / supervisor (walking boss)** | Directs a gang or an operation |
| **Planner** | Plans vessel stowage, yard layout, and equipment assignments |
| **Mechanic / electrician** | Maintains cranes and equipment; often specialized in hydraulics, drives, and controls |
| **Marine terminal security** | Gate control, access, and TWIC compliance |

On the U.S. West Coast, longshore work is typically dispatched through **hiring halls** under agreements between the ILWU and employers. Many workers start in entry-level roles and train up into skilled positions such as crane operator.

## Access and Credentials

- **TWIC** (Transportation Worker Identification Credential) is required for **unescorted access** to secure areas of regulated maritime facilities.
- Equipment operators complete **terminal-specific training and certification** for each machine.
- **High-visibility clothing, hard hats, and safety footwear** are standard in operating areas.

<Pitfall title="Assuming the Operator Sees You">

**Common mistake:** a pedestrian or checker assumes a straddle carrier or top handler driver can see them because they can see the machine.

Many terminal machines have **large blind spots**, especially close in and behind. Make **eye contact** or get a signal before crossing, use **designated walkways**, and stay out of equipment lanes.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

Terminals use **STS cranes** at the berth, **yard tractors** or **straddle carriers** to move boxes, and **RTGs, RMGs, top handlers, reach stackers, and empty handlers** in the yard. The **spreader** locks into **corner castings** with **twistlocks**. Waterfront jobs range from **crane operators and lashers** to **clerks, planners, and mechanics**. Access requires **TWIC** and machine-specific training.

<TryIt title="Match the Machine">

1. Stacking empty containers six high in a depot. *(Empty handler.)*
2. Lifting a loaded 40-ft box off a rail car in a small yard, reaching to the second track. *(Reach stacker.)*
3. Stacking import boxes in a block five wide and four high. *(RTG.)*
4. Moving a box from under the STS crane to the yard on a chassis. *(Yard tractor.)*

</TryIt>
