## Why Securing Matters

At sea, ships **roll, pitch, and heave**. Stacks of containers experience huge forces, especially in heavy weather. Poor securing leads to **collapsed stacks** and **containers lost overboard**, a danger to the crew, other vessels, and the environment.

---

## Below Deck: Cell Guides

In the holds of most container ships, containers drop into **cell guides**: vertical steel rails that hold the stacks in place. They need little or no lashing below deck. The crane operator lowers each box into the guides.

## On Deck: Lashing Systems

On deck, above the **hatch covers**, containers are held by a system of fittings:

| Fitting | Job |
|---|---|
| **Stacking cones** | Locate containers on top of each other (mostly below deck or as specified) |
| **Twistlocks** | Lock container corners together vertically; may be **manual**, **semi-automatic (SATL)**, or **fully automatic** |
| **Lashing rods** | Long steel rods hooked into container corner castings, angled down to the deck or lashing bridge |
| **Turnbuckles** | Tension the lashing rods |
| **Lashing bridges** | Raised platforms between bays that let lashings reach higher tiers |
| **Bridge fittings / buttresses** | Connect adjacent stacks or brace them |

### Twistlock Types

- **Manual**: lashers turn a handle to lock and unlock.
- **Semi-automatic (SATL)**: locks automatically when a container is landed; must be **unlocked manually** (often with a pole) before discharge.
- **Fully automatic**: locks and releases with crane movement, with no lasher needed at the corners.

On the wharf, workers often **insert or remove twistlocks** from container corners as the crane hovers. This "**cone station**" or "**coning**" work requires strict procedures because people are working right under the load.

---

## The Cargo Securing Manual

Every ship has an approved **Cargo Securing Manual (CSM)** (required under **SOLAS**) that specifies how cargo must be stowed and secured, including **stack weight limits**, **lashing patterns**, and **fittings**. The ship's **stowage plan** places heavy boxes low and respects limits for each stack.

Overweight or misdeclared containers (see **VGM**, Lesson 2.2) can overload stacks and lashings even when the lashing is done right.

## Lashing Safely

Lashing is one of the more hazardous waterfront jobs:

| Hazard | Control |
|---|---|
| **Heavy rods and turnbuckles** | Proper technique, team handling, ergonomic tools |
| **Working at height** on lashing bridges and hatch covers | Guardrails, fall protection where required, three points of contact on ladders |
| **Falling objects** (twistlocks, rods, debris) | Hard hats; stay out from under crane operations |
| **Moving containers overhead** | Coordinate with the crane and signal person; never be under a suspended load |
| **Trip hazards** on deck | Housekeeping; lighting at night |
| **Weather** | Wet, icy, windy conditions on open decks |

## Other Cargo Types

| Type | Description | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| **Breakbulk** | Individual pieces: steel, lumber, machinery, project cargo | Cranes with slings and spreader bars; dunnage and lashing in holds |
| **Ro-ro** (roll-on/roll-off) | Vehicles and rolling equipment driven aboard | Drivers, ramps; vehicles lashed to deck points |
| **Dry bulk** | Grain, coal, ore, potash | Conveyors, ship loaders, grab cranes; dust and engulfment hazards |
| **Liquid bulk** | Oil, chemicals, LNG | Pipelines, loading arms; strict fire and spill controls |
| **Project / heavy lift** | Very large or heavy items (wind turbine parts, modules) | Engineered lift plans, often tandem or ship's heavy-lift cranes |

The Pacific Northwest handles all of these, from containers and autos to grain export terminals along the Columbia River.

<Pitfall title="Discharging Without Unlocking">

**Common mistake:** a crane tries to lift a container from a deck stack, but a **semi-automatic twistlock** at a bottom corner was missed during unlocking.

The crane lifts the container **and** pulls on the one below, potentially damaging the ship, overloading the crane, or dropping a box. Lashers must **confirm all corners unlocked**, and operators must lift slowly and stop at any sign of resistance.

</Pitfall>

---

## Key Takeaway

Below deck, **cell guides** hold containers. On deck, **twistlocks, lashing rods, turnbuckles, and lashing bridges** secure the stacks per the ship's **Cargo Securing Manual**. **Semi-automatic twistlocks** must be unlocked before discharge. Lashing involves **heavy gear, heights, and overhead loads**. Other cargo includes **breakbulk, ro-ro, dry bulk, liquid bulk, and project cargo**.
