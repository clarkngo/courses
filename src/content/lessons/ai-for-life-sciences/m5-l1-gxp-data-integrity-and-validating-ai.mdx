## Why Quality Teams Are Cautious

GxP systems rest on one promise: the data can be trusted, and you can prove how it was created and changed. Generative AI challenges that promise in three ways:

1. **Non-determinism.** The same prompt can produce different outputs. Traditional validation assumes the same input gives the same output.
2. **Opacity.** You can't trace why a model produced a particular sentence the way you can trace a calculation.
3. **Change you don't control.** Cloud models are updated by vendors, sometimes without notice, so the system you validated may not be the system you're running.

None of this makes AI impossible in GxP. It means the controls have to be designed around these properties.

## ALCOA+ Applied to AI

Data integrity is commonly summarized as **ALCOA+**: data should be Attributable, Legible, Contemporaneous, Original, and Accurate, plus Complete, Consistent, Enduring, and Available.

| Principle | What it means for AI-assisted work |
|---|---|
| **Attributable** | Who generated, reviewed, and approved the content, including which tool and version contributed. |
| **Legible** | The record is readable and understandable, including what the AI did. |
| **Contemporaneous** | AI-assisted entries are recorded when the work happens, not reconstructed later. |
| **Original** | The source data is preserved. AI summaries never replace raw data. |
| **Accurate** | Output is verified against source before it becomes a record. |
| **Complete** | Rejected or regenerated outputs that influenced a decision are retained as your SOP requires. |
| **Consistent** | The same process is followed every time, with defined prompts and review steps. |
| **Enduring / Available** | Records, including relevant prompts and outputs, are retained and retrievable for inspection. |

In the U.S., **21 CFR Part 11** governs electronic records and signatures, and in the EU, **Annex 11** of the GMP guide covers computerized systems. Both apply to systems that create or hold GxP records, whether or not AI is involved.

<Pitfall title="The AI Summary Replaces the Source">

**Common mistake:** an AI-generated summary of batch records or monitoring data is filed, and the reviewer signs off on the summary without checking the source.

The signature attests to the data. If the summary dropped an excursion or misread a value, the signed record is wrong. Summaries can speed review; they can't stand in for it.

</Pitfall>

## Risk-Based Validation

Modern computerized-system thinking is **risk-based**: put validation effort where failure would affect patient safety, product quality, or data integrity. FDA's Computer Software Assurance (CSA) guidance, finalized in 2025, and industry frameworks such as ISPE's GAMP 5 (Second Edition) and its AI guidance both take this approach.

Key questions at each step:

- **Intended use:** What exactly will the AI do, and what will it **not** do? A vague intended use ("help with deviations") can't be validated.
- **Risk:** If the output is wrong, what's the worst realistic outcome, and would anyone catch it?
- **Controls:** Is there mandatory human review? Is the model version **locked**? Are outputs constrained (picking from a list rather than free text)?
- **Test:** Use real-world-representative data, including hard cases and known failure modes. For classification, measure what's missed as well as what's caught.
- **Monitor:** Track performance over time. Define what happens when the vendor updates the model.

## The EU's Draft Annex 22

In July 2025 the European Commission and PIC/S released a draft **Annex 22 on Artificial Intelligence** for the EU GMP guide, alongside revisions to Annex 11 and Chapter 4. As drafted, it:

- Covers AI models used in **critical GMP applications**, those with direct impact on patient safety, product quality, or data integrity.
- Expects **static, deterministic** models in those critical uses, with defined intended use, test data independent of training data, and performance metrics at least as good as the process they replace.
- Excludes **dynamic** (continuously learning) models and **generative AI and LLMs** from critical GMP applications, while allowing them in non-critical uses with a qualified human responsible for the output.

Annex 22 was a draft for consultation when this course was written. Check its current status, but the direction is clear: in critical GMP decisions, regulators want predictable, testable models, and generative AI stays in supporting roles under human control.

<TryIt title="Write an Intended-Use Statement">

Choose an AI use you'd like to bring into a GxP process (for example, AI-suggested categories for deviation trending). Write:

1. A one-paragraph **intended use**: the task, inputs, outputs, and who acts on them.
2. What the AI is explicitly **not** allowed to do.
3. The worst realistic failure and how it would be detected.
4. Three test cases, including one designed to make it fail.

Share it with your quality unit as a conversation starter, not a request for approval.

</TryIt>
